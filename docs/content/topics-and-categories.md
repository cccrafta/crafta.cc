# Topics and Categories

What Crafta writes about, as shown by the posts, ideas, feeds and the owner's saved preferences. Category definitions are one line each in the project `CLAUDE.md`; the framework's "Adapting to the Subject" section says how each type is weighted. This page adds what the categories look like in practice. Related: [[about-crafta]], [[content/sources-philosophy]], [[content/post-anatomy]].

## The six categories in practice

- **Garments** (16 published). One named garment, its origin, construction and adoption: CPO shirt, bib overall, Harrington, safari jacket, chore coat, duffle, N-1, Perfecto. The researched ones argue from construction ("every feature was added when something else failed"). Origin story, material criticality and patina are required here. Footwear construction (Goodyear welt) also sits here.
- **Materials** (10). A fabric or fibre and the process that makes it what it is: canvas, moleskin, cotton voile, chambray, ramie, seersucker, linen, corduroy, shell cordovan, tropical wool. The strongest ones hang on a process (moleskin's nine finishing steps) or a physical property and its consequence.
- **Culture** (5). Meaning and adoption rather than the object: boro, the "invisible layer" under a jacket, Peaky Blinders costume ("The Garments of the Returned"), denim mills going luxury ("Working Craft"), heritage brands making summer weights.
- **Technique** (4, all short pre-vault posts). A craft process: sashiko, katazome, hand-welting, natural against synthetic indigo. The idea bank holds 15 Technique ideas, the most backlog relative to output.
- **Design** (4, all short pre-vault). In practice this has held manufacturing and mill topics (Okayama mills, loopwheel knitting, indigo dyeing, vegetable tanning). Newer Design ideas lean toward design principles instead (resoling as philosophy, "reinforce where the body wears").
- **History** (4). Chronology-led: Breton stripe, tuxedo, military surplus, waxed cotton.

The boundary between Design, Technique and Materials is loose in the older posts (indigo dyeing is filed as Design, natural indigo as Technique) (inferred from the frontmatter).

## Recurring subject areas

Each is backed by posts, ideas, feeds or memory.

- **American and European workwear lineage.** Bib overall, chore coat, CPO, moleskin and bleu de travail, canvas, donkey jacket; ideas on the coverall, bar tack, cinch back, officer pant, FFA jacket, Bedford jacket, Western shirt, HBT. The core feeds (Heddels, Long John, Archival Clothing, Denimhunters) sit here.
- **Military origins of civilian clothes.** CPO, N-1, duffle, Breton stripe, safari jacket, military surplus; ideas on HBT and the flight jacket.
- **Japanese craft and reproduction.** Sashiko, boro, katazome, noragi, Okayama, loopwheel; contemporary-maker paragraphs in nearly every long post cite Japanese makers (Buzz Rickson's, Sugar Cane, Kapital, Japan Blue, Fob Factory, Champion's Harajuku reissues). Six Japanese-language or Japan-based feeds are tracked. Ideas: the Osaka Five, ring spinners, the samue, the sukajan, "the reproduction game", garment care.
- **Denim and indigo** as a running thread across Materials, Technique and Culture.
- **British heritage outerwear and tailoring.** Harrington, duffle, guernsey, waxed cotton, tuxedo (Henry Poole), Peaky Blinders tweed.
- **Material behaviour and ageing.** Patina is a required beat (framework); weight windows recur as an argument (the CPO's 20 to 26oz melton, the bib overall's 8oz denim).
- **Workwear and sportswear as one lineage** (new, October 2026). Five ideas created 2026-10-10/11 and the sweatshirt draft come from one research note, which also serves the owner's activewear design brief (see [[about-crafta]]).

## The owner's taste

From the saved memory note on taste (undated, older than the October 2026 work), cross-checked against the posts:

- **Makers named with approval:** Japanese craft makers who treat garments and bags as objects of care (UES, Mister Freedom, Bryceland's, Alberton Japan, Kaptain Sunshine, Sugar Cane, Blue Blue Japan, Studio D'Artisan); small-batch makers who source specific materials (Halley Stevensons canvas, Martexin wax, NOS military duck, British Millerain waxed cotton); heritage brands with real production lineage (Barbour, Filson, Carhartt, Le Laboureur, Vetra, Saint James, Armor Lux); niche high-quality makers (Trakke in Glasgow, Handy by Tom Bonamici in Portland, Bleu de Chauffe in France, Westley Richards in Birmingham, Private White V.C. in Manchester); bespoke and tailoring houses (Henry Poole, Cifonelli, Anderson & Sheppard, Richard Anderson). The posts bear this out: Mister Freedom and Sugar Cane in the CPO post, Henry Poole in the tuxedo post, Westley Richards cited by the framework itself.
- **The principle in the owner's words (memory):** "Makers who make things the way they should be made. Construction integrity over volume. Materials sourced with intention. Objects designed to be maintained, not replaced."
- **Style references for prose:** Heddels, Monocle, Permanent Style (logged in [[writing/lessons]]).

## What Crafta does not cover or want

- Luxury houses used for brand cachet, fast fashion and mass-market brands, "heritage" marketing without production history, and products where the story is louder than the construction (memory). The CPO post shows the stance applied: it reports that Schott's 50/50 blend "softens the spec to reach a price point" rather than condemning it.
- Product lists, buying guides and selling (framework: "They are not selling").
- **Geographic scope.** In scope: Japanese, Southeast Asian (Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, Malaysia, Philippines), British, French, Italian, Irish, American, West African, and other European and East Asian traditions. Out of scope: Indian subcontinent (India, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Sri Lanka) and Middle Eastern content, because these regions do not fit the owner's style or editorial identity (memory). When a feed such as Selvedge surfaces such content, skip it, and do not suggest these regions as post topics, research directions or sources. `AGENTS.md` records the same rule. Current ideas conflict with it: "Handwoven Indian Cotton in American Workwear Silhouettes" (created 2026-10-10) and "Madras" (pitched "from Indian handlooms") are pending, and the Seersucker idea mentions Persian courts. Ask the owner before marking them rejected. Southeast Asian, Korean and West African topics appear in ideas (Southeast Asian denim, Seoul workwear, bogolanfini).

## How ideas move to posts

The steps are defined in `CLAUDE.md` (Content Workflow) and the `idea-bank`, `discover`, `mood-board` and `generate-posts` skills. What the vault shows in practice:

- Ideas arrive in batches from feed briefings (`bank/Feeds/`) and from the "Possible Angles" and "Rabbit Holes" sections of research notes. Angles not chosen are saved as ideas, which is why one research session can add five ideas at once.
- Ideas are cheap and many: 73 notes, 66 pending, none rejected. Statuses lag reality: "The Bib Overall" and "Moleskin - The Fabric That Feels Like It Shouldn't Exist" are still `pending` though both posts are published. Check `bank/Posts/` and WordPress before trusting an idea's status.
- Near-duplicate ideas exist (two broken twill notes, two HBT notes, two kakishibu notes); merge rather than add a third.
- Idea notes are explicit about evidence gaps ("only the one-line Heddels summary has been read so far; fetch the full article before pitching"). Keep that habit.
