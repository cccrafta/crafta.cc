## In One Line
Midwest workwear maker credited in the vault with the modern bib overall (1911) and a run of workwear firsts. It is a source for how overalls were engineered, less for how it is made now.

## Lineage
H.D. Lee Co. was founded in Salina, Kansas in 1889, the same year as Carhartt.[^1] The 1873 Davis and Levi Strauss patent covers "waist overalls" (jeans) with suspenders and no bib; the bib followed in 1911 from H.D. Lee.[^2] The vault's research timeline lists the bib (1911), the Union-All (1913), the zipper fly (1927) and Sanforization as Lee firsts, taken from a Heddels history that is not saved as a source note.[^3]

## Making and Material
No source in the vault for current factories or cloth mills. Sanforization is listed as a Lee step in the research cache.[^3]

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. [unsourced] denim workwear fades with wear; no repair offer is documented.

## Today
A Lee x Nigel Cabourn collection called "OX'ed Silver" launched in the US on 8 October 2026, drawing on archival American workwear and Japanese craftsmanship.[^4]

## Design
The 1911 bib is a design decision documented in the vault: a bib added to the riveted waist overall. The brand's wider range is mass-market denim. [unsourced]

## Depth
A good paper trail for the bib overall, with a post and a research note already written. One idea note covers Lee within the "Big Three" market split (Levi's West, Lee Midwest, Wrangler South and West) by the 1920s and the rivet patent war; this is an Idea claim, not a source.[^5]
