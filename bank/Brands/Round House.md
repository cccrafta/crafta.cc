## In One Line
An Oklahoma overall and jeans maker founded for railroad workers. The maker says it is the oldest operating manufacturer in Oklahoma and the largest remaining US maker of jeans and overalls; both are its own claims.

## Lineage
Round House was founded in 1903 in Shawnee, Oklahoma, then Indian Territory, four years before statehood, and is named for the railroad "round house" repair station; over 100 trains passed Shawnee daily.[^1] Its first customers were railroad workers.[^1] The motto "Fit Best, Wear Longest" is said to be unchanged since founding.[^1]

## Making and Material
The maker states current output of about 300,000 units a year, 25,000 pairs a month and 1,250 pairs a day, using 12 million feet of thread a month, with Oklahoma-grown cotton in some products.[^1] The Canvas research note cites a Round House double-knee work pant (Lot 2202) in 12oz duck, triple-stitched with flat-felled seams, noting that the knee wears first so a second layer doubles the life.[^2] That detail is in a research note, not the history page, so it is a secondhand route to the product.

## Today
Still makes jeans and bib overalls in the US; the Bib Overall research note records about 300,000 units a year, the figures taken from the maker's page.[^1]
