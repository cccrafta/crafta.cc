## In One Line
London hatter since 1773 that went into liquidation in 2026 and was taken over by Laird. A live lineage rescue where production's return to London is announced, not yet shown.

## Lineage
Miller Christy and Joseph Storrs set up the business at Whitehart Court, London, on 1 March 1773 [^1]. The Christys took over the Stockport felt works in 1824 per the maker; Permanent Style says Stockport was bought in 1821, a conflict [^1][^2]. The Bermondsey factory made bowlers; in the 1990s all operations moved to Witney [^1]. Liberty acquired Christy & Co in 2011 [^1].

## Making and Material
Permanent Style (2012) saw felting in Stockport and blocking in Witney, and called it one of about 10 makers worldwide doing both, with flanges over 150 years old [^2]. Christys made hats for Lock & Co and Bates, and about a third of output was military and police hats; the factory made 10,000 bowlers and toppers that year [^2]. Staff fell from over 3,000 to about 30 [^2]. A 2025 reader comment says felt hoods are now imported from Portugal, which is unconfirmed [^2].

## Patina and Care
No source. Felt hats can be steamed and reblocked `[unsourced]`; no repair offer is documented.

## Today
In 2026 Laird Hatters acquired Christys, "bringing production back to London" [^1]. FashionUnited (March 2026, medium reliability) reports voluntary liquidation after 253 years, Laird's owner Alex Torun-Shaw, insolvency when approached in October 2025, and manufacturing to move back to London [^3]. The liquidation date rests on one secondary source.

## Design
Bowlers and toppers are fixed forms; the design value is the block and the felt, not styling. Seen only through description.

## Depth
A rare surviving chain from felting to blocking, and now a rescue story. The ideas note on Christys to Laird tracks it.
