## In One Line
American sporting goods maker whose 1917 and 1927 catalogues are among the vault's primary evidence for what athletic clothing was before the cotton sweatshirt; the evidence is the maker's own catalogues, which sell, not describe, the products.

## Lineage
The 1917 catalogue calls the sweater "the athletic garment par excellence".[^1] The UMass chapter records that Wright & Ditson, founded in Boston in 1871, was bought out by the Spalding brothers in 1891 and its name continued into the 1940s.[^3] No Spalding founding date appears in the vault. [unsourced] The company dates from 1876.

## Making and Material
The 1917 catalogue says Spalding made goods in its own factories in Brooklyn, Chicago and San Francisco, and describes full-fashioned jerseys "knit to exact shape on the machine and then put together by hand".[^1] It lists 10-oz canvas padding and canvas rubber-soled shoes.[^1] By 1927 the catalogue lists worsted and cotton team jerseys and aviators' equipment with moleskin outer shells.[^2] Catalogue copy is selling language, so the claims are the maker's.

## Today
No source in the vault says the brand makes clothing now. The name appears in the vault only through the catalogues and the purchase of Wright & Ditson. Contemporary relevance is not documented.

## Design
Fashioned knitting shapes the jersey to the body so no seam fights the arm, and the 1927 aviators' moleskin shells show an athletic maker supplying a flying garment. Design is a taste score: historic construction logic and a link to flight clothing fit the owner's military-to-civilian interest, but the catalogues show catalogue products and not distinct design.

## Depth
