## In One Line
A Lake Charles, Louisiana heritage menswear shop opened in 2018 in the front of a welding workshop. It earns a place for one frank number: most of its heritage denim buyers do not weld or ride motorcycles.

## Lineage
Founder Josh Guillory is an architectural blacksmith who welded staircases and chandeliers [^1]. He found Japanese denim culture through Instagram hashtags around 2010 and opened Iron Shop Provisions in 2018 in the front showroom of his workshop [^1].

## Making and Material
The shop retails; the own label, A New Medium (ANM), launched in 2023 with a denim jacket and a wool overshirt, using Japanese fabrics and Los Angeles sewing per the Working Craft research [^1]. The profile names the overshirt cloth as Hainesworth wool, a spelling that may refer to the mill listed as Hainsworth [inference]. No weights, mills or factory names for the jacket are recorded.

## Patina and Care
No source on how the jacket or the shop's denim ages.

## Today
In Guillory's words, "90 percent of our customers, if I had to guess, don't ride motorcycles or weld"; the people he talks to by direct message and feedback work in a cubicle [^1]. The Working Craft post uses that line for the gap between heritage denim's workwear image and its buyers.

## Design
Workwear shapes sold to office workers: a denim jacket and wool overshirt in Japanese fabrics [^1]. The vault records no detail beyond the garment names.
