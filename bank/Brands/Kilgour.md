## In One Line
Savile Row tailor, in the vault for one fact: its cutter rebuilt Fred Astaire's Top Hat tailcoat and found the dance engineering inside it. That makes it a case of tailoring built for movement.

## Lineage
Kilgour French & Stanbury made Astaire's tailcoat for *Top Hat* in 1935.[^1] A Savile Row house with a long bespoke record. [unsourced]

## Making and Material
In 2012 cutter Del Smith tried to recreate the coat and found an enlarged sleevehead and an exaggerated rope shoulder for arm movement, and waistcoat edges rounded so they would not catch on a raised leg.[^1] The cloth, the workroom location and the current production are not in the vault.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. Bespoke garments are normally repaired and altered by the house that cut them. [unsourced]

## Today
Not covered beyond the 2012 recreation. Current ownership and range are not in the vault.

## Design
The recreated tails show a design logic the owner values: the shape is set by what the body does (dance), not by silhouette fashion.[^1] No view on the current collection.

## Depth
One documented case: the Astaire coat, with the cutter's reading of it. Rabbit hole: pattern drafts and fitting practice for performance garments, compared with the fitting account at Anderson & Sheppard already in [[Research/Tuxedo]].
