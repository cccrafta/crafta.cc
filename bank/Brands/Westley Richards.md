## In One Line
A Birmingham gunmaker and outfitter, established 1812, selling a safari jacket for actual field use. Its own history page traces the jacket to British India, not Africa, which tempers its Hemingway-era marketing.

## Lineage
The publication note gives an 1812 start and over 200 years of outfitting hunters and adventurers.[^1] Its safari-origins page traces the jacket to the 1842 Afghan disaster, Lt. Hodson's khaki Corps of Guides uniforms at Lahore around 1846, and the "Shikar Jacket"; Captain Henry Shakespear's 1860 *The Wild Sports of India* specifies five front pockets.[^2] The firm presents its jackets as direct lineage from those built for Hemingway's generation, a promotional claim the vault does not test.

## Making and Material
The Selous Safari Jacket has real horn buttons engraved with the company name, four cartridge slots on the right breast pocket, a small left-arm pocket, an action back, buttoned cuffs for rolling the sleeve, bellows pockets, a belt and working epaulettes, and it is made in Portugal.[^1] Fabrics include a 50/30/20 Tencel/cotton/linen blend, a 66/32/2 linen/cotton/elastane, 100% linen and a 2025 Japanese cotton/linen/Tencel blend.[^1] The Bushveld uses 100% cotton drill, described as tear-resistant, thorn-proof, tsetse-fly proof and densely woven.[^1] The clothing is made in Portugal, not Birmingham; guns are Birmingham work, an inference from the publication note.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. The Bushveld's claims are durability claims, not ageing observations.[^1]

## Today
The Linen Selous is listed at GBP 695 and the Bushveld at GBP 565.[^1] The range includes Campaign Safari Shirts and Explorer bags and leather goods.[^1] The Safari Jacket post cites the firm as still making the jacket for field use.

## Design
Functional detail given a reason: cartridge slots, ear-plug pocket, action back for rifle or camera.[^1] Scored on taste: field-first, detail-led, close to the owner's function-driven interests.
