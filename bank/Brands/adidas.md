## In One Line
Bavarian sportswear giant. Crafta cares about it for one military-to-civilian object, the German Army Trainer, whose designer is still disputed, and for how a sport shoe changes meaning on the street.

## Lineage
Sport in History places adidas in Bavaria and calls it the world's leading sports footwear maker by the 1960s; the Superstar was designed to take the basketball market from American rubber companies, then took on hip-hop meanings adidas never intended [^1]. The 1954 training shoe launch is cited as the point where sportswear became "a style trend worn on the street" [^2]. Founding year and founders are `[unsourced]` in the vault.

## Making and Material
The 1982 Bundeswehr indoor shoe was made at ZDA Partizanske in Czechoslovakia, about 500,000 pairs by 1989 (Wikipedia DE, low reliability) [^3]; Heddels says hundreds of thousands were made, mainly in Slovakia [^6]. Current production and materials are `[unsourced]`. adidas Terrex carries Gore-Tex ePE products per Trek & Mountain, which suggests the group buys technical membranes rather than making them.

## Patina and Care
No source. A white leather GAT's ageing is not documented in the vault.

## Today
adidas reissues the army shoe as BW Army, priced at $115 per Heddels [^6], and relaunched it in 2025 per Highsnobiety (T3) [^7]; Ape to Gentleman shows it in a GAT revival piece (low reliability) [^8]. Barbour collaborations have included adidas [^9]. Fan culture around rare trainers appears in casual-culture accounts, with a trip to Amsterdam for rare pairs cited by Contemporary British History.

## Design
The GAT is the piece that matters here: white leather, gum sole, no logo-led styling; the army-issue shape resembles the Samba per Wikipedia DE [^3]. The rest of the range is not judged from vault sources.

## Depth
The GAT designer dispute: an adidas spokesperson says it "designed a trainer, called the BW-Sport" [^4], while the Bundeswehr museum in Dresden "only had records that the design was made by Puma" [^4]. Grailed notes both firms bid and says "We may never know" [^5]. That is a self-contained research thread.
