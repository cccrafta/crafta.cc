## In One Line
The Burnley mill that wove Grenfell Cloth, a dense uncoated cotton made windproof by its weave, and the origin of the Grenfell jacket that sits beside the Baracuta in the Harrington story.

## Lineage
The firm was T. Haythornthwaite & Sons Ltd, Lodge Mill, Burnley, from 1918, with a separate marketing company formed in 1931 [^1]. Dr Wilfred Grenfell lectured in Burnley in 1922 at the invitation of mill-owner Walter Haythornthwaite; the cloth was produced in 1923 after a year of experiments [^1]. The Rake tells the same meeting as an alternative origin for the Harrington, with Grenfell's brief that clothing "must be windproof, but must breathe" [^4]. The mill's pages date Everest use to 1933 (a tent at 27,000 ft) and 1936, plus the Oxford Ellesmere Land (1934/5), British Graham Land (1934/7) and 1954 Daily Mail Abominable Snowman expeditions [^1]. They do not confirm a 1924 Everest claim, Shackleton, Antarctica or the RAF; those stay unconfirmed. The Everest 1953 and Admiral Byrd claims in the London Brands note are brand-sourced and unverified.

## Making and Material
The cloth was woven from "the finest Egyptian Cotton" on looms "specially strengthened", and it was so naturally waterproof that dyeing was almost impossible [^1]. It is uncoated, high-density and windproof, with moisture escaping; the pages give no thread count [^1]. Garments are made in Grenfell's own east London factory (Lea Bridge, E17), but the sources do not say who weaves the cloth today [^2]. Haythornthwaite stopped making garments in 1991, a Japanese company held the brand for a period, and the Azam family bought it back [^3]. The Rake's Hong Kong coverage says the Azam family has run London factories since 1984 (Cache A, not fetched).

## Today
Grenfell jackets are still sold; the weaving arrangement is a gap. The Rake's Harrington feature treats the Grenfell as the cloth-first alternative to the Baracuta G9 [^4].

## Design
The design is the cloth: a plain, tightly woven cotton that sheds wind and light rain without a coating, which is why a short blouson cut in it works without lining tricks. The silhouette that carried it, the Grenfell jacket, is simple and functional.

## Depth
Expedition records (Everest 1933 and 1936, Graham Land, Ellesmere Land) give a documented use history for a cloth. The 1922 lecture and the 1923 trials are told by the mill itself. Rabbit holes: the Grenfell against Ventile and Barbour wax, and who weaves the cloth now.
