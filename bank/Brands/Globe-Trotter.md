## In One Line
An English luggage maker founded in 1897 whose cases rest on vulcanised fibreboard, a material it patented in 1901 and still uses.

## Lineage
Globe-Trotter says it was founded in 1897 by David Nelken in Saxony [^1]. Malcolm Payton acquired it in 1985 after the death of Henry Nelken [^1]. The maker says Queen Elizabeth II carried its cases in 1947 and Hillary in 1953, and that BOAC made it the official case in 1960 [^1]. All of this is the maker's own account.

## Making and Material
The vulcanised fibreboard was patented in 1901 and is still in all its cases; the maker says it has "yet to be imitated successfully", which is a maker claim [^1]. Globe-Trotter says cases are handmade in England with Victorian-era methods and machinery, without naming the factory [^1]. The London Brands research records the brand as London-based since about 1932 with the factory unconfirmed. A claim of Broxbourne, 98 processes and about 10 days is unverified in cache B, and Wikipedia gives about 70 staff.

## Today
The maker presents the cases as still handmade in England [^1]. The vault has no independent coverage of current production.

## Design
The case is a hard, rectangular fibre shell, defined by its material and by its history of use on royal and expedition travel, as the maker tells it [^1].
