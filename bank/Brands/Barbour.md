## In One Line
Waxed-cotton field and motorcycle clothing made in South Shields since 1894, now also the subject of a denim turn (Levi's, A.P.C.). The vault's reference case for a coating, a garment and a repair service kept together.

## Lineage
John Barbour founded the firm in 1894 as an oilcloth importer and perfected a paraffin wax finish after linseed oil cracked in cold [^1]. The International motorcycle suit followed in 1936 for the Six Day Trials and was worn by virtually every British team from 1936 to 1977 [^1] [^2]. A two-piece suit for HMS Ursula became standard issue in the submarine service [^2]. Dame Margaret Barbour designed the Bedale, Beaufort and Border in 1980; Royal Warrants date from 1974, 1982 and 1987 [^1].

## Making and Material
The Bedale is 6oz waxed canvas, and Gear Patrol names the usual cloth as 6oz Sylkoil [^1] [^5]. Heddels counts 160 parts per Bedale, 36 people to make one and about 3,000 a week [^1]. A 2017 Heddels snippet says only some waxed jackets are still made in South Shields, with the rest made in Portugal, Turkey and Bulgaria [^7].

## Patina and Care
Heddels reports 13,000 jackets re-waxed a year at South Shields and one Beaufort kept for 25 years [^1]. The vault's publication note gives over 60,000 a year; the figures conflict and are kept as stated [[Sources/Publications/Barbour]]. Gear Patrol's claim that the denim Bedale patinas "matched only by leather" is an untested opinion [^5].

## Today
Collaborations include Engineered Garments (AW 2018) and Tokihito Yoshida (2010) [^4]. The Levi's collection launched 30 October 2025 [^6]. Barbour describes the A.P.C. line as "Barbour's wax with A.P.C.'s denim", at about EUR 390 [^7]. The Denim Jungle Fatigue Casual Jacket (GBP 280) has no stated weight or mill, and its place of manufacture is "Unknown" on one listing and UK on another, unresolved [^8].

## Design
Short, practical cuts: the Bedale's equestrian length with side vents, the Beaufort's raglan sleeves and rear game pocket [^1].

## Depth
The Ursula suit is a research note on its own: the prototype is dated 1939 by The Vintage Showroom and 1940 by Barbour, and the surname is spelled Phillips and Philips [^2] [^3]. The Falklands story is smaller than the legend: Put This On finds Barbours there in small numbers, mostly private purchases [^4].
