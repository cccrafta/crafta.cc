## In One Line
British mountaineering clothing maker that the vault meets only as a tester's jacket for Gore-Tex ePE, a PFAS-free membrane. Useful as a field-test case; the company itself is unstudied.

## Lineage
The company is a long-standing British climbing brand. [unsourced] No founding year, founders or factory history are in the vault.

## Making and Material
The Makalu jacket in the Trek & Mountain test uses a standard ePE laminate with a 75D polyester face fabric and solution-dyed backer.[^1] Production location is not in the vault.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. A shell membrane's ageing and re-proofing are not covered for this brand.

## Today
Named by Trek & Mountain as one of four brands with ePE products on the UK market in March 2024, alongside Patagonia, Rab and adidas Terrex.[^1] Gore's stated plan was to replace ePTFE by autumn 2025.[^1]

## Design
A technical shell design driven by use; the vault holds no view of its cut or detailing.

## Depth
Two independent field reports on the same cloth: several months of autumn and winter use in the Cairngorms and Peak District, and a Scottish test against a Patagonia jacket.[^1][^2]

## What Trusted Sources Say
- [[Sources/Publications/Trek & Mountain]] — mixed: the Makalu was reliably dry and fine for steady walking, but "clearly not quite as breathable as GORE-TEX PRO".[^1]
- [[Sources/Publications/Live For The Outdoors]] — mixed to neutral: in Scotland the tester noticed no difference from the previous membrane "apart from a thinner feel".[^2]
