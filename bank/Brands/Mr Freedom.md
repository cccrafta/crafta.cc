## In One Line
A London boutique label (Tommy Roberts, 1969) whose 1970 football jumper, held by the V&A, marks the stage where sport kit became fashion. It is not the same brand as Mister Freedom of Los Angeles.

## Lineage
Mr Freedom is a London boutique, founded in 1969 by Tommy Roberts [unsourced]. The V&A holds a September 1970 scarlet football jumper, accession T.203-1974, labelled "Mr Freedom, Made in England" and donated by Mr Freedom to the Cecil Beaton Collection.[^1] The record calls it a fashion shop's version of a team shirt, with the number as graphic, not a playing kit.[^1] Whether the label continued in unbroken form after the boutique period is not in the vault.

## Making and Material
The jumper is machine-knitted wool with "15" knitted into the front and smaller "15" on the sleeves in blue and white, cream cuffs and neck band, short sleeves, round neck.[^1] The label says made in England; the V&A record names no factory and no yarn.[^1]

## Design
The documented piece is a pop translation of team kit: a graphic number on a knit jersey made for the shop floor, not the pitch.[^1] Scored on taste: playful and historically interesting, but costume-leaning and not function-led for the owner's focus.

## Depth
The jumper is one museum object. The vault's workwear-and-sportswear research uses it as the dated "sport kit as fashion" stage, next to the Champion Bowie sweatshirt (2003) and the Kent & Curwen cricket sweater (1997) in the same V&A record.[^1] Do not merge with Mister Freedom (Los Angeles, Christophe Loiron), a separate vintage-workwear maker.
