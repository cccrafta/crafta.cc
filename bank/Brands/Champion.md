## In One Line
Sweatshirt maker with a dated patent trail for the Reverse Weave; the vault's case of a sport garment that became a heritage object.

## Lineage
Champion began on 6 May 1919 as Knickerbocker Knitting Mills in Rochester, New York, run by the Feinbloom family, per the maker's own page [^1]. Samuel N. Friedland's patent of 9 August 1938 describes a tubular knit turned 90 degrees, and a 14 October 1952 patent, "Athletic Garment Or Like", is credited to Friedland and Feinbloom [^2]. The maker's timeline says Reverse Weave was invented in 1934 and its banner says "Created in 1938. Patented in 1952", which is internally inconsistent; the patents take precedence [^1] [^2].

## Making and Material
The maker says the fabric is cut on the grain to prevent shrinkage [^1]. Heddels reports current Reverse Weave as mostly blended fabric made in Cambodia or Honduras, with some Made in USA product for Japan [^3]. The Houyhnhnm piece on the Harajuku opening notes a 1952 reissue with the first vertical side "expansion gusset" [^4].

## Patina and Care
No dedicated ageing source. Heddels places Reverse Weave as the mid-tier benchmark at $70-150, implying a hard-wearing garment, but does not describe wear [^5].

## Today
Reissue lines True Archives and Homage Archives sit alongside collaborations with Supreme, Beams Plus and others [^3] [^4]. The NBA supplied Champion uniforms from 1989 to 1997 [^2].

## Design
The panel logic is the design: cut with the grain turned, gusset at the side. Mass-market lines weaken the signal, so taste lands at the reference mark for useful, not inventive, design.

## Depth
Patents, maker history, trade-press centenary and Japanese reissue coverage allowed a full post, The Patent That Fixed the Sweatshirt. The MoMA hoodie claim and the flocking date remain maker claims without independent checks [^1].
