## In One Line
A menswear house with stores in Tokyo and Hong Kong that mixes tailoring and workwear, and whose safari jacket in Solaro cloth is a case study in turning a colonial field garment into a craft piece.

## Lineage
The Journal's publication note says the house has stores in Tokyo and Hong Kong and was founded by Ethan Newton and Tony Sylvester [^1]. The vault's research places Newton, an Australian, in Harajuku, Tokyo, around 2015 [unsourced]. The stated philosophy is a "mix of tailoring and vintage, workwear and tailoring", soft Italian make in English cloths, and made-to-order on the logic that it is "better for the world if we only make what we need" [^2]. The October 2026 briefing lists "Ten Years of Bryceland's" (7 Aug) [^7].

## Making and Material
The Safari Jacket in Smith Woollens Solaro is 100% wool, with four bellowed pockets, an inverted back pleat and a self-tie belt, made by Ascot Chang (the same maker as The Armoury Safari II) [^2]. The jacket also comes in HBT Olive [^2]. A sweatshirt in the cache is described in product copy as loopwheel knit in Wakayama and made up in Tokyo, "worn as sportswear" (cache, product copy). Named cloth and named makers are the strength.

## Patina and Care
Permanent Style notes Bryceland's has chainstitch machines for hemming, a basic alteration service [^4]. No source describes how its pieces age.

## Today
Permanent Style's London guide lists the Chiltern Street shop as a workwear and tailoring mix [^3]. The "A Spotlight on Made-to-Measure" briefing entry (9 Oct) shows an active made-to-measure line [^7]. Bryceland's Journal, in feeds.md, publishes craft-focused menswear editorial with contributors such as G. Bruce Boyer [^1].

## Design
Soft shoulders, English cloth, workwear pockets: a tailored jacket that moves like a chore coat. The safari jacket keeps the bellowed pockets, belt and pleat of the original and changes the cloth and the make.

## Depth
The Journal is a long editorial record. The Solaro jacket is the key piece: the vault's Safari Jacket research puts it next to Huntsman and Westley Richards. The Ascot Chang link and the Wakayama loopwheel are small research threads.
