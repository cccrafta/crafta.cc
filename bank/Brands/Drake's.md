## In One Line
A London accessories and tailoring label, founded in 1977, that began with lambswool scarves and became known for ties. It is a test case for how far a London name says where the goods are made.

## Lineage
Founded in 1977 by three former Aquascutum staff: Michael Drake (head of menswear design), Jeremy Hull and Isabel Dickson, starting with oversized lambswool scarves [^1]. In the mid-1980s the Hill & Drake tie line, run with Charles Hill, was sold on to Turnbull & Asser; Drake's then hired tiemakers from across the country and moved them into its Clerkenwell office [^1]. A website followed in 2007 and a Clifford Street shop in 2011 [^1].

## Making and Material
Permanent Style's 2012 history gives no detail on how ties are made and names no mills; it notes that Holliday & Brown, where Drake once had ties made, now belongs to Mantero in Como [^1]. The research note records ties made in London, shirts in Chard, tailoring in Italy and knitwear in Scotland [^1]. The factory location is reported three ways: Clerkenwell in the source, Hoxton in a cache entry, and a Hackney tie factory in the publication note. These stay as a conflict.

## Today
Drake's collaborated with Blackhorse Lane Ateliers on denim; the vault holds only a search snippet [single-source]. Chambray appears in the vault's post as a cloth Drake's has used to bridge shirting and workwear.

## Design
Quiet, English-leaning, soft-constructed pieces around the tie. The house sits between tailoring and casual wear; the vault records no signature detail beyond that.
