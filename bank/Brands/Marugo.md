## In One Line
A Kurashiki maker of rubber-soled work tabi, founded in 1919 and still making them in Japan; it matters to Crafta because the split-toe shoe Margiela made famous began as footwear for working bodies.

## Lineage
Marugo's own history gives 1919 as the founding year of Marugo Tabi Co., crediting Fujiki Itaro with the sewn-on jika-tabi, made by sewing reshaped rickshaw tyres to tabi [^1]. Kurashiki city dates the tyre version to about 1917 and the company to 1919 [^3]. The inventor is contested: Japanese Wikipedia credits Ishibashi Tokujiro's glued-on sole, sold from 1922 [^5], and ABC names "Shojiro Ishibashi, 1922" [^4]. Marugo's chairman says rubber soles spread worldwide at once, citing Converse in 1917 [^2]. The 1932 "everlasting work gloves" were Japan's first rubber-coated gloves, per the company [^1].

## Making and Material
Production went abroad (China from 1968, Korea from 1986, the Philippines from 1992) and Japanese jika-tabi production restarted in 2012 [^1]. The range includes Kojima denim and Kurashiki canvas versions [^3]. No source describes the last, sole attachment or fabric in detail.

## Today
Demand for work tabi tapered, so Marugo launched ASSABOOTS in Europe in 2009 and in Japan in 2012, then Tabirira in 2014 without metal clasps [^2]. Its festival tabi hold about 60% of that segment, a maker claim [^2]. It opened MARUGO TOKYO in 2019 and MARUGO KURASHIKI in 2022 [^2]. Japanese Wikipedia puts Marugo near 30% of the market [^5].

## Design
Plain work shoes with a clear function; ASSABOOTS adapts them for fashion.
