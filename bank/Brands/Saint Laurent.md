## In One Line
A Paris couture house, relevant to Crafta for two garment translations: Le Smoking (1966) and the Saharienne (1968). Its garment ideas are borrowed from tailoring and the field; the house itself is a luxury label.

## Lineage
Le Smoking debuted in August 1966 in the "Pop Art" collection, inspired by a 1933 photograph of Marlene Dietrich.[^1] Only one haute couture version sold at first; the Rive Gauche ready-to-wear version was the commercial success, and Saint Laurent made more than 200 variations until his retirement in 2002.[^1] The Museum at FIT's Sporting Life exhibition links the Norfolk suit, a hunting garment whose relaxed style the museum dates to the 1860s, to later influence on Yves Saint Laurent and others.[^3] The 1968 Saharienne, a bush-jacket translation, appears in the published Safari Jacket post, with no source note behind it.

## Today
Lyst ranked Saint Laurent the number one brand for Q3 2025 for the first time, with the Le Loafer among the top products; this is a T3 retail-demand ranking, second-hand through a summary.[^2] It measures heat, not craft quality.

## Design
Garments taken from other tailoring and field traditions: the dinner jacket for women, the bush jacket for the city.[^1] Scored on taste as an influential translator, but the owner discounts luxury cachet and brand story.
