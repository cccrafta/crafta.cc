## In One Line
New York maker tied to the Perfecto motorcycle jacket and, in the vault, to a widely sold budget wool CPO; the evidence is mostly product specifications and a published post that cites Schott's own timeline.

## Lineage
A published Crafta post on the Perfecto dates the design to 1928 and lists Schott NYC's Perfecto timeline as a reference.[^1] The post has no tracked sources (pre-vault migration), so the date and the link to Schott are unverified. [unsourced] Schott dates its start to 1913.

## Making and Material
The current CPO model 7810 is 20 oz, 50% wool and 50% polyester, with anchor buttons and dual pockets, USD 125, described by the brand as "lightweight".[^2] Heddels' older roundup recorded a 24 oz pure melton version at USD 195 with corozo buttons in olive, and a 20 oz wool black version.[^3] The CPO research marks whether the melton version is still made as unclear and the weight as lighter than the original.[^2] Factories and leather sources are not recorded.

## Patina and Care
The Perfecto post says heavy steer-hide leather "will feel like armor for the first few months", then softens and creases at elbows and shoulders; the post is unsourced and speaks of leather jackets in general, not Schott's.[^1] No repair offer is documented.

## Today
Research describes the 7810 as the most widely distributed CPO in the current market, in more than 15 colours.[^2]

## Design
The Perfecto's asymmetric zip, snap collar and belted waist are each answers to a rider's problem, per the post.[^1] The 7810 is a watered-down form of a heavy military shirt. Design is a taste score: the original pieces are strong, the budget CPO less so.

## Depth
A research note covers CPO weights and prices; the Perfecto has a published post. Neither uses a Schott primary source.
