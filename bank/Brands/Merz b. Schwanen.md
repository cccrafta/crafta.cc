## In One Line
A German knitwear maker that Heddels and Put This On name for loopwheeled sweats and henleys; the vault holds no maker page, so founding, factory and process rest on those mentions.

## Lineage
No founding date or founder is documented in the vault. Heddels says the house revived historical German knitwear, starting from a reproduction of a vintage henley [^2]. Loopwheeling itself dates to the mid-1920s for sweatshirt fabric and faded in the 1940s and 50s [^1].

## Making and Material
Heddels lists Merz b. Schwanen among the specialists using loopwheel machines, a near tensionless tube-knit process; only a few such machines still run, in small factories in Japan, Canada and Germany [^1]. Loopwheeled fabric feels plump and slightly slubby, and such items cost roughly twice as much as non-loopwheeled ones [^1]. A trade blog's 12 oz organic cotton figure was found but not read, so it is not used here. No mill, factory town or yarn source is named in the vault.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault.

## Today
Put This On recommends Merz b. Schwanen for loopwheeled quality among heritage henleys [^4]. Heddels places it in the end tier of sweatshirts, priced from $175 up, beside mostly Japanese makers [^3]. The same article says a higher price does not make a sweatshirt objectively better, only more specific and time-intensive [^3].

## Design
Plain, vintage-based shapes: sweatshirt and henley blocks rather than fashion pieces. Scores the garment type; no piece is described in the vault.

## Depth
Three Heddels and Put This On mentions, none a profile. A maker visit or interview would replace the inference; the idea note on German knitwear revival is not yet in the vault.
