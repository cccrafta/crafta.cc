## In One Line
Portland women's workwear label founded by landscapers after jeans failed under physical work. A rare maker that drafted women's workwear patterns from scratch and ended up at the same crotch gusset climbing pants use.

## Lineage
Founded in 2018 by landscapers Kate Day and Kyle Marie Summers (also cited as Kyle Marie Begley) with apparel developer Sara DeLuca; an Amazon blurb says 2015, a conflict [^1]. The origin story: women landscaping found jeans failed under physical work [^1].

## Making and Material
The Maven pant is a canvas trades pant with a crotch gusset and double-front panels [^1]. Patterns were drafted from scratch for women's proportions, with input from thousands of working women; each pocket's size and placement is stated to be tested [^1]. Search results for the Maven X give 10 pockets, a hammer loop and more. The page cites "military-grade fibres, waterless dyeing and recycled materials" [^1]. Factories and mills are not in the vault.

## Patina and Care
A "Rewear + Repair" collection is listed on the page [^1]; the vault has no detail on how it works.

## Today
Made for working women in trades and landscaping, per the maker's own page [^1]. Independent coverage is missing.

## Design
Plain functional trades-pant design; the pockets and the gusset are the design. Judged from description.

## Depth
Research/Workwear and Sportswear notes that Dovetail independently arrives at the gusset that climbing pants use, though "nobody documents it as originally workwear" (footnote 70 there).
