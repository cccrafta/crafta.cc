## In One Line
Family-run Savile Row tailor with military work at its centre; the vault's best-documented example of a tailor tied to regimental uniform.

## Lineage
Permanent Style's 2016 profile calls the house family-run and about 150 years old [^1]; the vault's London Brands table gives 1865 and a basement workroom at 10 Savile Row, which is the source of the frontmatter year. The firm took over the workrooms beneath the shop in 2012 [^1]. Permanent Style's later list of tailors calls the house "traditional, military-influenced, strong-shouldered" and "remarkably consistent" [^2].

## Making and Material
Military tailoring is about 30% of the work, with weekly visits to Sandhurst and uniforms made for Princes William and Harry [^1]. The house had 22 regiments among its clients 20 years before 2016 and 8 at the time of writing [^1]. In April 2016 it launched ready-to-wear from GBP 995, half-canvassed, made by Wensum in Mauritius [^1].

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault on ageing or repair of the garments.

## Today
The strong-shouldered, "clean and close" house cut is the style Permanent Style describes [^1]. The ready-to-wear line shows the house reaching a wider customer than its regimental base [^1].

## Design
A firm, structured shoulder with a close cut, from a military habit; taste score 3 for a conservative house with a clear and consistent style, and no stronger design voice shown in the vault.

## Depth
The Permanent Style piece gives a rare set of numbers: share of military work, regiments served, price of first ready-to-wear, and the workroom move. The vault also holds an Idea on military tailoring after the regiments.
