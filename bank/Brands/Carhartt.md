## In One Line
Detroit duck workwear maker with a primary-source patent for its overall; the vault treats it as a core American workwear case and tests the climbing claim.

## Lineage
Carhartt Manufacturing was founded in 1889 in Detroit, the same year as H.D. Lee, per the vault's Bib Overall research. The 1915 overall patent (filed 20 November 1914, granted 31 August 1915) names Hamilton Carhartt, Hamilton Carhartt Jr. and Oscar E. Kratz of Detroit as inventors [^1]. The CO1 chore coat dates from 1923 and is described as unchanged since the 1950s in the Canvas research.

## Making and Material
The patent describes a continuous facing strip, double-row two-needle stitching, reinforced buttonholes and pencil and rule pockets [^1]. Carhartt uses cotton duck, which softens with wear [^2]. No source in the vault gives current factory locations.

## Patina and Care
Duck canvas breaks in with use, and a Wear Ware writer mentions "tattering on old Carhartt" among examples of worn garments, a low-reliability note [^2] [^5].

## Today
Forums name Carhartt double-front pants among workwear used for climbing; Mountain Project praises them and UKClimbing says Carhartts and Dickies are common on US sites, both T4 [^3] [^4]. The vault's research finds no 1960s-70s source that names Carhartt in climbing.

## Design
Heavy-duty cut with generous room through hips and knees, a pattern the Bib Overall research treats as engineered, not styled. The taste score places Carhartt at the reference mark for utilitarian workwear: sound, rarely inventive.

## Depth
Patent, canvas history and two forum threads give a clear structure: documented construction, plausible climbing use, unsourced historical link. The patent is a T1 anchor for the overall; origin claims for the brand beyond the patent come from vault research without a footnoted source here.
