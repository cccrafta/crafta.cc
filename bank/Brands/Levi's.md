## In One Line
The company that put its name on Jacob Davis's 1873 riveted-pocket patent and still sells the trouser that came from it; its documented origin is the reason it matters to Crafta.

## Lineage
Patent US 139,121 was filed by Jacob W. Davis on 9 August 1872 and granted on 20 May 1873, assigned to Davis and Levi Strauss & Co. of San Francisco. It describes metal rivets at the pocket openings, moving the strain from thread to metal. Davis could not afford the $68 fee alone and wrote to Levi Strauss to split it.[^1] The patent covers waist-high trousers with button-on suspenders and no bib; the bib came later, in 1911, with H.D. Lee.[^1] Sources in the vault conflict on the first cloth: a canvas research note says the first riveted pants were duck canvas, switched to denim later, while another note calls denim the miner's cloth from the start; the patent itself says only "fabric" and "cloth."[^1]

## Making and Material
The vault records no current factories or mills for Levi's. Construction is documented by contrast: Permanent Style notes the 501 fly is two stitched pieces, which Blackhorse Lane replaced with a single strip as a response to a perceived weak point.[^2] Cloth is denim; no weights or named mills are recorded.

## Patina and Care
A Wear Ware piece compares the fade of old vintage clothing to the "whiskers" on old Levi's.[^6] No repair or care offer is documented in the vault. [unsourced] Levi's runs tailoring and repair services in some stores.

## Today
Permanent Style's Clutch Cafe guide points to Levi's made-to-measure as an option when Full Count's cut does not fit.[^3] The Barbour x Levi's collection launched on 30 October 2025, mixing Levi's trucker cuffs, back-cinch and arcuate pockets with Barbour's waxed cotton and corduroy collar.[^4] Gear Patrol framed the Barbour Denim Bedale as using Levi's as a springboard.[^5]

## Design
The Type I and trucker shapes are the template that most denim jackets still follow; the rivet, back-cinch and arcuate stitching are functional details that became signs. Design is a taste score: right and recognisable, but the main line is the known piece done safely, and the owner's interest lies more with the Japanese reproductions of it.

## Depth
A primary patent, a 150-year archive and a founding story that Crafta has already used for the bib overall and for pocket-corner rivets in the workwear-and-sportswear research.[^1] Open threads: first cloth (canvas or denim), reissue lines, and how the 501's construction changed.
