## In One Line
Savile Row house at 15 Savile Row, with ledgers from 1846 and the 1865 commission that became the dinner jacket. Permanent Style calls it the founder of Savile Row.

## Lineage
The house grew from James Poole's military uniforms in the Napoleonic Wars; his son Henry is a founding figure of Savile Row [^1]. The page says only "over 200 years"; 1806 as the start date comes from Research/London Brands. Archive ledgers date from 1846 [^1]. In 1865 the Prince of Wales commissioned a short smoking jacket, in celestial blue, that became the dinner suit [^2]. The four founders of the Tuxedo Club (Astor, Goelet, Mills, Lorillard) were Poole customers in the 1860s, and Henry Poole died in 1876 [^2]. Permanent Style calls it the founder of Savile Row [^3].

## Making and Material
Handmade at 15 Savile Row [^1]. More than 6,000 fabric choices [^1]. Permanent Style describes a strong English cut with less shoulder and chest padding than Huntsman, "more flexible" [^3]. A Travel Trench coat is made with GORE-TEX [^1]. Research/London Brands notes 120+ restored ledgers.

## Patina and Care
Bespoke garments are made to be altered and kept; the vault has no care or repair offer for the house.

## Today
Trunk shows in Europe, the Americas and Japan [^1]. The archive ledgers are a working record. The Gentleman's Journal page on the dinner jacket's history returned a 404 and is not used for claims [^4].

## Design
A classic English cut with less padding, and the dinner suit as its best-known invention [^2][^3]. Design score reflects taste, not tailoring skill alone.

## Depth
