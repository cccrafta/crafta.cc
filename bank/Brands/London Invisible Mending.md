## In One Line
A London repair business that rebuilds holes in woven cloth with thread taken from the garment's own hem; it is the clearest repair-as-practice service in the vault.

## Lineage
Michael Norman runs it as a second business. He found skilled weavers by calling two old colleagues who still practised the technique.[^1] The technique is French or invisible reweaving, which rebuilds warp and weft by hand. No founding year is recorded.[^1] Permanent Style puts it in a context of cheaper ready-to-wear and synthetics making repair uneconomical, and notes a US reweaver that suspended service when its weaver retired.[^1]

## Making and Material
Threads are harvested from internal seams or hems, so the repair matches the cloth. It works on woven fabrics only, not synthetics. About 75% of repairs are moth holes.[^1] A cigarette-sized hole costs from GBP 90 and takes about six hours of work, with about three weeks' turnaround.[^1]

## Patina and Care
The service is the care: it keeps a garment in use rather than replacing it. Permanent Style names two London alternatives, the British Invisible Mending Service (Thayer Street) and The Valet (Piccadilly Arcade).[^1] Ageing of the repaired cloth is not described.

## Today
Drop-off is at Graham Browne, 4 Bow Lane, or by collection across London.[^1] The work depends on a small number of weavers who still hold the skill.[^1]

## Design
There is no garment to judge; the craft is the product. Design is a taste score, and the owner's interest in repair and in work that leaves no trace puts it at a neutral 3 because the score is for garments, not services.

## Depth
A single source explains the method, cost and market. A rabbit hole is already saved: repair with the garment's own thread.[^2] Worth following: how many weavers remain, and how reweaving compares with visible repair such as sashiko.
