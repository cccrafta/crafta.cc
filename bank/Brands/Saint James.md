## In One Line
A Normandy knitter and one of the three principal French makers of the Breton top, with a long record of supplying the naval shirt. The vault holds no Saint James own-site source, so most claims come via Heddels and the Breton research note.

## Lineage
The French Navy introduced the Breton shirt in 1858 with 21 white stripes of 20 mm and 21 blue stripes of 10 mm on the torso.[^1] Heddels says Saint James was established as the main manufacturer in the late 19th century; the Breton research note dates its Normandy knitting to 1889, a date held in a research note and not in a source note.[^1] Orcival names Saint James, Armor Lux and itself as the three principal manufacturers.[^2]

## Making and Material
The Breton research note says Saint James still produces on original principles in Normandy; no source note holds this, and cloth, yarn weight and loom detail are not in the vault.[^1] [unsourced] The marinière is a heavy cotton jersey in the common account.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault.

## Today
The research note lists Saint James and Armor Lux as still producing on original principles.[^1] Orcival is named as one of three principal manufacturers alongside it.[^2] Coco Chanel took the stripe into fashion in 1917, which sits in the trade context, not in Saint James's own history.[^1]

## Design
The navy stripe knit with a boat neck, specified by the Navy in 1858.[^1] Scored on taste: a naval garment with documented function and little decoration.

## Depth
