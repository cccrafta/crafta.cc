## In One Line
A French workwear maker credited with the first standardised bleu de travail in 1844, which makes it a reference for the origins of the chore coat.

## Lineage
Heddels says that in 1844 Adolphe Lafont created the first standardised bleu de travail [^1]. The vault's Bleu de Travail idea note goes further, calling it "possibly the oldest surviving workwear brand in the world" and grouping it with Le Laboureur and Vetra; that is the note's own wording, not a sourced finding. The May 2026 weekly briefing calls Le Laboureur and Lafont "the T1 makers", which is a claim from the vault's own briefing and not a source. Continuity of the current company is not documented.

## Making and Material
The cloth is moleskin, with warp and weft dyed the same colour [^1]. Colour coded the trade: black for carpenters, white for painters, blue for mechanics and drivers [^1]. The West German Army used dyed moleskin [^1]. No source covers current production, factories or construction details.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault.

## Today
Heddels says Bill Cunningham popularised the chore coat in the US [^1]. No source in the vault describes what the company makes now.

## Design
The garment is the plain, boxy work jacket in a single cloth colour. The colour code, not decoration, carries the design.

## Depth
A research note on the bleu de travail, the moleskin chain and the trade colour code would be new. The Moleskin research and post cover the cloth; the brand is a small part of it.
