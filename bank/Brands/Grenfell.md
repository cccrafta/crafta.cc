## In One Line
A dense, uncoated Egyptian cotton cloth developed in Burnley in 1923 for Arctic and expedition use, now cut and sewn into outerwear in the brand's own east London factory. It is the clearest vault case of a fabric designed to a written performance brief.

## Lineage
Sir Wilfred Grenfell lectured in Burnley in 1922 at the invitation of mill-owner Walter Haythornthwaite and remarked on how hard it was to find suitable Arctic cloth; the cloth was produced in 1923 after a year of experiments, and Grenfell suggested the name.[^1] The stated requirements were light, strong, weatherproof, windproof, and able to let body moisture out, which the page calls the most important.[^1] A marketing company, Haythornthwaite & Sons Ltd, was formed in 1931 to sell the cloth and garments.[^1] Haythornthwaite stopped making garments in 1991; a Japanese company then held the brand for a period before the Azam family bought it back (dates differ between sources; the archive entry is thin).[^4] The ownership gap is why Lineage is not 5.

## Making and Material
Garments are made in Grenfell's own factory in Lea Bridge, E17, a former Met Police forensics lab.[^2][^3] Managing Director Mo Azam's family has run London outerwear factories since 1984, making coats for Katharine Hamnett, Casely-Hayford, Burberry (about 400 a week), Aquascutum and Daks.[^3] The cloth is spun from "the finest Egyptian Cotton", needed specially strengthened looms, and was so naturally waterproof that dyeing it was almost impossible; the maker's pages give no thread count.[^1] It is a cotton, no-coating, high-density weave relying on closeness of weave for windproofing and breathability, the same principle later associated with Ventile.[^1] Who weaves the cloth today is not stated.[^2]

## Today
Garments are made in east London under Azam family ownership.[^2] The Harrington research note lists Grenfell cloth as the alternative origin of the windproof-breathable idea behind the Harrington jacket; see In the Vault. The brand also appears in the safari jacket research as a present-day maker.

## Depth
The maker's history page lists expedition use: a Grenfell tent pitched at 27,000 feet on Everest in 1933, where F. S. Smythe slept 13 hours; Everest again in 1936; Oxford Ellesmere Land 1934/5; British Graham Land 1934/7; the 1954 Daily Mail Abominable Snowman expedition.[^1] The pages do not mention Everest 1924, Shackleton, Antarctica or the RAF, so those claims found elsewhere stay unconfirmed by the maker.[^1] Everest 1953 and Admiral Byrd come only via Mo Azam in The Rake [single-source, brand-sourced].[^3] A comparison of Grenfell, Ventile and Baracuta cloth is an open idea.
