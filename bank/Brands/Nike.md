## In One Line
Sportswear maker the vault meets in two places: a 2003 synthetic rugby jersey that replaced cotton, and a 2026 split-toe sneaker with SKIMS; strong on engineered fabric, weak on documented making.

## Lineage
No source in the vault gives Nike's founding. [unsourced] Founded in 1964 in the US and named Nike in 1971. The vault's reasons for caring are its fabric work and its place in the tabi story.

## Making and Material
In 2003 Nike supplied Dri-Fit jerseys to England, France and South Africa after a two-year consultation: 62% cotton, 34% polyester and 4% spandex, 154g lighter than cotton when dry and 362g lighter when wet, with mesh panels and rubber grip prints.[^1] The museum's account contrasts it with a cotton jersey that "retained up to 3kg of water" in wet conditions.[^1] The NikeSKIMS Mesh Rift, on sale from 26 January 2026, is a split-toe design with a breathable mesh upper and suede, with "a similar base design to the popular Nike Air Rift".[^2] Factories are not recorded.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault.

## Today
Hypebae reports the NikeSKIMS Mesh Rift in Black, Velvet Brown and Archaeo Brown, sold through Nike, SKIMS and select retailers.[^2] Hypebae says nothing about a separate relaunch of the original Air Rift.[^2] An idea note links the Rift to barefoot-running thinking and to other split-toe sport shoes.[^3]

## Design
The documented work is functional: lighter wet weight, mesh panels, grip. The Mesh Rift is a celebrity-brand collaboration around an old shape. Design is a taste score; the rugby jersey is strong, the 2026 shoe is a trend vehicle.

## Depth
A national-team jersey with a published fabric split and a shoe with a longer back-story in the tabi notes. Missing: how Nike's fabric work changed other sports kit, and any maker-authored account.
