## In One Line
A US textile mill group that is taking over the looms of the closed Cone Mills White Oak plant to restart American selvedge; the vault holds one reference on duck canvas history and trade-feed items on the loom move.

## Lineage
A canvas history says mills such as Mount Vernon Mill in Maryland increased duck canvas production in the mid-1800s [^1]. The page does not link that mill to the Trion, Georgia group, so the connection is not asserted here. The company's founding date is not in the vault.

## Making and Material
Duck canvas is a tightly woven cotton cloth first used for sails and tents, which starts stiff and softens with wear [^1]. A Heddels-based feed note for 25 April 2026 says Mount Vernon acquired all 45 Draper X3 shuttle looms from the Cone Mills White Oak plant (closed December 2017), moved them to Trion, Georgia with KaKa Cotton, and expected first selvedge rolls "in weeks" per president Bill Rogers [unsourced]. The same deal reportedly includes 45 Picanol President shuttle looms that could allow a Cramerton Army Cloth revival [unsourced]. These come from a weekly briefing summary, not a source note.

## Patina and Care
Canvas softens with wear and its smooth surface resists snagging, per the canvas history [^1]. No source covers how Mount Vernon denim ages.

## Today
A feed note of 10 October 2026 says a Wrangler x imogene + willie collaboration uses deadstock Cone White Oak selvedge in its first drop and "broken twill" from Mount Vernon Mills in later drops [unsourced].
