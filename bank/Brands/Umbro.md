## In One Line
English sportswear maker, founded 1924, that dominated club football kit before the 1970s and turns up in Patagonia's own origin story through an imported rugby shirt.

## Lineage
Umbro was established in 1924 by Harold and Wallace Humphrey.[^1] Together with Bukta it dominated English club kit supply before the 1970s.[^1] Umbro and Admiral both sold child-size Manchester United kits in 1973/74, the point at which the replica shirt became a retail product.[^1]

## Making and Material
No source describes Umbro's fabrics or factories. The 1970 rugby shirt that Yvon Chouinard brought back was heavy cotton.[^2]

## Today
The Westminster Menswear Archive holds a large Umbro group following a 2024 centenary exhibition.[^4]

## Design
Kit was designed for the body in play, then reappropriated as leisurewear. The vault holds no design analysis of Umbro's own shirts.[^1]

## Depth
Four Reference notes: a sport-history paper, two Patagonia origin accounts, and an archive record. The colour of the rugby shirt is disputed: Heddels gives yellow-blue, Patagonia's page gives blue with red and yellow stripes.[^2][^3]
