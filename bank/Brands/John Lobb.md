## In One Line
This note covers John Lobb Ltd, the family-owned St James's Street bespoke bootmaker, not the separate Hermes-owned John Lobb brand. For Crafta it is a London apprenticeship and last-making lineage.

## Lineage
The firm describes itself as "the original British bootmaker since 1849", run by one family for more than 170 years.[^1] Founder John Lobb (1829-1895) was a Cornish farm boy who won awards at Victorian international exhibitions and held a Royal Warrant from Edward, Prince of Wales.[^1] William Lobb (1902-1963) created the Double Monk; John Hunter Lobb (b. 1940) is chairman and managing director and a highly influential last maker.[^1] The London brands research notes a date conflict: the maker says 1849, while some sources date the London shop from 1866 (see In the Vault).

## Making and Material
The fifth generation is in the business: William John Lobb (director 2002), Jonathan Hunter Lobb (Director of Production) and Nicholas David Lobb (strategy); apprentices rotate through finishing, closing, clicking, making and last making.[^1] The page does not say where current production takes place.[^1] Carréducker's founder James Ducker apprenticed at John Lobb in St James's and became a full shoemaker there, so the workshop trains makers who go on to start their own houses.[^2] Leathers and construction methods are not stated in the vault.

## Today
One shop at 9 St James's Street SW1A 1EF, run by the family.[^1] It is independent of the Hermes-owned John Lobb brand.[^1]

## Design
