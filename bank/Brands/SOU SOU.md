## In One Line
Japanese label that revived the jika-tabi work shoe and works with a named Hyogo factory; most of what the vault knows comes from the company's own press story, so the figures are the company's claims.

## Lineage
SOU SOU's PR TIMES story says 98% of jika-tabi sold in Japan are made abroad and only three Japanese producers remain.[^1] ABC News repeats "a dozen makers once, now three major ones", but the Margiela tabi research notes the figure comes from the same company, so the two are not independent.[^2][^3] The story says a first New York buyer was reportedly a Ralph Lauren director.[^1] No founding date appears. [unsourced] The label started in Kyoto in the early 2000s.

## Making and Material
The story names Takasago Sangyo of Hyogo, a running-shoe maker, as the main factory, producing about 20,000 pairs a year.[^1] Takasago first refused the project, and its chairman Kako Akiharu then backed it.[^1] Construction is the jika-tabi form: split toe, rubber sole. Upper cloth is not described in the vault.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. [unsourced] Rubber soles and cotton uppers wear at the sole and big-toe seam.

## Today
ABC News says about half the clientele is overseas.[^2] The story shows demand from Western buyers, which the research on the last tabi makers examines.[^3]

## Design
The shoe keeps the work form and applies printed textiles, which is a traditional silhouette with a modern surface. A factory partnership with a performance-shoe maker is the interesting part. Design is a taste score: Japanese craft and a rescued working shoe match the owner's profile.

## Depth
A company press story, one ABC News piece, a research note and an idea note. The independent evidence is thin.
