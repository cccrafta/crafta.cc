## In One Line
Abrasion-resistant nylon brand first launched by DuPont; the vault holds only a Wikipedia entry, so most of this note is provisional.

## Lineage
DuPont introduced the name in 1929, first for rayon, per the Wikipedia entry; a 1948 DuPont advertisement cites tyre cord from 1928, and nylon versions date from about 1966 [^1]. Invista, owned by Koch, holds the brand now [^1]. All of this is a T4 source and has not been checked against DuPont or Invista records.

## Making and Material
The common 420D nylon 6,6 construction is cited, with marketing multiples of 2x to 4x abrasion resistance that the entry flags as unverified [^1]. Weave and weight are missing from the source. Eastpak is named as a major customer [^1].

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. Heavy nylon is known to scuff and fray at edges rather than soften [unsourced].

## Today
The brand is licensed as a branded fabric to bag, workwear and outdoor makers [unsourced beyond the Eastpak mention]. The vault's workwear and sportswear research treats it as an example of fabric chosen for abrasion, not feel.

## Design
Design here means the cloth: a plain, hard-faced nylon that does one job. Taste score 3 for a fabric that is functional but not interesting to look at, while the story of a brand ingredient is.

## Depth
One T4 source. A real note would need DuPont archive material, Invista's own specification sheets, and a mill-side explanation of the yarn.
