## In One Line
An Alabama knitwear maker founded in 1902 that pitched an all-cotton practice jersey and made crew-neck sweatshirts from 1930; it now sits in Heddels' entry tier of sweatshirts.

## Lineage
Russell's own history says Benjamin Russell founded it on 3 March 1902 in Alexander City, Alabama, with 10 sewing machines and 8 knitting machines [^1]. It dates the all-cotton practice jersey, pitched by Benny Russell, to 1926 and crew-neck sweatshirt production to 1930 [^1]. Heddels credits the blueprint to Benjamin Russell Jr. in 1920, and Wikipedia also says 1920 [^1] [^2]. The date conflict, 1920 or 1926, is kept as stated.

## Making and Material
The maker's history describes cotton jersey as an alternative to wool for athletes [^1]. The company was renamed Russell Mills in 1962 and Russell Corporation in 1973, and Fruit of the Loom acquired it in 2006 [^1]. No source in the vault describes current factory, fabric weight or construction.

No source in the vault.

## Today
Heddels lists Russell in the entry tier of sweatshirts, under $70, where the tier trades on price and not on loopwheel or heavy fabric [^2]. The brand now sits within Fruit of the Loom's ownership, which the vault does not follow beyond the 2006 purchase [^1].
