## In One Line
American house the vault meets as the maker of fashion rugby shirts and the 1972 piqué polo, and as the parent of RRL; no source documents how its main line is made.

## Lineage
Heddels says the rugby revival of the 1960s and 70s made the shirt a staple of Ivy League and preppy style, and that Ralph Lauren "has made fashion rugby shirts since".[^1] The Workwear and Sportswear research records the 1972 Ralph Lauren piqué polo after the 1926 to 27 short-sleeve shirt and Izod's 1951 US licence.[^2] No founding date appears in vault sources. [unsourced] Founded in 1967.

## Making and Material
No source in the vault describes where or how the main line is made. The one material fact is piqué for the 1972 polo.[^2]

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. [unsourced] Rugby shirts and polos in cotton fade and soften with wear.

## Today
SOU SOU's company story says its first buyer in New York was reportedly a Ralph Lauren director, a company claim about its own sales.[^3] RRL, the sub-brand, appears separately in vault lists and has its own note.

## Design
A fashion rugby shirt is the sport garment kept for its look; Heddels says the heritage version is now a separate garment from the sport one.[^1] Design is a taste score: the main line is built on cachet and not on construction logic the owner values, so a low score.

## Depth
