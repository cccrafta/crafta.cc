## In One Line
Manchester maker of the G9, the jacket that became the Harrington. The brand is the originator of the pattern, though the vault has conflicting dates and a production location that is no longer plainly British.

## Lineage
The G9 dates from 1937, made by John and Isaac Miller in Manchester, who had been making raincoats for Burberry and Aquascutum; G9 stands for Golf and 9 holes [^1]. Fraser tartan lining was approved in 1938; the chieftain count conflicts, 24th (Heddels, The Rake) against 25th (aggregate note) [^1][^3][^4]. The G4 was made in 1944 [^1]. The "Harrington" name came through John Simons: 1965 in the aggregate note, 1966 in Heddels [^1][^3]. Not London; the London brands note marks it "Not London (Manchester)".

## Making and Material
Construction is specific: umbrella back yoke with a built-in vent, raglan sleeves, deep pockets for golf balls and a two-button dog-ear collar [^2]. The original cloth was high-thread-count cotton; modern versions are 50/50 poly-cotton with a Coolmax tartan lining, and "Baracuta Cloth" is proprietary [^1][^2]. Production is contested: Stuarts says "Designed and made in the UK since 1937" and also "Made in England, though production now takes place mainly in Europe, including Italy" [^2].

## Patina and Care
No care or reproofing offer in the vault. A poly-cotton blend with a tartan lining should wear differently from the original high-count cotton `[inference]`.

## Today
Contemporary Harrington Makers 2026 calls Baracuta the "gold standard" and "the originator" [^5]. Private White V.C. makes a Ventile Harrington in Manchester as a rival interpretation [^6]. Related ideas in the vault cover the G4, the McGregor Drizzler, John Simons and Grenfell against Ventile against Baracuta cloth.

## Design
The G9 is a clean pattern: short blouson, plain collar, tartan lining; the design solved a golf swing and a showery day. The shape is widely copied.

## Depth
Two disputed facts (Harrington naming date, chieftain number), a proprietary cloth, and the G4 and Drizzler relatives. The Harrington research note and post already use it.
