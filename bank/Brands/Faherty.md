## In One Line
A US lifestyle brand that appears in the vault for a blog on CPO history and a Circulose commitment, with no production record.

## Lineage
No source gives its founding. [unsourced] It is a 2010s American casual brand. The brand's CPO history blog says the Navy CPO shirt appeared in the 1930s, was adopted after WWII by fishermen and dock workers, and became popular outdoors in the 1960s-70s, with Pendleton, L.L. Bean and Eddie Bauer adopting the silhouette [^1]. That is the brand's own account of the garment, not of itself.

## Making and Material
The blog says modern Faherty versions use canvas and loopback knit in place of wool [^1]. No source names factories or mills.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault.

## Today
FashionUnited lists Faherty among the brands added in December 2025 to the Circulose commitments [^2]. That is a commitment, not output.

## Design
The CPO shirt changes the cloth from wool melton to canvas and knit, so it keeps the silhouette without the weather function.

## Depth
The CPO blog is a useful secondary account for the CPO research, to be set against Cockpit USA and Freenote. The brand has no archive.
