## In One Line
A Hawick knitwear house dated to 1815 that closed its Hawick plant in 2008; the vault records its history and its place in British football casual dress, with no current construction or material record.

## Lineage
A Hawick history note says Pringle was founded in 1815 by Robert Pringle and began as an undergarment manufacturer [^1]. It places the house in a town where organised hosiery dates to 1771 and where 250 woollen mills stood by the 1870s [^1]. The note says the firm of Joseph Dawson, who invented a mechanical cashmere de-hairing process in the 1870s, later acquired Pringle and Ballantyne [^1]. The note's publication field names "multiple sources", so the claims are medium reliability.

## Making and Material
Pringle's Hawick plant shut in 2008 [^1]. Survivors of the Hawick cashmere trade include William Lockie, Johnstons of Elgin and Barrie Knitwear [^1]. The vault does not say where Pringle knitwear is made now, which yarn it uses or how it is knitted.

## Today
Contemporary British History records Pringle sweaters in the late 1970s to 1980s casual culture: memoir examples include Cardiff fans in Pringle and Burberry, and Pringle sweaters replacing press trousers on the terraces [^2]. The article frames casual dress as "high-end European sportswear and designer clothing", not worn-out training kit, and does not say the garments were functional [^2]. No current range or price is documented.
