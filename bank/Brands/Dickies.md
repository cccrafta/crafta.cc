## In One Line
A US work-trouser brand that appears in the vault only in climbing forums and a product pick, as the cheap workwear that climbers actually wear.

## Lineage
No source in the vault covers origin. [unsourced] Dickies is a Texas workwear company dating to the early 20th century. The vault's Workwear and Sportswear research records "Not found: any 1960s-70s source naming Carhartt, Dickies or Levi's in climbing".

## Making and Material
No source on factories or cloth. A UKClimbing thread says a Dickies Redhawk user found the trousers heavy, with no stretch and uncomfortable zips that "stay wet once wet", and preferred Craghoppers Kiwi stretch [^2].

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault.

## Today
The Gear Patrol piece on 1970s climbing style gives only a modern product pick (painter's pants, $22), with no 1970s Dickies claim [^1]. UKClimbing says Carhartts or Dickies are common climbing trousers in the US [^2]. The Mountain Project 2011 thread shows an original poster planning to try Dickies [^3].

## Design
Plain work trousers. The sources value them for price and toughness, not for cut.

## Depth
Little to study beyond the climbing-trouser thread: weight, stretch, zips and wet behaviour are the only documented properties, from forum anecdotes.
