## In One Line
A German sport-shoe maker. The vault holds it only as one side of an unresolved question: who designed the West German Army trainer, adidas or Puma. Nothing here describes Puma's own making.

## Lineage
In the late 1970s West Germany sought new army training shoes, and adidas and Puma both bid; about 500,000 soldiers had been issued the shoe by 1989.[^1] Per Luke Leitch's reading of Bundeswehr History Museum records, the initial prototypes were proposed by Puma; Puma says it has no records of a shoe made for the government.[^1][^2] adidas says it designed the BW-Sport and the Dresden museum "has no records of the Three Stripes"; German Wikipedia says adidas received the production contract in 1982.[^1][^2][^3] The conflict is kept open: Heddels notes that the Bundeswehr may have written the spec and used several contractors.[^4]

## Making and Material
The vault documents the GAT, not Puma production. The white indoor model resembles the adidas Samba and was made at the ZDA Partizanske plant in Czechoslovakia (T4 source); the blue outdoor model resembles the Puma Easy Rider II in its rubber studs and midsole dimples, but the article does not say Puma made it.[^3] [unsourced] Puma's own shoe construction and materials are not covered.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. Heddels notes old surplus GAT pairs can have unstable soles, which says nothing about Puma products.[^4]

## Today
