## In One Line
Furniture company that sells Dieter Rams's shelving and publishes his ten principles of good design; it matters to the vault as the source of a design vocabulary that Patagonia's founder cites, not as a garment maker.

## Lineage
Vitsœ's own page says Rams began a storage system in 1957 that became Vitsœ, founded 1959.[^1] The company is the maker's channel for Rams's principles: long-lasting, honest, and "as little design as possible".[^1] The Workwear and Sportswear research verifies the Rams principles and records that Chouinard cites Rams per secondary quotation, which is a second-hand link.[^2]

## Making and Material
No source in the vault describes how or where the shelving is made, or in what material. [unsourced] Anodised aluminium and plywood are used in the shelving system.

## Patina and Care
The principle "long-lasting" says a product should not be fashionable and should outlive trends, which speaks to durability, not to a documented ageing process.[^1] No repair or resale offer is recorded in the vault.

## Today
Vitsœ is current: it publishes the principles on its own site.[^1] No trusted outlet in the vault covers the company or its products.

## Design
Principle 7 says good design "avoids being fashionable", and the page frames design as honest and unobtrusive.[^1] The principles are a design position, written by the maker, and are not proof that a product meets them. Design is a taste score: function-led and anti-cachet, close to the owner's profile, but about furniture and not clothing.
