## In One Line
A Manchester jacket maker that sews in its own factory from almost entirely UK-sourced materials; it matters to Crafta because the Ventile Harrington and the parachute-cloth collaboration both turn military cloth into civilian outerwear.

## Lineage
The maker is named after Private Jack White V.C., and its Harrington listing says it was "first stitched in the 1950s and still made in Manchester" [^1]. The founding year is in conflict: the publication note gives 1853, while the Safari Jacket research records the factory as "~1900s" and "100+ years" [unsourced]. Both are kept as stated; neither is confirmed by a dated primary document.

## Making and Material
Jackets are made in the maker's own Manchester factory with almost exclusively UK-sourced materials [^1]. The Harrington uses Ventile, a 100% cotton cloth of 205 gsm, with RIRI copper hardware [^1]. The Raeburn collaboration is a reversible Ventile and parachute field jacket made at the same factory from 23 surplus air force parachutes [^2]. A 2020 moleskin bomber appears in the Moleskin research note [unsourced].

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault on how Ventile or the moleskin ages. [unsourced]

## Today
A 2026 round-up lists Private White V.C. among the heritage British Harrington makers, in Manchester with a premium finish [^3]. The Raeburn collaboration shows continued use of surplus cloth, limited by the parachute stock [^2].

## Design
Close to the military pattern: a Harrington cut in Ventile and a field jacket with a reversible shell [^1] [^2]. The material chain is short and named.
