## In One Line
A cloth designer and tailor working from an East London studio, with tweed woven by UK commission weavers. It treats tweed as Britain's original sportswear and tests that idea with reflective yarn.

## Lineage
Founded by photographer Guy Hills and weaver Kirsty McDougall in an East London weave studio; the maker's About Us gives 2006 [^1]. A secondary source says 2002, and Drapers records the tenth anniversary in 2016, which fits neither date cleanly; the conflict stays open [^1][^2]. The first delivery to Anderson & Sheppard came in 2006 [^2]. The flagship opened in Mayfair in 2014 and moved to 47 Dorset Street, Marylebone, in 2018 [^2].

## Making and Material
Cloth is woven in the UK, mainly by commission weavers. Drapers names Drove Weaving in Langholm, part of Lochcarron of Scotland, with runs of 60-600 m [^2]. McDougall develops swatches on a 24-shaft sample handloom and the house shows two collections a year [^2]. Most ready-to-wear is made in Tottenham [^2]. The maker frames tweed as the first British sportswear [^1].

## Patina and Care
No source on how the cloth ages or on repair.

## Today
The studio wove reflective 3M yarn with British and Shetland wool for Fred Perry; the idea came from the paint on double yellow lines [^2]. A co-branded Converse high-top sold 30,000 pairs in 2011 [^2]. Anderson & Sheppard remains a cloth customer in the vault's record [^2].

## Design
Pattern play on conservative cloth: the reflective yarn keeps the tweed hand while adding a function a road cyclist can use. The collaborations lean on pop cultural names; the cloth underneath is traditional.

## Depth
Two documents, one maker and one trade profile, plus an idea note on tweed as function-first cloth. Weights, yarn counts and mill agreements are not in the vault.
