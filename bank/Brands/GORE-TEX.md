## In One Line
The waterproof breathable membrane that outdoor and some tailoring brands buy in as a laminate. The vault covers the move from PTFE to ePE (expanded polyethylene), almost entirely through the maker's own claims.

## Lineage
Founder W. L. Gore & Associates and the original 1970s PTFE membrane are `[unsourced]`. The vault's thread is the shift to an ePE membrane [^1].

## Making and Material
Gore describes the new laminate as built on an ePE membrane, PFAS-free* ("made without intentionally added PFAS; may contain trace amounts"), including the DWR, with a reduced carbon footprint per Higg MSI from a lower-mass membrane [^1]. Its performance wording is qualitative: "Durably Waterproof", "Very Breathable", "Totally Windproof" [^1]. Live For The Outdoors found no noticeable difference in Patagonia and Mountain Equipment gear, "a bit thinner", and noted Gore "isn't eliminating PFCs completely" and aimed to be majority ePE "by sometime in 2025" [^2]. The Powder page is a stub (HTTP 403), so its claims are unverified [^5].

## Patina and Care
A membrane does not patina; shells lose DWR beading and are reproofed. The vault's DWR idea note covers why PFAS-free finishes stop beading; no care source in the vault otherwise.

## Today
adidas Terrex ePE products are listed in Trek & Mountain [^3]. Henry Poole offers a Travel Trench coat made with GORE-TEX [^4]. Gore's product page lists GearJunkie 2023 Gear of the Year and The Great Outdoors 2024 Best Innovation (Brand) [^1].

## Design
A material, scored on the cloth it makes possible: a laminate that sits inside a shell and lets tailoring or sport design stay conventional.

## Depth
ePE versus PTFE, the PFAS-free asterisk, the DWR problem and the mass-balance question are four separate research threads already seeded as Ideas.
