## In One Line
A Wisconsin overall maker founded in 1895 that moved from railroad workwear to children's clothing; the vault holds no Reference note, only lines in the bib overall research that rest on Wikipedia and company histories.

## Lineage
The Bib Overall research note says the company was founded in 1895 in Oshkosh, Wisconsin as Grove Manufacturing Company, making hickory-striped denim bib overalls for railroad workers and farmers [unsourced]. It adopted the "B'Gosh" tagline in 1911, borrowed from a vaudeville routine [unsourced]. In the 1960s and 70s it moved to children's apparel after a catalogue drew 10,000 requests for children's versions, and gradually left the workwear market [unsourced]. All three lines cite Wikipedia and company histories in [[Research/The Bib Overall]], which the research note rates T2 or T3.

## Making and Material
The hickory stripe is a blue and white vertical stripe in cotton twill, an alternative to plain denim; the research note says OshKosh built its name on it for railroad workers [unsourced]. It cites a 1929 Sears catalogue description of hickory stripe as a lighter summer cloth, without naming a source note for it [unsourced]. No mill, factory or construction detail is documented.
