## In One Line
A Paris couture house whose flou and tailleur ateliers show a working split between soft and structured making, and whose menswear appears in the vault only through the German Army Trainer trend.

## Lineage
Founded 1946 [unsourced]. The vault's evidence is the house museum's account of two couture ateliers: flou makes soft dresses and tailleur makes structured outfits, which needs "a different technique" [^1].

## Making and Material
Dior has one flou and one tailleur haute couture atelier, each of about 30 people [^1]. A toile in plain cotton canvas must be approved by the artistic director [^1]. The vault has no source on ready-to-wear or menswear production, or on cloth.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault.

## Today
Highsnobiety says Kim Jones's last Dior menswear collection (Fall 2025) revived a GAT-like silhouette [^3]. Hypebeast lists Dior Homme among labels making their own GAT versions [^2].

## Design
The ateliers' toile-first process is documented; the current menswear is documented only as a trend entry. The house's design is scored on the owner's taste, which does not value luxury cachet.

## Depth
La Galerie Dior is a primary archive on atelier method. The "Tailoring and Flou" idea note follows how Margiela collapsed that split. The ateliers are the research asset, not the brand's product line.
