## In One Line
Swedish recycled-cellulose pulp brand that took over Renewcell's plant; the vault's case study in how a material brand survives bankruptcy on licensing and brand commitments.

## Lineage
Renewcell went bankrupt in February 2024, and Altor bought it in June 2024 and renamed it Circulose [^2] [^3]. The Trellis analysis says Renewcell's product cost about twice its alternatives, H&M had committed $29M, and capsule collections rarely converted to store lines [^3]. The 2024 founding year in the frontmatter marks the renaming, not the technology.

## Making and Material
The Sundsvall factory, called by the company the world's first commercial-scale chemical textile recycling facility, makes pulp from cotton textile waste [^1]. FashionUnited reports the Ortviken plant would restart in Q4 2026 aiming at 60,000 t a year [^2]. Spinnova trialled the pulp in 2023, without published volumes [^4]. A Textile Focus piece sets out the barriers to textile-to-textile recycling [^5].

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. The product is pulp for fibre, so wear behaviour depends on the garment maker.

## Today
On 24 June 2026 the company named four new brands (EILEEN FISHER, Marimekko, Samsøe Samsøe, King Louie) under a licensing model, with no volumes stated [^1]. FashionUnited counts 11 brands committed [^2]. Production and restart remain targets, not results.

## Design
Design here means the cloth: a viscose-like fibre from recycled cotton, rated by the vault as a promising material with unproven supply. Taste score 3.

## Depth
Five sources give a full timeline: Renewcell's failure, Altor's purchase, the restart plan, the licensing model, and the recycling barriers. The vault's open gap is any independent measure of fibre quality and cost.
