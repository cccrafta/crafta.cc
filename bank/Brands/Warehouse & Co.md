## In One Line
A Japanese reproduction maker that rebuilds American workwear and military garments from specific vintage references; it matters to Crafta for how far its detail work goes, from the cloth to the stamped buttons.

## Lineage
No founding date or founder is documented in the vault. The Heddels coverage describes it as a reproduction house, where garments follow named vintage models, including an M1940 army denim coverall [^1]. The Bib Overall research records that it produces bib overalls from specific vintage American references [unsourced].

## Making and Material
The 12 oz army denim coverall has an indigo warp and a silver-gray, sulfur-dyed weft, and Army-stamped buttons, at about $281 [^1]. This note rests on a fetch summary and is a stub, so it is thin [^1]. Heddels names Warehouse among the leading Japanese waffle-knit makers [^3] and in the end tier of sweatshirts [^2]. John Gluckow of Warehouse Co. appears in a Heddels zipper episode noted as a pitch in the Zipper idea [unsourced].

## Patina and Care
A sulfur-dyed weft against an indigo warp is chosen for how the cloth fades, per the coverall listing [^1]. No source describes how a worn pair looks.

## Today
Warehouse is current in Heddels guides to waffle knits and sweatshirts, and the Clutch Cafe is mentioned in the London Brands research [^2] [^3]. The vault holds no current catalogue.

## Design
Garments follow dated military and workwear patterns down to hardware and dye, rather than reinterpreting them [^1].

## Depth
One stub and two guide mentions, plus several vault notes that use it as the reference for reproductions. Needs a maker interview and a first-hand review.
