## In One Line
A Scottish mill on the River Lossie that makes cashmere from raw fibre to finished garment, one of three Scottish cashmere survivors named in the vault.

## Lineage
The Hawick heritage note, from Scottish Borders sources, names three survivors: William Lockie (est. 1874), Johnstons of Elgin (est. 1797) and Barrie Knitwear (est. 1903) [^1]. The vault's Cashmere research adds that it sits on the River Lossie in Elgin [^2].

## Making and Material
The Cashmere research describes Johnstons as the "only Scottish cashmere company that controls the entire process from raw fiber to finished garment", and as a supplier of yarn to major fashion houses [^2]. That claim traces to Put This On, Johnstons itself and the Barrie/Chanel Métiers d'Art coverage in the research footnotes [^2]. No weights or fibre grades are given.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault.

## Today
The vault names the mill as a living producer, with its yarn supplied to fashion houses [^2]. A "Contemporary Cashmere Makers" note is referenced in the research but was not found, so it is not linked here.

## Design
The vault has no description of the cloth's hand or look. The cloth's value is in the controlled process.

## Depth
The Cashmere research and post describe what one goat produces in a year; Johnstons sits at the process end of that chain. A research note on integrated Scottish mills against Italian ones would use this.
