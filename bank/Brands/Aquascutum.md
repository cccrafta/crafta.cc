## In One Line
London shower-proof coat house with a real nineteenth-century trade mark, now mainly a label other makers once worked for; the London Brands research rejected it for cachet with no current lineage evidence.

## Lineage
The Aquascutum name was trade-marked in 1853 for John Emery's shower-proof textile [^1]. The 1851 founding year in the frontmatter is [unsourced]. Baracuta's founders made rain coats for Burberry and Aquascutum in the mid-1930s [^2]. Michael Drake, head of menswear design, left Aquascutum in 1977 to found Drake's with two colleagues [^4].

## Making and Material
Contemporary coverage describes technical cotton fabrics with a more formal, tailored finish than Baracuta [^3]. Grenfell's East London factories made coats for Aquascutum, among others [^5]. No source names where Aquascutum coats are made today.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault.

## Today
Ape to Gentleman, quoted in the vault, rates its Harrington "every bit as good as Baracuta, if not better" [^3]. Casual-culture memoirs list Aquascutum alongside Armani, Versace and Burberry as expensive prêt-à-porter worn at a time of mass unemployment [^6].

## Design
Tailored, slightly formal take on the raincoat and the Harrington silhouette [^3]. The vault has no first-hand description of construction.

## Depth
A trade-mark date, a supplier trail (Baracuta, Grenfell) and a staff lineage (Drake's) make a small but checkable history. Subcultural adoption is documented only through the Casuals paper [^6].
