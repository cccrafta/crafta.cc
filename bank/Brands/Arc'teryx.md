## In One Line
Vancouver maker of technical mountain shells; the vault holds it only as an early adopter of the Gore-Tex ePE membrane.

## Lineage
Founded in Vancouver in 1989 by climbers [unsourced]. The vault has no lineage source.

## Making and Material
Arc'teryx had launched a Pro ePE shell by the time of the Trek & Mountain report, which says Pro and Active ePE versions from other brands were not yet available [^1]. The Powder note, a stub from a page that returned 403 and is flagged unreliable, names Arc'teryx among ePE brands and says the Beta AR was the first jacket with Gore-Tex Pro ePE [^2]. Laminated shells with welded seams and tight patterning are typical of the label [unsourced].

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. Reproofing of shells is a known care step for laminate garments [unsourced].

## Today
Sells to alpine climbers and, in the city, as outerwear [unsourced]. ePE is the PFAS-free membrane successor to the older fluoropolymer one; the vault tracks the transition in the textile-innovation research.

## Design
Clean, seam-minimal shells with articulated sleeves and low-profile hoods [unsourced]. The look is function-led and rarely decorated.

## Depth
A good case for the ePE research: how a membrane change shows in a flagship jacket, and what the DWR problem does to the claim of PFAS-free shells. Arc'teryx's own page returned empty in the vault's earlier pass [^1].

## What Trusted Sources Say
No trusted coverage in the vault beyond Trek & Mountain, which treats the Pro ePE shell as a milestone, not as a review [^1].
