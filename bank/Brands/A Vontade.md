## In One Line
Tokyo maker of reproduction-leaning military patterns; in the vault it is known for one jacket, a 9oz double-indigo denim jungle fatigue jacket. Useful as the denim counter-example to the original poplin jungle jackets.

## Lineage
Founding date, founder and history are `[unsourced]`; the vault has no lineage note. Heddels places the maker in Tokyo and says the jacket is based on 1960s-70s US subtropical jackets [^1].

## Making and Material
Made in Japan per Heddels. The jacket is a 9oz warp- and weft-dyed double-indigo denim with a concealed button front, four flap stow pockets, strap side adjusters, BDU-style elbow patches and sleeve gussets [^1]. The research note reads 9oz against the original 5.5oz poplin as about 1.6 times heavier `[inference]`. Mill and factory are not named.

## Patina and Care
No source. The Heddels article says nothing about ageing, though indigo denim fading is an obvious question for a post `[unsourced]`.

## Today
The jacket retails at $486 at Sonder Supplies [^1]. Other lines and stockists are `[unsourced]`.

## Design
Signature in the vault is the denim jungle jacket: the military pocket and gusset layout carried over into a heavier cloth. Heddels called it "contemporary, but one we can certainly get behind" [^1]. Taste score set from that one piece only.

## Depth
One jacket worth a research angle: what changes when a tropical poplin pattern is cut in 9oz denim. The idea note on indigo on ripstop covers the same question.
