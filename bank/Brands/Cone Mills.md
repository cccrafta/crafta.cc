## In One Line
The American denim mill whose White Oak plant was the last large-scale selvedge weaver in the US. It closed in 2017; its looms and deadstock are the afterlife.

## Lineage
The vault dates the White Oak plant (Greensboro, NC) closure to December 2017 and calls it the last large-scale selvedge weaving in America (Ideas notes, citing Heddels). The founding date and earlier history are `[unsourced]`.

## Making and Material
The vault's own notes say Mount Vernon Mills acquired the 45 Draper X3 shuttle looms from White Oak, via a KaKa Cotton partnership, and moved them to Trion, Georgia (April 2026 weekly briefing); the same notes also mention 45 Picanol President shuttle looms. Counts come from the vault's own feed briefings and Ideas notes, not from Reference notes.

## Patina and Care
Selvedge shuttle-loom denim fades and ages as indigo denim does `[unsourced]`; deadstock White Oak is valued because the cloth is no longer made.

## Today
A Wrangler x imogene + willie collaboration uses deadstock Cone Mills White Oak selvedge for its first drop, with later drops in broken twill from Mount Vernon Mills (October 2026 weekly briefing). The mill itself does not make new cloth.

## Design
White Oak selvedge is a reference cloth for American denim; judged on reputation, not a swatch seen.

## Depth
A closed mill with a surviving loom set and a deadstock afterlife: the White Oak Revival and American Denim Mills ideas already cover it.
