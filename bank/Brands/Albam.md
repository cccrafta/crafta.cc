## In One Line
A Soho menswear label founded in 2006 that began by building a British supply chain for basics and went on to Ventile outerwear. It earns a place for stating what English production costs.

## Lineage
Founded 2006 by Alastair Rae and James Shaw (Mr Porter gives 2007; the maker's page says 2006) [^1]. The first shop opened on Beak Street, Soho, in October 2007, per Spitalfields Life, a secondary source [^1].

## Making and Material
The Classic T-shirt started with a Loughborough knitter, a Derbyshire factory and a Leicester dye house [^1]. By 2010 Permanent Style recorded a Ventile Alpine jacket with taped seams, RiRi zips, shirts made in runs of about 70 per colour, and trousers cut by a trouser cutter with a higher darted waist [^2]. Ventile cost about GBP 20 per metre against GBP 1-2 per metre for typical hiking fabrics [^2]. Search summaries say T-shirts and shirts are now made in Portugal and outerwear in British factories; the maker's page does not confirm it [single-source on current split] [^1].

## Patina and Care
No source.

## Today
Heddels lists Albam among brands manufacturing in England [^3]. A 2026 round-up of Harrington makers describes Albam's version in linen blend or cotton canvas with off-white zip tape and welted hand-warmer pockets [^4]. The Barbour jungle jacket research found no denim jungle jacket from Albam.

## Design
Plain, workwear-derived pieces cut with care: a darted trouser waist, a fisherman's cagoule iterated season by season [^2]. Good-looking versions of known garments; the signature detail is the off-white zip tape on the Harrington [^4].

## Depth
One 2010 visit and a founding page. Worth a follow-up on the Ventile Alpine jacket and the current factory split, since the Permanent Style article is dated.
