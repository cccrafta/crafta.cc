## In One Line
London menswear designer who builds collections from his Jamaican and Scottish family history, using British mill cloth where it exists; the V&A Dundee holds a jute-and-cotton bomber from his 2017 Juteopolis collection.

## Lineage
Daley grew up in Leicester. His father is Jamaican-British and his mother Scottish; his parents ran a reggae club in Edinburgh in the late 1970s and early 1980s, his Jamaican grandfather was a shoemaker and his mother's family were knitters. He graduated from Central Saint Martins menswear in 2013 with Culture Clash, mixing Caribbean cool, Scottish craft and Japanese silhouettes.[^1] The 2017 Juteopolis collection points to his Dundonian family and the Dundee jute mills.[^2]

## Making and Material
W Magazine records tweed and cloth from UK mills, plus British headwear makers and shoe manufacturers.[^1] When many UK mills closed in the pandemic he made much of the Stepping Razor collection in Japan with smaller suppliers outside Tokyo, and Beams ordered pieces early on.[^1] The V&A Dundee describes the Juteopolis bomber as jute and cotton.[^2] No named mill or workshop is recorded.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. No repair offer is documented.

## Today
V&A Dundee shows the Juteopolis jacket, lent by Daley, in a decolonisation series by dress historian Dr Sally Tuckett tracing Dundee's jute trade to the cloth sent to the colonies to clothe enslaved people.[^2] This is the museum's reading of the object, not a claim made by the maker.

## Design
Hats are cut oversize "to fit a big fro or a lot of dreads", a specific fit decision for a wearer rather than a trend.[^1] Design is a taste score: Japanese silhouettes, mill-led British cloth and a maker's reason behind each cut sit well with the owner's profile, but only one jacket is described in the vault.
