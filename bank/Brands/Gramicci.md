## In One Line
Californian climbing-pant maker whose diamond gusset and webbing belt make it the vault's cleanest case of a garment built for the body under load.

## Lineage
Gramicci says it began in 1982 in a garage in Ventura, started by Mike Graham, a Stonemaster [^1]. The name comes from the 1974 staged ascent by a climber called "Michelangelo Gramicci" [^1]. Graham had been a Chouinard Equipment contractor from 1978 [^3]. The origin conflicts across sources: the maker says Ventura, the vault's research notes retailers say Yosemite, and the maker's second page places the garage in Oxnard and blurs 1982 with a "40 years since first pair" claim [^2].

## Making and Material
The maker describes "hard-wearing canvas, inspired by the work-wear world", and says Stonemasters earlier wore painter pants, jeans and OG fatigues [^2]. The diamond gusset was "found on a pair of kung-fu trousers" to allow 180-degree movement, a maker claim; the one patent in the vault, US4649574, was filed in 1986 and granted 1987, so it post-dates Gramicci and does not show who invented the gusset [^1] [^5]. The nylon webbing belt is modelled on backpack straps and adjusts with one hand [^1].

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault on how the pants age.

## Today
Surfers and skaters adopted the pants by the mid-1980s and Japan by the early 1990s through Beams and Oshmans [^2] [^4]. Houyhnhnm notes the NN-Shorts (2014) and NN-Pants (2015) with a snug tapered silhouette [^4]. REI listed the label as its top non-house brand in 1998 and sales reached $27M in 1999, when Graham left [^3].
