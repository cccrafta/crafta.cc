## In One Line
Boston sporting goods maker whose 1901 catalogue records wool football jerseys and no cotton sweatshirt, which is the baseline the vault uses for what athletic clothing looked like before the sweatshirt.

## Lineage
The UMass chapter dates Wright & Ditson to Boston, 1871, bought out by the Spalding brothers in 1891, with the name continuing into the 1940s.[^2] The Crafta post on the sweatshirt uses its catalogue as the pre-cotton reference.[^3] The vault records no founders' names.

## Making and Material
The 1901 catalogue sells football jerseys of Australian wool "knit to shape of body and arms", and the Workwear research quotes its claim that the knit is elastic and strong and gives "free use to the limbs without binding".[^1][^4] The No. 1P jersey is listed at USD 4.00; padded jerseys and a canvas football jacket at 50 cents also appear.[^1] The catalogue lists no cotton sweatshirt.[^1] Factory location and process are not in the vault.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. [unsourced] Wool jerseys felt with wear; no repair offer is documented.

## Today
The name continued after the Spalding purchase into the 1940s and no later activity is recorded.[^2] It is a historical reference and not a current maker.

## Design
Shaping the knit to the body, rather than cutting and sewing flat cloth, is the design decision the catalogue states, and the Patent post builds on it.[^3] Design is a taste score: construction logic for the body under load suits the owner's profile, while the goods are standard catalogue items.

## Depth
One primary catalogue, one university press chapter, a research note and a post cite it. The vault gives a good picture of one moment, 1901, and little else.
