## In One Line
Seattle maker of Mackinaw wool and Tin Cloth garments for Klondike-era work; the vault holds it mainly as a material reference.

## Lineage
The vault's Canvas research records Filson as founded in 1897 in Seattle, outfitting Klondike prospectors, with Tin Cloth as its oil-finished cotton duck and 2-ply yarns [^3]. Carryology names Filson as a collaborator of Nigel Cabourn [^2].

## Making and Material
Denimhunters describes the Mackinaw Jac-Shirt at 26oz, 100% virgin Mackinaw wool, lined in 8oz cotton flannel [^1]. The same review says a wearer needs "100+ hours" to break it in [^1]. No source in the vault names a factory or mill for the current line.

## Patina and Care
Break-in is the documented behaviour: the 26oz wool relaxes over about 100 hours of wear [^1]. The Canvas research says Tin Cloth is oil-finished duck [^3], but the vault holds no first-hand ageing report. Archival Clothing's vintage Filson breakdown sits in the 21 April 2026 weekly feed note, not as a Reference.

## Today
Filson remains a working outerwear and bag brand, collaborating with designers such as Cabourn [^2]. The vault has no current production or ownership source.

## Design
Boxy, functional cuts with heavy fabrics and cargo pockets [unsourced for details]. Taste score 4 matches the reference calibration for Filson as a maker with a clear, durable design logic.

## Depth
Two direct sources, a research-note footnote and a feed mention. The CPO shirt research uses the Jac-Shirt as a comparison point between a shirt and a jacket.
