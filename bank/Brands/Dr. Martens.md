## In One Line
A British boot, recorded in the vault only as part of the skinhead and punk uniform alongside the Harrington. No source describes how it is made.

## Lineage
The Harrington subculture source places Dr. Martens boots in the standard skinhead wardrobe of the mid-to-late 1960s, with Ben Sherman and Fred Perry [^1]. The brand's own founding, the Griggs company and the 1960 boot are general knowledge, not in the vault [unsourced].

## Making and Material
No source. The boot is Goodyear-welted with an air-cushioned sole [unsourced]; the vault holds no maker page, factory record or leather detail, and no source on where it is made today.

## Patina and Care
No source on how the leather ages or on resoling.

## Today
The vault records a circle of subcultural uses: skinheads before punk, then punk and ska pairings with distressed jeans [^1]. An academic paper, "Put on Your Boots and Harrington!", is cited in that source as a lead [^1]. No current range, owner or production claim is in the vault.

## Design
The boot is a shared uniform piece across movements; the Harrington source treats it as evidence of material continuity between skinheads and punk [^1].
