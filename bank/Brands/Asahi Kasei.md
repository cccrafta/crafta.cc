## In One Line
A Japanese chemicals and fibres group that makes ROICA stretch yarn. It matters to Crafta as the stretch component in recycled stretch denim and as a test case for mass-balance claims.

## Lineage
The vault records a Japanese chemicals and fibres group and maker of ROICA [^1]. No founding date or history is recorded.

## Making and Material
ROICA lists ISCC PLUS mass-balance certification alongside ISO 9001, 14001 and 45001 for its plants [^1]. ROICA EF carries Recycled Claim Standard certification, with certificate numbers shown for Japan, Thailand and Taiwan; the page notes certifications differ by plant and yarn [^1]. ROICA EF is made with pre-consumer recycled content and is said to be 30% lower in CO2 emissions than regular yarn, an Asahi Kasei claim [^2]. In the SAISEI collection it is woven by Kaihara Denim with Lenzing ECOVERO fibres in three fabrications: super stretch (Monster Stretch), comfort stretch and low-stretch selvedge [^2][^3].

## Patina and Care
No source. How stretch yarn ages inside denim is not covered; the Kaihara blend is described as having a subtle bulkiness and reduced drape [^2].

## Today
SAISEI debuted at Kingpins Amsterdam on 23-24 October 2024 [^2]. A biomass-balanced nylon 6.6 concept was shown with another maker; that maker is out of scope and is not developed here [^4].

## Design
A fibre, not a garment. The design question is what the yarn does to the cloth: stretch added to a selvedge line is the tension the research note flags between purist and blended denim.

## Depth
A mass-balance claim is a bookkeeping allocation under the scheme's draft guidance, so a buyer of a certified yarn may not hold the bio-based material physically [inference] [^1]. That makes the maker a good subject for a research note on certification.
