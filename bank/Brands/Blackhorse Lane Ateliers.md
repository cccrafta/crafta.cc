## In One Line
A Walthamstow jeans factory that cuts and sews its own denim in London and repairs other brands' jeans too, which makes it the vault's clearest example of denim made and mended in one building.

## Lineage
The label dates from 2016 and was founded by Bilgehan "Han" Ates, a tailor who had run London garment factories for about 30 years [^1]. The Our Story page gives the figure as 20+ years; the sources disagree and the note keeps both [^2]. The atelier sits in Blackhorse Lane, E17, in the same building as Han's earlier factory [^2]. Denimhunters records the brand's claim to be the first to make jeans in London in "50+ years", which is the maker's claim and not checked against another source [^3]. Han's earlier work was mostly tailored garments, not workwear [^4].

## Making and Material
Production is in-house. The Our Story page names roles for sampling, made-to-measure, and repair and alterations, and describes a wash R&D lab [^2]. Denim comes from Japanese selvedge (Kurabo is named on the brand's channels), Italian and Turkish mills [^1]. London has no spinning or weaving, so the cloth is imported [^1].

Construction is the part the trusted sources describe. Permanent Style reports a one-piece fly, taken from an 1896 jeans pattern Han found, where the Levi's 501 uses two stitched pieces [^4]. The seams are felled with no overlock, the waistband is chain-stitched all round, belt loops are tacked into seams and pocket bags are heavier (Permanent Style 2018, Denimhunters 2017) [^4][^5]. The pattern started when the team analysed Rudy Budhdeo of Son of a Stag's top 12 jeans and combined their best features [^4][^9]. Turkish denim keeps some lines cheaper: an 18oz NW1 at £290 and the E5 from £155 (dated prices) [^4].

## Patina and Care
The brand offers lifetime free repairs and a wash lab for finishing [^1]. Permanent Style lists it as a place that repairs other brands' jeans, at Coal Drops Yard [^6][^8]. Heavy selvedge with felled seams is built to be mended and not replaced.

## Today
Jeans, trousers and shirts are sold from the Walthamstow atelier and a Coal Drops Yard shop. Heddels lists the brand with Albam, Dawson and Hawksmill among English denim makers [^7]. Community indigo workshops run on an allotment about 15 minutes away, and 20% of floor space is pledged to a charity supporting local craftsmen [^2].

## Design
Plain five-pocket jeans whose interest is in the build: single-piece fly, chain-stitched waistband, felled seams. The pieces answer specific weak points of the 501 pattern, which is the kind of detail the owner takes apart. The silhouette is conventional; the proportions are not the point.

## Depth
The 1896 pattern, the top-12 analysis and the wash lab are all documented only through a handful of interviews. A research note on London denim production, or on the one-piece fly against the two-piece, would add to the vault. No Blackhorse Lane denim jungle jacket was found for the Barbour jacket research.
