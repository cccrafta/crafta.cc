## In One Line
A Japanese maker of very heavy denim, loopwheel knits and CPO shirt jackets that Heddels repeatedly places at the top end of quality.

## Lineage
No source in the vault gives the founding date or place; the vault's Posts note describes it as a Goshu-based brand [^6]. [unsourced] The brand is a 2000s Japanese repro-era label. Its lineage is therefore sourced only as a maker working in the Japanese heavyweight tradition.

## Making and Material
The Ultra-Heavy CPO is in two-tone twill with a darker weft, chainstitch runoff, felled seams, custom copper buttons and front hand pockets, at $400 [^1]. The vault's CPO research cites a 14.7oz selvedge denim version as a material departure from wool melton that keeps construction standards ("the weather function is gone but the build quality isn't") [^6]. The 14oz zip hoodie is loopwheeled at $310 [^2]. Heddels places the brand among end-tier sweatshirt makers, mostly Japanese [^3]. Heddels also notes that Japanese makers, Iron Heart among them, lead waffle henley quality [^4]. A 7.5oz corduroy western shirt shows the brand going lighter, against a decade in which "anything below 21 ounces of denim barely registered" [^6]. A 65/35 cotton-linen chambray at 7oz and a lightweight chambray made to fade appear in the weekly briefings [^7].

## Patina and Care
The brand builds for fade: heavy denim and a chambray "designed for fading" are noted in the briefings [^7]. No repair offer appears in the vault.

## Today
Denim, CPO jackets, hoodies, henleys and summer-weight shirts. Put This On's henley round-up puts it in the heritage tier, "best for pairing with leather jackets and field coats" [^5].

## Design
Heavy, plain, over-built pieces in dense cloth. The CPO shirt jacket keeps the military silhouette while changing the cloth. Details are specified, not decorative: chainstitch runoff, copper buttons, felled seams.

## Depth
A study of weight as a design variable: 14oz knit, 14.7oz denim CPO, 7oz chambray. The CPO comparison with Freenote and Cockpit USA is the most useful research thread.
