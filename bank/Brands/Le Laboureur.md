## In One Line
French workwear maker still producing moleskin garments in the bleu de travail tradition; in the vault it stands for the French counterpart to American workwear, with thin construction detail.

## Lineage
An idea note gives the founding as 1956, with the tradition reaching back to the late 19th century, and calls it "the most authentic" French workwear maker; the note does not source this, so treat it as unverified [unsourced].[^3] Heddels lists it among the brands behind the modern revival of vintage French workwear.[^1] Hast Paris, itself a maker, says Le Laboureur is still operating.[^2]

## Making and Material
The vault names the cloth, moleskin: a fustian with a satin weave, one side shorn and brushed ("peaching").[^1] No factory, mill or garment weight is recorded for Le Laboureur itself.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. [unsourced] Moleskin is a napped fustian cloth that softens with wear; no repair or care offer is documented.

## Today
Still operational per two sources.[^1][^2] A May 2026 weekly briefing flagged French workwear gaining independent editorial attention and named Le Laboureur and Adolphe Lafont as the T1 makers to cover.[^4] Where it is sold is not recorded.

## Design
No source describes the cut. [unsourced] The pieces are the plain French work forms (moleskin trousers, work jackets) rather than reinterpretations. Design is a taste score: good, restrained and in the owner's workwear interest, but nothing distinct is documented.

## Depth
The brand sits inside a larger story: the bleu de travail, Adolphe Lafont, Vetra and moleskin as "what cord was to England".[^1] An idea note already proposes a post on it.[^3] A research note on Le Laboureur alone would need primary material from the maker.
