## In One Line
A Paris house that turns existing garments, work shoes and army trainers into its own codes; it matters to Crafta for documented techniques and military and workwear borrowings, held against cachet pricing.

## Lineage
Founded in Paris in 1988 by Martin Margiela and Jenny Meirens, per the house [^1]. The Tabi was the first shoe, shown in the first collection. The house, the V&A and MoMA say 1988; Galliera dates its retrospective from spring-summer 1989 and Highsnobiety says "1989 debut" [^2] [^7] [^8] [^12] [^14]. The Artisanal line began in 2006 [^1]. The Replica trainer's start conflicts: SS1999 for refurbished originals with its own Replica in the early to mid 2000s (Heddels), early 2000s (Hypebeast) [^9] [^11]. The original trainer's maker is unresolved [^9].

## Making and Material
The house says the Tabi takes up to four hours of base preparation and 11 artisans, finished with a single white stitch, a maker claim [^2]. SSENSE says an Italian craftsman, Mr. Zagato, made the first boots [^10]. Bianchetto is white overpaint applied "in the same way as walls are painted"; paint chemistry and coats are not given [^4]. SS27 coated floral prints in transparent resin and unpleated them, so the surface fractured along the old folds [^16].

## Patina and Care
Cracking is intended: the matt white "will naturally crack and fade over time" and records use [^3]. Garments are washed inside out; shoes and bags with the paint are not washed [^4]. No repair service is documented.

## Today
The house runs MaisonMargiela/folders exhibitions in China [^5]. Margiela grew 8.4% in 2025 inside OTB, with Glenn Martens as creative director [^15]. A GAT costs about $750 [^13].

## Design
A split toe, a painted-white shoe, a trench coat rigid in front and supple behind [^16]. Taste anchor 5.

## Depth
House glossary pages, a Galliera retrospective, V&A and MoMA records and a Heddels GAT history [^7] [^8] [^9] [^10]. Chains are in [[Research/Why Margiela Tabi, Bianchetto and the GAT Are Everywhere]].
