## In One Line
A London studio that remakes surplus military cloth into new outerwear. The method is documented; where the garments are made is not, and sources point to different places.

## Lineage
Christopher Raeburn graduated from the RCA in 2006 and launched his studio in 2009, with a first collection of outerwear made from genuine military parachutes under a "Remade" ethos.[^2] The studio calls itself London-based since 2009, with a registered office at Soames Walk, SE10 (Greenwich).[^1] This is a designer's practice built on the military-to-civilian idea, not an inherited maker lineage.

## Making and Material
The Private White V.C. collaboration, a Reversible Field Jacket, uses Ventile on one side and military parachute canopy on the other, made at the Manchester factory from 23 surplus air force parachutes.[^3] The studio's own page mentions Nomex-based fireproof menswear.[^1] The V&A write-up does not say where the garments are made.[^2] A reported Hackney "Raeburn Lab" in a former Burberry factory (Euronews, 2019) may have moved.[^1] Production location is a live conflict between Greenwich, Hackney and Manchester, and is not resolved here.

## Today
Collaborations include Moncler, Barbour, Rapha, Fred Perry and Timberland; studio tours are offered.[^1]

## Design
Reworked surplus cloth: parachute canopies, Ventile, Nomex; the reversible field jacket is a documented example.[^3] Scored on taste: function-derived and material-led, though the design identity leans toward fashion collaboration.

## Depth
