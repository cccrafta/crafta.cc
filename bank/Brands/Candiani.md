## In One Line
Italian denim mill on the Ticino river; the vault's example of a mill selling cloth as a luxury material.

## Lineage
The Working Craft research records the mill as founded in 1938 between Milan and the Alps and run by the fourth generation of the family [^2]. The Sourcing Journal buyers' report quotes Candiani seeing a shift toward "the essence of jeans when they were truly meaningful" [^1].

## Making and Material
The Working Craft post places the mill at Robecchetto con Induno on the Ticino river and describes a 60% cashmere denim chore coat and post-consumer recycled denim with a green-cast indigo [^2]. No source in the vault gives loom type, weight ranges or yarn spinning details.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. Recycled and cashmere blends will fade differently from 100% cotton, but the vault does not document how.

## Today
For FW26-27 the mill speaks of practicality and a return to "meaningful" jeans [^1]. The luxury frame, with cashmere denim at coat prices, is the Working Craft post's reading of where denim has moved.

## Design
Candiani designs cloth, not garments. The cloth range spans stretch-free rigid denim to blended novelty cloth [unsourced]. The taste score reflects an honest middle position: interesting material work, little visible in the vault.

## Depth
Two documents only. A fuller note would need the mill's own account, a visit report, and independent tests of the recycled cloth.
