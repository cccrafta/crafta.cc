## In One Line
A family-owned London umbrella shop and basement workshop, described as the last in London to make umbrellas on site. It is an accessory maker, so it touches Crafta's repair thread only at the edge.

## Lineage
The firm began in 1830 and the New Oxford Street shop opened in 1857; the fifth generation of the Smith family still owns it.[^1] Lightweight steel frames arrived in 1851.[^1] The "last in London to make umbrellas on site" line is the article's description, not an audited fact.[^1]

## Making and Material
The workshop is in the basement, with 19th-century tools on wooden benches; a bell rings each time an umbrella is finished, about three a day.[^1] Most umbrellas now use nylon twill, with silk on request; parts come from specialist suppliers across Europe, many of whom have closed, so the shop keeps adapting.[^1] Accounts are still kept by hand in ring binders; the manager is named as Phil Naisbitt.[^1] Cloth weights, frame steel and handle woods are not stated.

## Patina and Care
The article does not describe a repair service.[^1] The London brands note says repair is only implied. No source on how the cloth or frames age.

Retail shop and workshop at one address, making on site at a small daily volume.[^1] [unsourced] The vault holds no information on sales channels or collaborations.
