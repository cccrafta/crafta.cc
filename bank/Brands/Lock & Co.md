## In One Line
St James's hatter dating to 1676 that finishes and repairs hats in its shop but buys the blocks from outside makers. Worth citing for the repair service and the bowler origin, with the making claims kept honest.

## Lineage
The shop dates to 1676 (Robert Davis) and has stood at 6 St James's Street since 1765; it calls itself the world's oldest hat shop and is still family-owned.[^1] In 1849 Edward Coke commissioned a hard hat for gamekeepers; Lock designed it and Thomas Bowler & Son produced it, hence "coke" or "bowler".[^1] Lock also claims to have "created the world's first bowler hat".[^2] The ledgers were rehomed at the London Metropolitan Archive in 2014 and can be viewed by the public.[^1]

## Making and Material
Hats are "hand-prepared in our London shop" by a Hatter, but Lock uses outside manufacturers in Spain, Belgium and the UK, and mills in the UK and Ireland for wool and cashmere; couture pieces are handcrafted in store.[^2] Permanent Style reports that Christys made hats for Lock & Co and Bates, neither of which makes its own.[^3] Escorial wool felt has a natural "memory" that returns it to a curled state after stretching.[^2]

## Patina and Care
Repair services: steam and brush, full re-block on a wooden block, brim re-block, sweatband and lining replacement.[^2] A conformateur dating from 1852 is still used to fit hard hats.[^1] How the felt ages is not described.

## Today
Still trading from St James's Street. Permanent Style's London guide calls it "the most famous hatter in the country".[^4]

## Design
Classic hat shapes; the bowler is the signature. No view from trusted sources on current design beyond the shapes themselves.

## Depth
Ledgers open to the public, a dated fitting tool, and a conflict worth a note: the maker claims it "created" the bowler while its own history says Thomas Bowler & Son produced it.[^1][^2]
