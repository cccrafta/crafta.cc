## In One Line
An east London maker of duffle and pea coats that says it cuts and sews everything in its own factory; the evidence is the maker's own page and nothing else.

## Lineage
Founded in 2001 in east London (96b Wallis Road) by Mamun Chowdhury and Rob Huson.[^1] The duffle itself has a documented military-to-civilian line: the Royal Navy coat that Gloverall civilianised in London in 1951, covered elsewhere in the vault. London Tradition's own company history is young and self-reported.[^1][^2]

## Making and Material
Maker's claim: every garment is cut, sewn and finished in its London factory, with genuine horn toggles and hand-stitched leather loops on the duffles.[^1] The publication note adds double-faced wool.[^2] No mills are named and no weights are given.[^1]

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. [unsourced] Wool duffles with horn toggles are repairable in principle; no repair offer is documented.

## Today
Queen's Award for Enterprise (International Trade) in 2014 and 2020, per the maker.[^1] The London Brands research files it under "worth checking" and flags the own-site-only evidence as single-source.[^3]

## Design
The duffle shape is a fixed template: hood, toggles, patch pockets. No source describes London Tradition's cut or proportions. Design is a taste score; the form suits the owner's military-to-civilian interest, but nothing sets it apart from other duffles on record.

## Depth
Little to study beyond the maker's page. The useful questions are who weaves the cloth and how it differs from Gloverall's duffle.
