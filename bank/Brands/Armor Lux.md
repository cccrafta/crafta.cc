## In One Line
A Quimper knitwear maker, operating since 1938, that is one of the three principal producers of the marinière. It stands for the Breton stripe still made by its original manufacturers.

## Lineage
Heddels dates the start of Armor Lux operations to 1938; Orcival followed in 1939 [^1]. The research note places it in Quimper [^1]. Orcival's own history names Armor Lux, Saint James and Orcival as the three principal makers [^2]; that page returned an error and its claims came from search results.

## Making and Material
The vault's Breton research describes faithful reproductions of the naval shirt, with dense tubular knit as the feature that separates a marinière from a striped shirt. The published Breton post states the producers still use heavy cotton, tubular construction and the original stripe proportions; that is the post's own summary rather than an Armor Lux source. No mills, weights or factory detail are recorded for Armor Lux itself.

## Patina and Care
No source.

## Today
Still produces marinières on the original principles, per the vault's Breton research. Wider range, collaborations and sales channels are not in the vault.

## Design
The garment is a fixed pattern: stripe proportion, boat neck and tubular body. The brand's interest is faithful reproduction, not a new eye [^1].

## Depth
A named maker in a well-documented garment story: the 1858 naval uniform, Chanel in 1913, Picasso, Gaultier [^1]. Armor Lux itself has no source note beyond its start date; a maker page would raise the score.
