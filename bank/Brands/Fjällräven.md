## In One Line
A Swedish outdoor maker whose Greenland Jacket answered the problem of heavy, slow-drying expedition clothing with a dense cloth and a wax finish. It anchors the vault's argument that sport clothing and workwear solve one problem.

## Lineage
The brand states roots to 1960 [^1]. Åke Nordin released the Greenland Jacket in 1968, at 32, in his new G-1000 fabric, waxed with a Greenland Wax he devised [^1][^2]. The maker ties it to a 1966 Greenland expedition of Scandinavian alpinists and researchers, to whom Nordin had supplied backpacks and tents but no clothing; the team wore boiled wool and leather that, in the maker's words, was heavy and slow to dry [^2]. The dates conflict: 1966 is the expedition, 1968 the jacket, and some sources use 1966 as the start.

## Making and Material
Nordin had tried the fabric in his tents and first ruled it out; it worked after tweaking and treatment [^2]. The G in G-1000 stands for Greenland [^2]. Retailer blogs give the cloth as 65% polyester and 35% cotton and say it began as tent fabric; the maker pages give no blend, and the 1968 composition is reported both as cotton canvas and as poly-cotton [^1][^2]. Greenland Trousers followed in 1970 per search summaries only [^2]. The wax came from Nordin's youth, when he waxed the backs of his trousers while ski jumping to keep out snow [^2]. The research table calls it a user-applied wax.

## Patina and Care
Evidence is testimony: one owner bought a jacket in 1976 and another in 2011 and has mended it several times; another owner bought two in 1980. There is no lab data [^1]. A mending or repair offer is not confirmed in the vault.

## Today
The vault records the Greenland Jacket as the model the maker still points to for how it develops jackets [^1]. Current ranges, factories and sizes are not covered.

## Design
Relaxed fit with practical pockets [^2]. A search summary says side pockets were left off because a harness blocks them and chest pockets added; this is single-source and unverified [^2].

## Depth
Two maker documents and a table row in the workwear and sportswear research. The claims are the maker's own, so the next step is outside testing or independent history.
