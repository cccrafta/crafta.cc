## In One Line
A London outerwear maker, founded 1951, that turned surplus Royal Navy duffle coats into a civilian coat. It is the vault's main case of a military garment redrawn for ordinary use.

## Lineage
Founded in London in 1951 by Harold and Freda Morris; the name contracts "gloves and overalls", their earlier wholesale trade [^1]. They first sold surplus Royal Navy duffle coats left from the post-war period, then had new coats made to the same pattern [^1]. Heddels says Gloverall introduced the first fully civilian duffle in 1953, as surplus ran low, with smaller pockets and hood and lighter cloth; the maker's own dates differ, so 1951 and 1953 both stand [^3]. The maker says Harold, son of a Savile Row master tailor, redesigned the coat by 1953: slimmer, leather loops instead of jute rope, horn toggles and a check Italian lining [^1].

## Making and Material
The toggle-and-loop closure was designed to be simple to operate with gloved hands in extreme cold; replacement toggles and anchor buttons are sold under Care & Repair [^1]. Gloverall had its own Northamptonshire factory by 1962; today the Heritage Collection is cut, stitched and finished in England at a factory outside London, with the town unnamed [^1][^2]. A blog places it in Wellingborough [single-source] [^4]. No mills are named; fabrics include Italian wool blends, Melton and Harris Tweed [^1][^3]. The Original Monty follows the 1951 pattern, a maker claim [^1]. The outdoor range and accessories are made elsewhere [^2].

## Patina and Care
A repair offer exists: replacement toggles and anchor buttons [^1]. How the wool and the jute-loop version wear is not in the vault.

## Today
The Original Monty is made in an Italian wool blend with a cotton herringbone webbing interior [^3]. Current ownership and design base were not found.

## Design
A civilian duffle from a naval pattern: roomy cut over a uniform, large toggles, hood sized for a cap [^3]. Gloverall's own line is that it "didn't reinvent anything" and made the original accessible [^1].

## Depth
Two maker pages, one specialist account and one blog. The duffle post in the vault builds on them; factory and ownership remain the gaps.
