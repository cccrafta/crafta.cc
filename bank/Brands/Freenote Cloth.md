## In One Line
A US maker whose Midway CPO in 10oz Japanese melton is a CPO-adjacent shirt, lighter than the military cloth it borrows from.

## Lineage
No source gives its founding. [unsourced] The Midway CPO is not a strict CPO: Heddels says it is inspired by the M42 HBT shirt [^1]. The vault's CPO research calls it "CPO-adjacent... silhouette but not the function". The Weekly feed for 2026-04-25 notes a Natural Indigo Modesto selvedge developed over a year.

## Making and Material
The Midway CPO uses a 10oz yarn-dyed Japanese melton of 70% wool, 20% polyester, 5% nylon and 5% acrylic [^1]. It has two expandable chest pockets, a French placket, single-needle stitching with double-needle at stress points, and Italian horn buttons [^1]. It is cut and sewn in the USA and costs $400 [^1]. Heddels notes the cloth is well below the 20-26oz military melton range [^1].

## Today
The Midway CPO is a current product, priced at $400 [^1]. The vault's Canvas research lists Freenote under waxed canvas jackets, a weak link that is not supported by a source note.

## Design
Heddels names the garment's source, the M42 HBT shirt, and stays open about the departure from military melton [^1]. The wool-blend cloth keeps the shirt's silhouette and drops the compressed weight that makes a CPO a CPO.

## Depth
Heddels gives a full spec: composition, weight, pockets, placket, stitching, buttons, price [^1]. The set is a good comparison point against Cockpit USA and Faherty in the CPO research.
