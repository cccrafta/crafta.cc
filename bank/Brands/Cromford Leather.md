## In One Line
London leather-clothing maker with an atelier on Chiltern Street and a repair service; a rare British leather-garment workshop with a documented history.

## Lineage
The General Leather Company was founded in 1971 in London by Alan Sprooles, a Savile Row graduate, and Peter Goodall [^1]. Pauline Harris became director in 2015 and renamed it for Cromford, Derbyshire [^1]. All of this is the maker's own account.

## Making and Material
Garments are made at the company's atelier at 56 Chiltern Street, in ready-to-wear sizes S to 2XL, made-to-measure and bespoke [^1]. Permanent Style says coats are still made downstairs at the shop [^2]. The maker names no tannery, so the leather source is unknown.

## Patina and Care
Permanent Style lists Cromford among places that repair and alter leather clothing [^3], which makes repair part of the offer, not an afterthought. No source describes how its leathers age.

## Today
The company says it has produced for Margaret Howell and Mr Porter, for film and television, and coats for the Queen [^1]. The claims are the maker's and are not independently confirmed in the vault.

## Design
Classic leather coat and jacket patterns with made-to-measure fitting [unsourced for specific shapes]. The score reflects a competent traditional house rather than a distinct design voice.

## Depth
Three sources, two from Permanent Style, give history, location, and a repair role. A fuller note would need a workshop visit, the leathers used, and a comparison of its pattern work with other London leather makers.
