## In One Line
American canvas shoe maker in the vault for two facts: canvas deck shoes from 1966 that skateboarders adopted for the vulcanised sole, and a 2026 FDMTL split-toe collaboration; no source describes how a Vans shoe is made.

## Lineage
The Canvas research records that Vans begins in 1966 with canvas deck shoes, adopted by skateboarders because the vulcanised rubber sole gripped wood.[^2] The Crafta Canvas post says the vulcanisation step that bonds rubber to the upper needs about 170 degrees C, so nylon and polyester melt and cotton canvas holds.[^3] No founder or factory is named. [unsourced] The company started in California.

## Making and Material
Canvas upper with a vulcanised rubber sole is the construction named.[^2][^3] The FDMTL Authentic Tabi is a split-toe version in indigo denim with boro-style patchwork and sashiko-style stitching on a vulcanised sole.[^1] Where the collaboration is sewn is not stated.

## Patina and Care
No source in the vault. [unsourced] Canvas uppers fade and wear at the toe and flex points.

## Today
Hypebeast reports the FDMTL x Vans Authentic Tabi from FDMTL's FW26 runway preview, released "2026" with no price given.[^1] The Margiela tabi research lists it among sportswear and mass-market tabi spread in 2026.[^4]

## Design
The vulcanised canvas shoe is a mass-produced working object. The FDMTL version applies a Japanese repair vocabulary to it, which is the collaborator's contribution and not Vans' own design thinking. Design is a taste score: the base shoe is plain and functional; the notable work comes from a partner.
