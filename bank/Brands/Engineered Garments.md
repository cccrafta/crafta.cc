## In One Line
New York label designed by Daiki Suzuki and sold through Nepenthes. It reworks military and workwear patterns (the CPO shirt, the jungle jacket) with changed fabrics and details, and has done so for nearly two decades.

## Lineage
Daiki Suzuki designs the label; he joined Nepenthes, conceived in Tokyo in 1988 by Keizo Shimizu, around the first shop [^8]. The Engineered Garments founding year is `[unsourced]`. Research/CPO Shirt places it in New York and USA made.

## Making and Material
The CPO shirt in Research/CPO Shirt uses a 20oz melton wool shell with a cotton facing and corduroy collar. The jungle jacket exists in ripstop and sheeting, USA made, with a hidden button closure per stockist listings [^5]. The Fatigue Pant is in 12oz indigo broken-twill denim [^1]. The CPO shirt jacket uses two grey herringbone fabrics, three differently sized chest pockets, snap closure, hidden pockets and a two-snap throat strap, $247 [^4]. Suzuki removed the epaulettes from the jungle jacket but kept the gas flap, side tabs and concealed buttons (maker's account) [^1]; the SS22 version is in high-count twill and ripstop per Nepenthes [^3].

## Patina and Care
No care or repair offer in the vault. Wool melton, corduroy and cotton sheeting age by use `[unsourced]`.

## Today
Nepenthes London sells mostly its own labels including Engineered Garments and Needles [^6][^8]. Barbour x Engineered Garments, AW 2018 [^7]. FW25 CPO fabrics include Dark Navy Heavy Wool Elastique, Wool Striped HB and corduroy [^2].

## Design
The label changes the details that matter: snap placket in place of buttons, asymmetrical chest pockets, sleeve zip pockets and a curved hem on the CPO [^2]. A voile western shirt shows the habit of "wrong context, right result" (Research/Cotton Voile). This is the owner's reference for design.

## Depth
Nearly two decades of CPO revisits [^2], three jungle jacket cloths, and cross-links with Needles and South2 West8 make a deep well for a research note.
