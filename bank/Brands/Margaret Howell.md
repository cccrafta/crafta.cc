## In One Line
London label (1970) of plain, lived-in British clothing that names its cloth traditions and runs its own shirt workroom. The clearest "named mills, own workroom, archive" case in the London research.

## Lineage
Howell dates her start to finding an old pinstripe shirt at a jumble sale in 1970, which is a story date, not a company registration date.[^1] Her first workroom was set up in 1973 in Blackheath, South London, after years of making shirts in her bedroom.[^2] The first London store opened in 1977, with Maxfield (Los Angeles) and Joseph (New York) as early buyers.[^4] She licensed the brand to a Japanese businessman who built it to 80+ stores in Japan while she kept creative control.[^4] A Herald Business interview adds that Japan's TSI Holdings took a stake in 1990 [single-source].[^5]

## Making and Material
Shirts are made in a Harringay workroom opened in summer 2026: 17 people, patterns made in house and pieces still cut by hand, every stage on site.[^3] A single shirt carries about 12,000 stitches (six per cm) and takes 3-4 hours; buttons are horn or mother-of-pearl; shirts are hand-pressed.[^2] Other shirts are made in and around Florence and in other British factories, unnamed.[^3] Howell cites Harris Tweed, Irish linen with an irregular slub, and Scottish knitting factories.[^1] Early cotton came from David & John Anderson.[^3] Cromford Leather has made garments for Margaret Howell.[^7] The 40-year Fox Brothers link and a figure of about 4,000 shirts a year rest on one low-reliability interview [single-source].[^5]

## Patina and Care
The Simple Mac is water-resistant waxed cotton with raglan sleeves, internal storm placket, press studs and metal eyelet vents.[^6] No rewaxing or repair offer is in the vault.

## Today
Archive of about 3,000 pieces in the basement of the Marylebone shop, kept by a full-time archivist; 7 UK stores per Herald Business [single-source].[^4][^5]

## Design
Howell says "They're real clothes... They're not fantasy" and found French shirt patterns fitted better than English ones.[^4] She admires mid-century British design (Anglepoise, Ercol, Robert Welch).[^1] The eye is quiet and construction-led, close to the owner's taste.

## Depth
A dated workroom history (1973 Blackheath, 2000 Edmonton "Unit 7" with one machinist per shirt, 2026 Harringay), a stitch-count account of a shirt, and an archive.[^2][^3]
