## Summary
Grailed Dry Clean Only (Marc Richardson, 23 Oct 2018). Retells Leitch and adds a Margiela timeline from the shoe's label story. Read via WebFetch (a model summary of the page), so second-hand.

## Key Claims
- "In the late 1970s" the West German Army sought new training shoes; adidas and Puma both bid; about 500,000 soldiers had been issued Bundeswehr Sportschuhe by 1989.
- Per Leitch's reading of Bundeswehr History Museum records, "the initial prototypes" were proposed by Puma; Puma says it was never involved; adidas acknowledges producing the shoe in the 1980s and 90s but the Dresden museum "has no records of the Three Stripes".
- Richardson's own conjecture: adidas made the trainers to order, unbranded, which could explain the missing records; he concludes "We may never know if Puma or adidas gave us the first German Army Trainer".
- Margiela "purchased dozens of pairs of BW Sport" that appeared at the "Spring/Summer 1999 Artisanal presentation"; first versions were vintage pairs cleaned, re-laced, stamped with the number logo and sometimes repainted; the 2001-2002 pairs with painted soles and handwritten notes are probably the most famous.
- The house story printed on the shoe label says Margiela found the silhouette in Austria, not Germany; the house began making its own Replica in the early to mid 2000s.
