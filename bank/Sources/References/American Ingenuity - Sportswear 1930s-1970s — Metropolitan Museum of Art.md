## Summary
The Met Costume Institute's exhibition (2 April to 16 August 1998) and catalogue by curator Richard Harrison Martin presenting sportswear as the distinctive American contribution to fashion. ## Key Claims
- Exhibition at the Costume Institute, Metropolitan Museum of Art, 2 April to 16 August 1998; catalogue by Richard Martin, photographs by Karin L. Willis, 96 pages, reissued by Yale University Press in 2013.
- Catalogue organised around five features (wrapping and tying, easy fastening, big pockets, mix-and-match separates, menswear-derived and carefree activewear elements) and profiles 23 designers including Claire McCardell, Bonnie Cashin, Tina Leser, Vera Maxwell, Clare Potter and Emily Wilkens.
- Per Wikipedia's citation of Martin ("All American: A Sportswear Tradition", 1985) and the Met director's 1998 foreword: sportswear is "an American invention, an American industry, and an American expression of style". Quotation not checked against the primary text.
- In 1932 Lord & Taylor's Dorothy Shaver showed new American sportswear designers together under an "American Look" banner (search summary and Wikipedia; Robinson 2003 cited there).
- Martin's 1985 FIT show and 1998 Met show frame sportswear as a design category (functional, mass-produced, separates) rather than as sports clothes.
