## Summary
Mike Knispel's profile of designer Nigel Cabourn, whose archive treats military, expedition, workwear and vintage sportswear as one body of primary sources. The article is dated 19 June 2026 and reports his death; that date came from the fetched page and should be confirmed against a second source before use.

## Key Claims
- Cabourn's Jesmond (Newcastle) studio archive holds more than 4,000 garments and about 3,000 books: military uniforms, expedition outerwear, workwear and vintage sportswear, each treated as a primary source and translated into new designs (e.g. a 1943 flight suit rebuilt in Harris Tweed).
- Born 1949 in Peterlee, County Durham; studied art and industrial design in Newcastle from 1967; started menswear label Cricket in 1969; Cricket liquidated 1984, relaunched under his own name.
- 1979 Paris trade fair produced a GBP 250,000 order from Japanese wholesaler Sam Sugure, starting a long Japan relationship; Army Gym flagship opened in Tokyo in 2009; at peak 26 stores, 16 in Japan.
- Lybro is his workwear line (British industrial clothing); Army Gym draws on vintage sportswear and military physical-training gear. This is a direct maker-level pairing of workwear and sportswear lines under one designer.
- Collaborators included Alpha Industries, Converse, Filson, Fred Perry, Karrimor, Red Wing.
- Signature Everest Parka (GBP 3,100); frequent use of Ventile and Harris Tweed; expedition inspirations Mallory, Hillary, Scott, Shackleton.
- Marubeni has held the Japanese licence since January 2025 (per article).
