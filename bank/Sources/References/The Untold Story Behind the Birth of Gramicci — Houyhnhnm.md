## Summary
Houyhnhnm feature on Gramicci's founding and its 1990s Japanese adoption as "new sports fashion". The English page is machine-translated (brand name spelled several ways) and WebFetch gave a summary, so treat wording as paraphrase; the Chouinard passage is garbled.

## Key Claims
- Founded 1982; founder Mike Graham was a Yosemite rock climber who made his own pants in a small California garage after tiring of what he wore.
- Reported link to Yvon Chouinard of Patagonia, who reportedly asked Graham to make products; the garage then grew into a workshop (garbled passage, single-source).
- Construction: a rhombus-shaped gusset in the inseam gives a 180 degree leg opening; webbing belt inspired by a backpack harness buckle, adjustable one-handed. Both are described as constant across the line.
- Fit logic: a snug, tapered silhouette that does not flap, because climbers need to see their footing; designers iterated patterns toward a "golden ratio" silhouette that still moves.
- Early 1990s: Japanese select shops Beams and Oshmans introduced Gramicci "as the new sports fashion".
- Brand logic as the article tells it: keep the gusset and belt fixed, slim silhouettes gradually; "functional beauty should be timeless".
- Modern Japanese lines named: NN-Shorts (2014) and NN-Pants (2015).
