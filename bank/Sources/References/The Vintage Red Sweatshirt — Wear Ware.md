## Summary
Short online-magazine piece on faded vintage red sweatshirts, built from quotes by dealers and collectors. No byline or publication date appears, and it is not a textile study. Useful only as attributed dealer observation on how dyed cotton sweatshirts age. Read through a fetch summary, so exact wording is unverified.

## Key Claims
- Samual Ward (Vertical Archives) says gray and white sweatshirts "tend to hold their same color over time", while red and blue dyes "fade and change color through wash cycles and sun exposure".
- Ward says red "eventually will transition to a salmon/pink color due to the all cotton fabric".
- Collector Chris Walker lists the fades he likes as "drastic fades, subtle ones, uniform, and ghost fades", giving "peaches, pinks, and dusty rose".
- Ward says this aging can only come from use over time, which is why dealers and collectors rate faded pieces higher, and compares it to the "whiskers" on old Levi's and the "tattering on old Carhartt".
- Ward says few dye colours were used on sweatshirts "from the 1960s and earlier".
- Emanual Edwards (The Goody Vault, Chicago) is unimpressed by the trend and says many garments have "the same allure once they're faded and aged".
- The page says nothing on texture, shrinkage, cuffs or measurements.
