## Summary
Profile and interview with Mike Graham, the Yosemite Stonemaster who founded Gramicci. Independent corroboration of the 1980-82 timeline and his Chouinard Equipment contracting; says nothing about fabric weights or what climbers wore earlier. WebFetch summary with some garbled passages.
## Key Claims
- Graham says he was experimenting with clothing ideas by 1982; his first own designs date to 1980; he made custom gear such as portaledges and packs from 1977.
- Graham kept meeting Yvon Chouinard in the late 1970s; Chouinard, having heard of his portaledges, offered sewing work ('I'll throw you all the business you want'). Graham arrived in Ventura in 1978 and helped make Chouinard's first climbing harnesses, then packs, then polypropylene underwear.
- He was a Chouinard Equipment contractor for many years: a direct line from Patagonia's gear shop to Gramicci.
- Name: in 1974 European climbers were claiming Yosemite ascents; Graham and partners staged a 'first clean Italian ascent of Half Dome' under the invented name Michelangelo Gramicci; the nickname stuck in Camp 4. He considered 'Stone Master' as the company name but chose Gramicci.
- The writer (not Graham) says he borrowed the gusseted crotch from martial arts pants; Graham says 'that's how Gramicci's gusseted climbing pants came about'.
- REI told Graham in 1998 that Gramicci was its top non-house brand; sales were $27 million in 1999, when Graham left.
- The article does not mention the belt, fabric or weight.
