## Summary
Maker's own account of Ventile, the dense long-staple cotton weather fabric developed at the Shirley Institute in the UK. Useful for the maker's stated mechanism and date; independent corroboration is thin. WebFetch returned a garbled summary.
## Key Claims
- The page says Ventile Classic stays true to the 'original concept developed at Shirley Institute in 1943'.
- It says the fabric was developed for the British Royal Air Force (wording partly garbled in the extraction).
- Ventile Classic is 100% cotton from 'extra-long staple cotton fibres' woven at very high densities.
- Mechanism: when wet the fibres swell and close the gaps in the weave, forming a weather barrier without a membrane or coating.
- The maker says the principle is unchanged after 'more than eighty years' and the range now includes recycled-cotton, hemp and wool blends.
- Not on the page: the 1930s origin, fire hoses, 1953 Everest, Swiss weaving, fabric weight. Retailer sources (e.g. Those That Know) place the 1953 Hunt expedition in Ventile but without evidence; treat as [single-source] marketing.
