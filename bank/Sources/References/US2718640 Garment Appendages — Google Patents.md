## Summary
Patent for fixing pockets, belt loops and hammer loops to overalls and other heavy-duty garments with parallel lines of stitching, so the appendage does not part from the garment at the seam. It records triple-needle stitching as an existing but idle machine capability in 1952. Full text was readable but OCR-garbled.
## Key Claims
- Inventor Jerome L. Suckle of Wyncote, Pa.; no company assignee; filed 30 December 1952, granted 27 September 1955; expired.
- Names hammer loops among appendages that wear heavily: "garment appendages, pockets, belt loops, hammer loops, etc., subject relatively great amount wear".
- Failure point is the join: "occur at seam or other means uniting appendages garment"; the text does not say specifically that hammer loops tear out.
- Fix is several parallel stitch lines through appendage and garment: "Triple lines stitching 64, 65, 66, 67, and 68" for a pocket, spaced "approximately one eighth inch therebetween"; a four-line variant is allowed.
- Hammer loop 45 is secured to the garment by triple stitching 86, 87.
- Says triple-needle machines are usually idle, so the method needs no new equipment.
- Target garments: "overalls other heavy duty garments" with bib, front pants, rear patch pockets, belt loops and hammer loops. The visible text does not mention a bar tack.
