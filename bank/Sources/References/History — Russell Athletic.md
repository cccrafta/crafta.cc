## Summary
Russell's own corporate history of its founding in Alexander City, Alabama and its claim to have invented the sweatshirt. The story is a maker's origin claim; Wikipedia gives 1920 for the same events, and no archival document was found to settle it.

## Key Claims
- "On March 3, 1902, Russell Athletic was founded by 26-year-old Benjamin Russell" as Russell Manufacturing Company, Alexander City, AL. "The tiny factory had 10 sewing machines and 8 knitting machines." First product: a knit shirt for women and children (a separate Russell page says undergarments).
- 1926: Benny Russell, the founder's son, pitched "a new idea for an all-cotton practice football jersey replacing the itchy, chafing wool uniforms worn at that time". The younger Russell was a player at the University of Alabama.
- "The iconic crew neck sweatshirt began production in the Russell Athletic mills in 1930", under the heading "The pioneer, inventor, and creator of the sweatshirt".
- By the 1960s the athletic division was the largest US maker of sports apparel and uniforms.
- Company renamed Russell Mills, Inc. in 1962 (American Eagle brand image), Russell Corporation in 1973. Acquired by Fruit of the Loom in 2006.
- Conflict: Wikipedia places the cotton jersey in 1920; Russell's other page (russellathletic.com historyofthelogo) says 1926. Russell's pages do not mention a ladies' union suit.
