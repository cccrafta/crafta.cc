## Summary
The maker's own account of why it exists: to keep Wakayama's tsuriami (hanging) loopwheel machines running. T1 for machine speeds, counts and production sites; commercial claims about sinker fabric are the maker's own and should be attributed.

## Key Claims
- Loopwheeler was founded in 1999 by Satoshi Suzuki to make "the most authentic sweatshirts" and to stop TSURIAMIKI machines and TSURIAMI fabric disappearing.
- Loop Wheel Machines were imported from Europe in the early twentieth century; from the 1960s faster, later computerised machines replaced them. In Wakayama a few kept running and evolved into the Japanese tsuriamiki ("suspended knitting machine", hung from the ceiling so yarn drops by its own weight).
- About 200 tsuriamiki still run, all in Wakayama, which the page calls the only place in the world they run. (A separate interview says Wakayama had about ten loopwheel factories in the 1980s and two by 1997; not on this page.)
- Speed: about 1 m of sweatshirt fabric per hour (pique about 0.3 m) versus over 10 m per hour on a sinker machine; 24 vs 240 rotations per minute.
- Maker's claim: sinker-knit fabric stiffens with washing and loses texture after around three years; vintage sweatshirts of the late 1940s to mid-1960s remain prized.
- Each size is knit on a machine of matching diameter, so garments have no side seams.
- Two Wakayama factories use the machines: Kanekichi Industry (established 1920) and Wada Meriyasu. Sewing is at Maruwa Textile's Aomori factory.
- Flat-seam stitching on a four-needle machine; all production is in Japan from yarn to finished garment.
