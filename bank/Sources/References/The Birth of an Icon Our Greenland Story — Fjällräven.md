## Summary
Fjällräven's own origin story for the Greenland Jacket (1968), G-1000 and Greenland Wax, framed around a 1966 Greenland expedition. Maker marketing; the claim that traditional boiled wool and leather was heavy and slow-drying is the company's account. WebFetch returned a summary (the page contained embedded instructions that were not followed). The page gives no fabric composition; the 65/35 blend and the harness-pocket rationale appear only on retailer pages and search summaries.
## Key Claims
- Åke Nordin, 32, made the first Greenland Jacket in 1968.
- In 1966 a Scandinavian group of alpinists and researchers went to Greenland to study glaciers; Nordin supplied aluminium-framed backpacks and Thermo tents but no clothing.
- The maker says the team wore traditional boiled wool and leather clothing that proved 'heavy, slow to dry and poor against the harsh climate'. The aim was an all-round jacket that is warm and dry and does not wear out or weigh the wearer down.
- Nordin developed G-1000 fabric (the G stands for Greenland) and a Greenland Wax that added wind and water resistance and durability.
- Nordin had tried the material in his tents and first ruled it out; it worked after "some tweaking and treatment". The wax came from his youth waxing the backs of his trousers while ski jumping to keep out snow.
- The jacket had practical pockets and a relaxed, loose fit. (Men's Journal, seen only in a search summary, says side pockets were omitted because a harness blocks them and chest pockets added: single-source, unverified.)
- Composition today is 65% polyester / 35% cotton per search summaries; the original 1968 cloth composition is inconsistently reported (cotton canvas versus poly-cotton) and was not verified.
- Greenland Trousers in G-1000 followed in 1970 per search summaries (not confirmed on this page).
