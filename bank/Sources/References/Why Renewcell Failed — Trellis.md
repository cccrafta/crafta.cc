## Summary
Elsa Wenzel, 20 March 2024 (updated 16 February 2026). Read as page text via curl (not a summary). Named analysts and company executives. Replaces the paywalled C&EN piece (cen.acs.org, HTTP 200 via curl but body not delivered).

## Key Claims
- Three main reasons given by sources: basic economics (Circulose cost about twice as much as alternative materials; nearly twice competing wood pulp, with fibre makers' viscose from it priced 50% above regular viscose), the industry's resistance to change, and brands that signed up for experimental capsule collections but rarely converted to store-line orders.
- Renewcell had 116 companies in its supplier network by October 2023, yet pulp deliveries fell to zero in November 2023 after 2,800 metric tons shipped in July; the Sundsvall plant (a retrofitted paper mill, claimed 120,000 metric tons/year capacity) produced a growing share of lesser-quality pulp.
- H&M Group had committed $29 million over seven years; it offered a $4.4 million short-term loan in December 2023 but ended support in early 2024; Renewcell filed for bankruptcy in February 2024 and the CEO had been dismissed in October 2023.
- Fibre is 3-5% of a garment's retail price according to Renewcell's chief commercial officer, who argued the premium was "pennies" per garment; Planet Tracker's analyst questioned whether brands ever demanded a higher-price alternative.
- Renewcell's pulp was sold to fibre makers, not made into cloth by Renewcell; the supply chain (collectors, sorters, pre-processors, fibre makers, mills) had to be built from scratch.
