## Summary
Encyclopedia entry on the Cordura brand; useful for the brand lineage and for pointing to primary citations (a 2004 Invista press release and a 1948 DuPont advertisement). It is T4 and gives no abrasion test data. Page text was read via WebFetch.
## Key Claims
- Developed and trademarked by DuPont in 1929, first offered as a rayon (regenerated cellulose); sourced to a 2004 Invista press release and a DuPont heritage page.
- A 1948 DuPont ad (Notre Dame Scholastic, 9 April 1948) says that in 1928 a rubber company wanted a rayon yarn stronger than cotton for tire cords, the result being "as strong as mild steel yet able to stand up under repeated flexing".
- World War II development was aimed at tires for military vehicles (uncited).
- By 1966 the brand name moved to nylon products (uncited); Eastpak was the first brand to use Cordura in luggage and by 1979 soft-sided Cordura luggage held about 40 percent of the market (partly uncited).
- Invista (Koch Industries) owns the brand; it also makes a 420D nylon 6,6 fibre used in blends with cotton (Cordura Baselayer, Denim, Duck), per Textile World, 22 March 2010.
- Only abrasion claim is that textile motorcycle clothing uses it "for abrasion resistance"; marketing multiples of 2x to 4x nylon seen elsewhere were not verified against test data.
- Banner on the page says weave and weight information is missing.
