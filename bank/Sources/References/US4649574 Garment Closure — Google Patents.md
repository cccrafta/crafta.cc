## Summary
Patent for Gi (martial arts) pants with an alternative closure; one embodiment adds a diamond-shaped crotch gusset for reinforcement and freedom of movement. It documents the martial arts diamond gusset that Gramicci later cites as its source, though the patent is dated 1987, five years after Gramicci's 1982 founding. Full description and claims were readable.
## Key Claims
- Inventor Frank J. Michels; filed and priority 17 April 1986, granted 17 March 1987.
- "In order to obtain improved reinforcement and flexibility a somewhat diamond-shaped piece or gusset 51 (FIG. 7)" is added; the summary says "in order to attain greater freedom of movement and to provide improved reinforcement".
- States the problem as stress: "Gi uniforms are utilized in marital art competition and as such may be subjected to extraordinary stress" (spelling as in patent), "particularly in and about the crotch area of the pants or trousers".
- The gusset is cut from the garment's own fabric and attached with a double seam around all edges, between the upper inner edges of the leg segments and the lower inner edges of the front and rear segments.
- Claim 9 ties the gusset to Gi pants (claim 8): "a somewhat diamond-shaped piece composed of the garment fabric".
- Date caveat: this patent post-dates Gramicci (1982) and describes an existing pants feature as an optional add-on, so it shows the feature in martial arts pants but is not evidence of who invented the diamond gusset.
