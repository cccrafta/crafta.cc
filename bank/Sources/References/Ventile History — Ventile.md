## Summary
Brand page for Ventile: a densely woven extra-long-staple cotton that gets its water resistance from fibres swelling when wet. The page gives 1943 and the Royal Air Force but no weights, Everest or patent details. Page text was garbled, so only short fragments were quoted.
## Key Claims
- Page gives 1943 as the start and links the fabric to the British Royal Air Force and the Shirley Institute; the text is garbled.
- Ventile Classic is 100% cotton, "extra-long staple cotton fibres", woven at exceptionally high density, made in Europe.
- Mechanism per the page: "fibres naturally expand, reducing spaces within weave" when wet, closing gaps; dry, the cloth breathes; no membrane or coating.
- Wikipedia's Ventile article (not the brand) adds: first developed by Shirley Institute scientists in Manchester; weights from 145 to 300 g/m2; since 2017 the trademark is owned by Stotz & Co AG, Switzerland, which sells the specification as etaProof; the 800 mm hydrostatic-head figure for MoD waterproof comes from a commercial site and is not an MoD document.
- Cited 1943 is a brand claim: the Shirley Institute patent was not found on Google Patents, and retail claims of a tenfold survival gain or 80 percent pilot survival are unverified marketing.
- US 4,286,012 independently describes Ventile as long-staple, doubled-yarn cotton in Oxford weave from a 1970s trade sheet.
