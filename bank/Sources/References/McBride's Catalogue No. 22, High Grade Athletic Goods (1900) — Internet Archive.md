## Summary
A Toronto sporting-goods retailer's 1900 catalogue (OCR text, University of Toronto Fisher Library scan; IA identifier mcbridescatalogu00perc). It shows what team and gymnasium clothing was made of and sold for at the turn of the century: premium wool, cheaper "sanitary" cotton, club-styled football jerseys, and duck for hockey knickers. Primary evidence of sport clothing as a mass-market commodity. Prices are Canadian dollars. Date taken from IA metadata (1900).

## Key Claims
- Wool at the premium: "All Wool Sweaters" in solid worsted 3-ply roll collar (hockey) $6.00, 5-ply $9.00; "All Wool Jerseys" $4.00 and $5.50; heavy all-wool "Jumbo" 6-ply sweater coat $10.00, shaker-knit sweater coat $12.00; all in "any colour or combination of colours".
- Gymnasium and basketball jerseys: all-wool $2.50 to $3.50 against "best sanitary cotton" $1.00 and lightweight cotton $0.75; gym knickers in white cotton $1.00, sateen $1.25; "Y.M.C.A. regulation trousers" $5.00. Cotton is the cheap tier, sold with a hygiene claim ("sanitary").
- Football jerseys: "Heavy sanitary cotton, vertical stripes, laced neck, any colors" $3.00; medium weight, "Manchester United and Aston Villa styles" $2.50. Football knickers in English swansdown $1.50 or white cotton $1.00.
- Hockey knickers in "white duck" (a heavy plain-weave cotton canvas also used in work clothing) at $2.00, with hip padding.
- The same catalogue sells "College, Club and Fraternity Pennants" and notes goods can be made "with Seal or Monogram for any College or Club": club identity was already a sales feature in 1900.
- Interpretation caution: the catalogue does not mention workwear; the fabric overlap (cotton duck, jersey knit) is the reader's inference.
