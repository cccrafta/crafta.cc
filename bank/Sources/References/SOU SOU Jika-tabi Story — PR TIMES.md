## Summary
SOU・SOU company story on PR TIMES (30 Sep 2024), a company-authored press story (T1 for the maker's account; promotional). Japanese; translated here. Read via WebFetch (a model summary of the page), so second-hand.

## Key Claims
- Founder-representative Wakabayashi (若林) found a black jika-tabi sample in the warehouse when new kimono-style clothes were not selling; his reasoning was that outside Japan jika-tabi already read as "cool" while in Japan they read as work shoes, so colourful textiles could make them fashionable.
- He insisted on Japanese production because "it is a traditional Japanese footwear, so it should be made in Japan"; about 98% of jika-tabi sold today are made abroad after big makers moved production overseas in the Showa 40s (about 1965-74).
- "Only 3 companies now produce in Japan" (日本国内で生産している会社は現在たったの3社).
- The first Japanese factory approached, Takasago Sangyo in Hyogo (running-shoe maker), refused at first; nearly the whole staff opposed because printed patterns meant extra work; the then chairman Kako Akiharu backed it. Takasago Sangyo remains the main factory.
- A New York show lost 1.5 million yen but the first buyer was reportedly a Ralph Lauren director; the Aoyama shop followed; SOU・SOU now sells about 20,000 pairs a year in Japan and abroad.
