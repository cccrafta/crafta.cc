## Summary
Chapter II of the 1892 Badminton Library volume 'Mountaineering', written by C. T. Dent, sets out what Alpine climbers were advised to wear at the end of the Victorian era. It is a first-hand period source for wool and tweed, Norfolk jackets, knickerbockers, pocket design and the practical case against heavy cloth. Read from the Internet Archive OCR text (mountaineering00dentiala_djvu.txt), chapter heading at line 2507.
## Key Claims
- Dent advises 'Tweeds answer excellently well, or any woollen fabric in which the fibres are cross-woven'; serge and flannel are 'too apt to tear' (OCR text, p. 40).
- He reports the costume 'generally agreed upon' among British climbers in the Alps as 'a Norfolk jacket, waistcoat and knickerbockers' (p. 40).
- The Norfolk jacket is recommended as loose in the armholes, buttoning close at the neck, with button-down flapped pockets; one pocket lined with mackintosh to keep matches or a knife dry; a game pocket large enough for maps (pp. 40-41).
- A strong collar loop is specified so the coat can hang over the shoulder 'threading the axe through the loop' (p. 41): early evidence of gear-carrying detail built into a garment.
- On weight: 'The mistake is commonly made of having the clothes too heavy'; 'Very thick clothes, too, take much longer to dry, and are intolerably heavy when soaked with rain' (p. 40). Better to carry a spare flannel shirt than wear 'a suit fitted for an Arctic voyage'.
- Knickerbockers 'allow free movement of the knee, and do not get so draggled and dirty as trousers'; the cost is long stockings taking room in the knapsack (p. 41).
- Double-seated knickerbockers are mentioned only as something 'some advise'; Dent says the need depends on how the climber descends rocks and 'ought to be unnecessary' (pp. 41-42). Other sources describe doubled seats as common, so this is a minor disagreement.
- Pattern advice reflects repairability: avoid pronounced patterns because 'the village tailor' may not match a repair, and carry a one-foot-square piece of the suit cloth 'to patch with' (p. 40).
- Woollens can be made to 'resist wet much more effectually' by dipping in a solution of alum (p. 42).
