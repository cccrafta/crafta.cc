## Summary
General-press account of the 1917 Converse All Star and Chuck Taylor's role. Good for dates and Converse marketing quotes; not a primary source. Contemporary canvas rubber-soled shoe listings are in the Spalding 1917 catalogue note.

## Key Claims
- Converse (founded 1908) created the "All Star" for basketball in 1917; it "featured a traction-filled rubber sole and high-top cotton-based canvas upper" and was slow to catch on.
- Converse made a tennis shoe and a traction-focused rubber sneaker in 1915.
- Chuck Taylor, an Indiana-born All-American player and coach, took a sales job with Converse in 1921 at the Chicago sales office; he helped add ankle support and rework the outsole formula for traction.
- "By 1923" Converse had put a patch with Taylor's name on the ankle ("the first signature sneaker of all-time by 1923", per SI; Converse's marketing VP Rodney Rambo calls Taylor "one of the first endorsed athletes in the world"). Other sources date the signature differently [date disputed].
- Worn by the first US Olympic basketball team in 1936, in the 1939 NCAA title game, and by nearly every NBA player when the league formed in 1949; multiple colours arrived in the late 1960s; Tree Rollins last wore it in an NBA game in 1979.
