## Summary
Lacoste's corporate account of the L.12.12 shirt. The maker states movement as the design reason and dates the shirt to 1933, but its own pages disagree on when René Lacoste first wore short sleeves on court (1920s vs 1933).

## Key Claims
- The L.12.12 was "Invented by René Lacoste himself in 1933."
- Design reason: he "chose to defy the dress codes of tennis of his time and thus free movement" and wanted "a sportier silhouette that was more adapted to the discipline."
- Fabric: "Small, finely honeycombed cotton piqué, the Lacoste signature material."
- Name decoded: "L for Lacoste", "1 for petit piqué", "2 for 2 short sleeves", "12 for the number of prototypes realised".
- Lacoste's corporate history page: nicknamed "the crocodile" by the American press in Boston in 1923 (a bet over a crocodile-leather suitcase); later had a crocodile sewn on his white blazers; world No. 1 in 1926-27 with seven Grand Slam titles; 1933 the brand put its logo on clothing; he patented a racket string shock absorber in 1960 and a steel racket in 1963.
- Search-result extracts of other Lacoste pages also cite André Gillier (hosiery manufacturer, Troyes) as co-founder in 1933 and a 1927 crocodile emblem drawn by Robert George; the pages fetched here did not repeat these, so treat as [unverified on this page].
- Not on this page: Gillier, 1927, 1929 or any collar detail.
