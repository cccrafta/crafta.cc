## Summary
Two US Department of Agriculture radio scripts describing work clothes designed by clothing specialists Clarice Scott and Margaret Smith, with design choices stated per job. They give a government record of designing garments by task analysis (reaching, stooping, kneeling), including underarm insets, pleats and snag-free pockets. Full OCR text was read; the related 1941 item is archive.org id newworkclothesfo1941unit.
## Key Claims
- Broadcast dated Friday 13 March 1942, USDA Radio Service; the Bureau of Home Economics had released the designs the previous summer to pattern companies and ready-to-wear makers.
- Method stated: designers "study a particular job, note how much reaching, stooping, kneeling it calls for", decide on safety features, then fit the garment to the job and to economical cutting from regulation-width cloth and easy laundering.
- Names designers Clarice Scott and Margaret Smith; 16 designs released by that date, for farm, factory, laboratory, kitchen and nurse work.
- 1941 script: pockets were placed "so they wouldn't catch on things" and outfits had "no loose sashes or ties" for work near machinery.
- A coverette (one-piece coverall) had snaps at the ankles to keep trouser legs out of dirt when kneeling, and "puts the fullness for stooping where it will do the most good"; the suggested cotton was seersucker for coolness.
- A field suit had half sleeves that snap on and off to protect from sunburn and scratches.
- The "Protect-all" coat had a hood cut in one with the coat, "roomy pleats, roomy pockets, and a square inset under each arm" for "plenty of room for action", and was made of "shower-proof cotton".
- The nurse's uniform had collapsible pockets that lie flat when empty and an adjustable belt with sliding buckle.
- The scripts do not use the terms gusset or action back; the "square inset under each arm" is the gusset in plain words.
