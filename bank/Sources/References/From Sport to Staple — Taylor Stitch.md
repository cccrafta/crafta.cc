## Summary
Brand-journal essay arguing athletic garments entered everyday dress because they were built for performance. Closest thing found to the Crafta thesis but it is retailer content, undated, unauthored, and makes no workwear comparison; use as a position to answer, not as evidence.

## Key Claims
- Central argument: durability, comfort and ease of movement carried athletic garments into everyday wardrobes, and they feel familiar without seeming dated.
- Rugby shirt: 19th-century hard-wearing uniform for a full-contact game, dense cotton jersey, rubber buttons to resist play.
- 1920s: French tennis player Rene Lacoste introduced a short-sleeve knit shirt in breathable cotton pique, replacing stiff long-sleeve court shirts (single retailer claim; Lacoste's own site dates the L.12.12 to 1933).
- Terry crewneck sweatshirt developed early 20th century as a football training garment, replacing itchy wool jerseys.
- From about the 1950s college students wore sweatshirts, rugby shirts and polos as a casual uniform pushing back on earlier formality.
- Over the following fifty years workplaces relaxed; the article calls this the "casualification" of dress.
- Gap confirmed: the article makes no workwear parallel (only links a separate corduroy post).
