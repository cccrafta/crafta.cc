## Summary
Museum exhibition page for *Sporting Life*, organised by Jennifer Farley and Colleen Hill from the permanent collection, tracing how sportswear and fashion have influenced each other from the mid-19th century to the present. Curatorial framing from the museum's own page; the page was read through a summarising fetch, so quotation is avoided.

## Key Claims
- Ran 25 May to 5 November 2011 in the Fashion & Textile History Gallery; over 100 garments, accessories and textiles, plus an online exhibition.
- Organised by activity: exercise and dance (including an 1896 gym suit, Claire McCardell, Norma Kamali 1981, Isabel Toledo), bicycling, hunting, sailing, swimming, golf, skating, motoring, surfing, skiing, baseball (varsity jacket).
- Hunting section: one of the oldest sporting activities, with dress tied to the privileged classes of the 18th and 19th centuries; includes a c.1926 Norfolk suit, whose relaxed style the page says was "first introduced in the 1860s", and its later influence on Yves Saint Laurent, Jean Paul Gaultier and Manolo Blahnik.
- Bicycling section: a c.1888 ensemble with a divided skirt designed for both mobility and modesty; a 1985 men's racing outfit in stretch materials.
- Textile innovation section pairs a 1930s Neva-Wet wool ski suit with Patagonia's 1995 nylon ski ensemble, and a neoprene wetsuit with a 1994 Donna Karan neoprene dress; spandex and neoprene from active sportswear increasingly appear in fashion.
- The page cites Jennifer Craik's point that the apparel industry's use of "sportswear" for casual clothing shows how far specialist sports clothing has shaped ideas about dress (paraphrase, not a verified quotation).
- Curatorial framing is two-way ("influenced each other"), not one-way function-to-fashion.
