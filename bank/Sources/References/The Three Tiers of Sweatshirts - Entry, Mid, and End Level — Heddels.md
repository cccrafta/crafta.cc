## Summary
Heddels buyer's guide (May 2019) that doubles as a map of who makes heritage sweats today and at what construction level. Credits the sweatshirt blueprint to 1920 and places Reverse Weave as the mid-tier benchmark.

## Key Claims
- The modern sweatshirt follows a blueprint "little changed in over 100 years", credited to college football player Benjamin Russell Jr. in 1920 (Russell's own site gives 1926; see Russell Athletic notes in the cache).
- Champion Reverse Weave was developed in the late 1930s and early 1950s (priced about $60 at time of writing) and is the mid-tier benchmark ($70-$150).
- Entry tier (under $70): Uniqlo, Gap, Russell Athletic, Comfort Colors, Pro Club; fleece-back, cotton or 50/50 blends.
- Mid tier also names Camber, Reigning Champ, House of Blanks (made by Canadian mill Roopa), Grown & Sewn, Lady White Co.
- End tier ($175 and up, to roughly $300): vintage-inspired detail such as flat-lock stitching, double-V gussets, set-in sleeves, often loopwheeled; some dyed with dorozome mud or kuri chestnut-skin.
- End-tier makers named are mostly Japanese: Warehouse & Co., Wonder Looper, The Strike Gold, Iron Heart, Buzz Rickson's, The Real McCoy's, Studio D'Artisan, The Flat Head, UES, Dubbleworks, Whitesville, Pure Blue Japan; also Merz b. Schwanen (Germany).
- The article states a higher price does not make a sweatshirt objectively better, only more specific and time-intensive.
