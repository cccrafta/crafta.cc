## Summary
Collector-reference profile of the M1941 herringbone twill (HBT) fatigue suit, quoting the Quartermaster fabric specification and construction. It documents a work uniform (replacing denim fatigues) that later became a field uniform, with a rear yoke and shoulder pleats. Read via WebFetch summary; compressed portions of the page were not retrieved.
## Key Claims
- Fabric: "Cloth, Cotton, Herringbone Twill, Specification QMC 6-261, 1938"; jacket and trousers 8.5 oz cloth; waistband, right fly and pockets lined with 7.5 oz unbleached drill. The page gives "8.5-ounce" without a per-yard unit.
- War Department approval 29 April 1938; one-piece HBT mass procurement from fall 1940; the two-piece suit decision came in spring 1941 (jacket PQD 45 dated 3 April or 20 May 1941; the page is inconsistent).
- Replaced the denim chore uniform used since the end of World War I; the page says HBT wore longer and suited field colours, and raised suit cost about 50 percent.
- The 1936 Infantry Board tested an olive-drab HBT coverall against the blue denim one.
- Jacket: unlined, single-ply, rear yoke, shoulder pleats, pleated front pockets with button flaps, banded waist with adjustable buckle straps.
- Trousers: khaki-pattern cut reused so existing khaki makers could produce them, two canted front pockets, two rear slit pockets, watch pocket, concealed fly, double-stitched leg seams and "extra material for bending" in the seat; amendment 2 (3 March 1942) raised the seat about 2 inches.
- The page says nothing about gussets or knee reinforcement; the later change to two large side cargo pockets (OD7 trousers) is mentioned but not dated here.
