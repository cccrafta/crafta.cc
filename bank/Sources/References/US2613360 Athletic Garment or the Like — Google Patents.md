## Summary
The second Champion patent, adding side gussets to the horizontal-rib sweatshirt and sweat pants to fix the width shrinkage that the 1938 patent left unsolved. It is the patent Champion cites as the Reverse Weave grant date.

## Key Claims
- Inventors Samuel N. Friedland and William F. Feinbloom; assignee Champion Knitwear Co., Inc., Rochester, N.Y. Filed 13 August 1951, granted 14 October 1952.
- Subject: sweat shirts and sweat pants "worn by athletes" and others in sports.
- Problem: laundering shrinks the garment more in width than a wearer can easily stretch it back. Claims describe a body "subject to reduction in width when laundered or stretched longitudinally".
- Body fabric is knitted with courses running horizontally (so it can be pulled back to length); inserts run the other way.
- Fix: stretchy "vertically extending expansion gussets provided at opposite sides of body of the shirt", typically about five inches wide (figure OCR-garbled on the page).
- Sweat pants: gusset along the inner side of each leg, "inconspicuously disposed", no seam on the outer leg.
- Intent: a close, snug fit that does not hinder arms, shoulders or head; loose hanging fabric can snag on other players.
