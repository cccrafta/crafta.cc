## Summary
Will Varnam's January 2019 Heddels profile traces Patagonia from Chouinard's blacksmith and climbing-hardware years through the rugby shirt, Stand Up shorts and synthetic fleece. Strong on the canvas Stand Up short; it does not itself call any garment workwear. WebFetch summary.
## Key Claims
- In 1970 Chouinard returned from Scotland with a team rugby shirt made by Umbro in England, described as yellow and blue striped heavy-duty cotton with a collar that kept slings and ropes off the neck. (Patagonia's own page describes blue with red and yellow stripes: colour disputed.)
- Chouinard Equipment then ordered rugby shirts from England, New Zealand and Argentina; a Hong Kong order of 3,000 shirts a month in eight colourways arrived late and poor, nearly bankrupting the company; Kris McDivitt became CEO/GM in 1979.
- Stand Up shorts date from the 1970s; Chouinard drew the pattern himself and the originals were sewn from number 10 canvas duck with a seat and large pockets.
- Origin of the name: the foreman's wife sewed them on a walking-foot machine normally used for leather patches on Chouinard Equipment packs, and joked that they 'stood up' by themselves.
- Chouinard's father was a laborer and tradesman who also repaired looms at a woolen mill; Chouinard taught himself blacksmithing in 1957 and forged pitons he sold at $1.50 each.
- By 1970 Chouinard Equipment was the largest US climbing hardware supplier; in 1972 the catalog carried an environmental essay, pitons gave way to chocks, and the clothing line was branded Patagonia with the Fitzroy logo (other sources say 1973: date disputed).
- The article places Synchilla and Capilene in the late 1970s; Patagonia's own history gives 1984-85. Treat Heddels' date as an error.
- Heddels does not use the word workwear; the nearest link is the heavy canvas and the tradesman and blacksmith background (the article's framing, not a stated claim).
