## Summary
Student-newspaper interview by Alys Key with Dr Martin Polley, a sport and leisure historian at the University of Southampton. Short and popular, but it gives an academic's statement of the sport-to-street argument and some dated examples. Reliability medium because it is a student paper; the speaker is a specialist. The page does not discuss class, amateurism or the military.

## Key Claims
- Polley: Victorian and Edwardian cyclists were "at the forefront of the movement for what was known as rational dress", helping the move "away from corsets and towards bloomers and trousers for women".
- He contrasts the Edwardian tennis player Dorothea Lambert Chambers, who played in a corset and long dress, with modern players; performance science is only one reason clothing changed, shifting attitudes to the body is another.
- Olympic swimmers wore all-wool costumes into the 1920s, against today's shark-skin-style suits.
- Suzanne Lenglen, the biggest name of the 1920s, was copied for her dresses and bandeau on and off court; the Duke of Windsor helped popularise the golfing sweater; Lacoste and Fred Perry were founded by sportsmen.
- The sport-fashion link is "partly about style, functionality and comfort" in everyday clothing, since many sportswear wearers "never come close to regular exercise"; it is also about aspiration, as sportswear "carries signifiers of activity, glamour, and vitality".
- Some sports still impose women's clothing rules on cultural norms rather than practicality (Wimbledon dresses or skirts; beach volleyball bikini rule loosened 2012).
