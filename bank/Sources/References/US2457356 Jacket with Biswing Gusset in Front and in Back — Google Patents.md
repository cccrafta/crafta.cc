## Summary
Patent for a sleeve-to-body insert (bi-swing) placed at both back and front of a zip jacket so the arm can move without binding. The text says earlier garments, mainly men's, had one only at the back, so it is a first-hand record that the back version already existed by 1947. Full specification and claims were readable.
## Key Claims
- Inventor Charles E. Enck; assignee Cresco Manufacturing Company, Ashland, Ohio; filed 31 December 1947, granted 28 December 1948.
- States the problem as binding across the shoulders and front: "It is well known that in garments having long sleeves there is considerable binding across the shoulders".
- Also names front restriction from rearward arm movement: "considerable restriction has been occasioned across the front of the garment by rearward arm movement"; aim is that "complete freedom of arm movement can be obtained".
- A bi-swing is an insert between the body panel and the sleeve, made of two panels stitched together along one edge so they fold flat against the garment when the arm hangs down.
- Per the patent, earlier garments, mainly men's, had a bi-swing only at the back; the invention adds a matching one at the front.
- Named use is "sport jackets and many other kinds of outer garments having full-length zippers"; the text also says the idea suits any garment with a sleeve.
- The patent text does not mention workwear or golf (golf appears only in the title of a later citing patent from Taylor Made Golf), so a work-origin for the bi-swing is not established by this source.
