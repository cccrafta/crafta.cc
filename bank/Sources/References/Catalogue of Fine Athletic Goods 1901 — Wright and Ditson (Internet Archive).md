## Summary
Boston/Providence sporting goods maker's catalogue, 1901-1902 per Internet Archive metadata. It shows the athletic knit garment before the cotton sweatshirt: jerseys and sweaters in wool, sold on the grounds of elasticity and free limb movement. Read via the OCR text (identifier catalogueoffinea00wrig).

## Key Claims
- Football jerseys, best quality: "made of the finest combed Australian wool, knit to shape of body and arms, and being elastic and strong, give free use to the limbs without binding or impeding them in any way." Full sleeve, roll collar; colours black, navy, white, royal blue, crimson.
- Best jersey No. 1P, full fashioned, $4.00 (striped to order $4.50). Standard quality No. 10P, "full fashioned, made of fine quality worsted, and knit to shape", $2.50.
- "Padded Jerseys" cost $1.00 to $1.50 extra "according to style of padding"; the catalogue also sells "Special Pads for Jerseys and Pants" to be sewn on.
- Athletic sweaters Nos. 1-A, 1-B, 1-C of "the finest quality Australian wool, fashioned to the body and arms. The heaviest and warmest sweater made." Priced $6.00 (extra heavy), $5.00, $4.00.
- Also lists a golf sweater (low neck, lambs' wool), a jacket sweater, and a canvas football jacket "substantially made, without sleeves", 50c.
- Basket ball uniforms and long-sleeve jerseys are listed in the same colour range, showing the knit jersey as the shared garment across sports at this date.
- No sweat shirt (cotton, fleece-backed pullover) appears in the OCR text of this catalogue.
