## Summary
Gramicci's own journal account of its origin in 1982 as a climbing short and pant made by Stonemaster Mike Graham. Brand marketing with no citations; useful as the maker's stated list of what climbers wore before and what Graham borrowed. WebFetch summary.
## Key Claims
- The page says the brand began in 1982 'after a few years of selling his hand-made wares to his friends'; the same page says '40 years since the first pair of Gramicci shorts', so first sale versus first garment is blurred.
- Before Gramicci, Stonemasters in Yosemite wore 'bright white painter pants, old jeans and olive-drab military fatigues' from army surplus stores; earlier generations 'stiff wool breeches and ill-fitting cords'.
- Design: 'A diamond-shaped gusset found on a pair of kung-fu trousers' claimed to give 'full 180 degree flexibility'. Source of the gusset is martial arts, not workwear.
- Fabric: shorts used 'hard-wearing canvas, inspired by the work-wear world' (maker's own wording).
- A nylon webbing belt built in, modelled on backpack straps, adjustable one-handed.
- The brand calls them 'the first true climbing shorts', with the G-Pants following by word of mouth through Graham's climbing network.
- By the mid-80s 'a small contingent of surfers and skaters' sought them out; the pants reached Japan by the early 90s.
- The page places Graham's workshop in a garage in Oxnard, Ventura County (the Gripped interview places early work with Chouinard in Ventura).
