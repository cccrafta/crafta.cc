## Summary
Dior's house museum explains how a couture house divides work between two atelier types. Read directly via curl. A museum-grade statement from a couture house, not from the Fédération, V&A, Galliera or the Met (none was found with the explanation).

## Key Claims
- "In a fashion house, the work is divided between two different types of ateliers: the 'flou' and the 'tailleur'."
- The flou makes dresses in soft fabrics; the tailleur makes the more "structured" outfits, which require a different technique.
- Each atelier has a head and a team of "hands" ranked first qualified hand, second hand or apprentice; Dior currently has one flou and one tailleur haute couture atelier of around thirty people each.
- Work starts from the designer's sketch; the atelier builds a toile, usually plain cotton canvas, which the artistic director must approve before the garment is made in the final fabric.
