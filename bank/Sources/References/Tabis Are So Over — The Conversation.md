## Summary
Essay by Catherine Greenwood (University of Lancashire), dated 2026-10-07 on Yahoo UK, originally The Conversation. Semiotic argument for how the tabi gained and lost insider meaning. Via WebFetch summary.

## Key Claims
- A split toe has no inherent avant-garde meaning; it was built by repeated exposure on runways, editorials and celebrities (Barthes).
- Once tied to Margiela, the shoe read as experimental style rather than a Japanese form; wearing it signalled insider taste because the shape, unlike a logo, had to be recognised (Bourdieu).
- Social media spread the shoe and explanations of it until it became shared visual vocabulary and lost insider-signal power.
- Timeline: 1988 Margiela reworks it for his first runway collection; July 2025 British Vogue calls it "unexpectedly mainstream" citing resale and cheaper imitations; summer 2026 peak; September 2026 Teen Vogue NYFW street-style headline "Tabis Are Over".
- Greenwood: not dead, only mainstream.
