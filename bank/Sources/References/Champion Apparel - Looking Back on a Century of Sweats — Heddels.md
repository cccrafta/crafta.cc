## Summary
Heddels brand history of Champion, tracing it from a 1919 college-team knitter to hip-hop and high-fashion collaborations. Useful for the tool-to-uniform-to-fashion arc in a single brand, plus a warning that most current Champion is blended and made offshore.

## Key Claims
- The Feinbloom brothers founded Knickerbocker Knitting Company in 1919; it later became Champion. Its first big break was making sweatshirts for University of Michigan sports teams.
- Reverse Weave: heavy cotton fleece cut on the cross-grain so it resists vertical shrinkage under heavy institutional laundering (the problem was industrial washing machines shrinking and warping team kit).
- In the 1940s and 50s the US Army used sweats for training, which carried the garment beyond campuses.
- Muhammad Ali trained in Champion sweats in 1963.
- Late 1960s: Champion developed mesh shorts, nylon-lined jackets and sports bras.
- Champion became tied to the NBA and the 1990s Olympic "Dream Team"; hip-hop artists (Raekwon, Method Man, Nas quoted) name-checked the brand.
- Collaborations with Supreme, Vetements, Peanuts, Todd Snyder, Beams Plus, WTAPS and others drove the hype era.
- Current Reverse Weave is mostly blended with some polyester and made in Cambodia or Honduras; Champion sporadically makes a Made in USA line, much of it for the Japanese market (Hinoya cited at $105-$195).
