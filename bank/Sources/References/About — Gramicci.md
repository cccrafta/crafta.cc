## Summary
Gramicci's own brand history: a Yosemite Stonemaster climber made the first true climbing shorts because painter pants and army surplus restricted high-stepping. WebFetch returned a summary with short quotes, so quotes below are as reported by the tool. Maker statement (T1 for origin claims, but self-told founding myth).

## Key Claims
- Brand dates its start to 1982 and says it "started out in a garage in Ventura, California" (other retailer copy says Yosemite; the maker page names Ventura).
- Founder Mike Graham is described as a Stonemaster "with a keen eye for design"; in 1970s Yosemite, climbers focused on speed and style needed clothing that moved better than painter pants or army surplus ("These new methods required a new uniform").
- First product was climbing shorts in tough canvas, for durability; trousers followed and were known as G-Pants.
- Crotch gusset: "A diamond-shaped gusset found on a pair of kung-fu trousers was added to the crotch for full 180 degree flexibility".
- Waist: a nylon webbing belt integrated into the waist, modelled on backpack straps and adjustable one-handed (useful while hanging on a wall), plus an elastic waistband for comfort.
- Name comes from a moniker Graham gave himself during an attempted "all-Italian" first ascent of Half Dome by Stonemasters (none Italian).
- Surfers and skaters adopted the pants by the mid-1980s; the brand "also found favor on the streets of Tokyo" and now has a Japanese base.
- Stated through-line: "hard-wearing, functional clothing, designed with movement firmly in mind".
