## Summary
Patent for a garment back that widens when the wearer reaches or swings the arms and is pulled back to shape by an elastic element when the arms return. It names work garments and uniforms, not sport, as the use case. Full text and four claims were readable.
## Key Claims
- Inventor Abraham Katz of New York; filed 12 February 1941 (Serial No. 378,593), granted 6 July 1943.
- Embodiment 1: "a pair of inwardly and oppositely disposed folds or pleats 29 and 30" on the inner surface, with outer pleats 33 and 34 visible outside and spare fabric between them.
- Embodiment 2 uses two overlapping back members, the second member's inner edge slipping under the first.
- "Bridging the gap between the inner pleats 29 and 30, is an elastic control element 39"; it is attached with snap fasteners or hook and eye so it can be removed before cleaning, sterilizing and pressing.
- Names "work garments", "uniforms, dresses, or the like"; no golf or sports use is named.
- Movements it designs for are stooping, lifting, and bringing the arms together or swinging them forward; it states "Free and unbound movement of the shoulders and swinging of the wearers arms forwardly of the wearer".
- Shows the expanding pleated back being patented for work and uniform garments in 1941-43, before the term action back dominates sportswear patents.
