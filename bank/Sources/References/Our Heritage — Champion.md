## Summary
Champion's own corporate timeline. Maker's account of founding, Reverse Weave, team supply and collegiate apparel. Useful as the maker's framing, but its Reverse Weave dates conflict with the patent record (see US2126186 and US2613360 notes), so use patents for dates.

## Key Claims
- "The company was started May 6, 1919 as Knickerbocker Knitting Mills", by three members of the Feinbloom family in Rochester, N.Y.
- Reverse Weave: "Invented in 1934, Champion applied for patenting in 1938 which was eventually granted in 1952." The page banner says "Created in 1938. Patented in 1952." (internal inconsistency).
- Construction wording: "Cut on the grossgrain, the Reverse Weave sweatshirt prevents shrinkage"; problem named as "the shrinkage that came with frequent washing of sports apparel"; called "the world's first engineered sweatshirt".
- 1926: Champion supplied uniforms to Wentworth Military Academy, "the first of many teams".
- 1934: "Champacraft" flocking process allowed college insignia to be printed affordably; with Moe's Sport Shop, University of Michigan hoodies and sweatshirts. Champion says this "gave birth to collegiate apparel as we know it" (maker's claim).
- 1950 PE uniforms; 1967 mesh jersey; 1992 US men's Dream Team outfitted.
- Reverse Weave hoodie shown in MoMA's 2017 "Items: Is Fashion Modern?" and entered MoMA's permanent collection in 2018 (per Champion; not independently verified here).
- Champion's separate US page calls itself "Inventor of the Hoodie"; the visible page text did not support that claim.
