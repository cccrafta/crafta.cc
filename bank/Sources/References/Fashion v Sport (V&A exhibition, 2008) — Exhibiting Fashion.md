## Summary
University of the Arts London research page on the V&A's *Fashion v Sport*, curated by Ligaya Salazar and timed to the 2008 Olympics, with a catalogue containing essays by Christopher Breward, Sophie Woodward and Mark Simpson. It frames the sport/fashion relation as a contemporary entanglement rather than a historical migration. Secondary documentation: the V&A's own exhibition page and press release PDF could not be read (the PDF was unreadable binary).

## Key Claims
- Curator: Ligaya Salazar (V&A Contemporary team). Catalogue: *Fashion v Sport*, V&A Publishing, 2008, 128 pages, ISBN 9781851775330, with essays by Christopher Breward, Sophie Woodward and Mark Simpson (title and authors verified via the UAL page and bookseller/library records; essay titles not verified).
- Dates disagree between sources: the UAL page says 5 September 2008 to 4 January 2009; library and press-timed sources give 5 August 2008 (Dazed Digital reported it opening in the week of 7 August 2008). Closing date 4 January 2009 is consistent. Flag before citing.
- Stated premise: fashion and sport have become "increasingly intertwined in recent years", two major cultural and economic forces; signs include luxury designers' sportswear ranges and athletes launching clothing brands.
- Three sections: designers drawing heavily on sportswear (Bernhard Willhelm, Charles de Castelbajac); sportswear customised by cult designers; "sportswear fanaticism" centred on sneaker collectors.
- Reported to have toured from the V&A to the Walker Art Gallery, Liverpool, until 31 May 2009 (FashionUnited, via search result).
- Not found: any V&A statement that treats workwear as a precursor. The show is about the present-day relationship.
