## Summary
Zach Liollio (27 July 2023) on the varsity jacket as a garment built to carry a letter, from Harvard baseball sweaters to school-life uniform to hip-hop and fashion. Strong evidence for the "ontology" shift: the object stops marking athletic achievement and starts marking belonging.

## Key Claims
- Construction: waist-length, knit collar, ribbed cuffs and hem, melton or boiled wool body, leather or faux-leather sleeves, two-tone muted school colours.
- Harvard is credited with the tradition: in the 1860s baseball players wore an "H" on gray pullovers; football followed about a decade later. Early sweaters belonged to the team, though MVPs could keep theirs.
- The "fireman theory" traces the lettering to 1800s volunteer fire brigades; rowing blazers and knit trim may also have influenced the design.
- Knit cardigans became warm-up gear in the late 1800s; button-front "coat jerseys" followed. The 1920s baseball boom (Babe Ruth in team cardigans) spread the style.
- Butwin Sportswear Company of Saint Paul, Minnesota, a prominent midcentury maker, held the patent for what became the classic varsity jacket.
- Pop-culture adoption: 1950s teen films, American Graffiti, Happy Days, Teen Wolf; the jacket stood for achievement but also the "jock bully" stereotype; hip-hop adopted patch-heavy versions in the 80s-90s.
- Makers cited: Cockpit USA ($790), Dehen 1920 of Portland ($625-$645), Falcon Garments ($505-$525).
- Heddels baseball Pt. 2 (companion piece, Aug 2023) repeats: after WWII letterman jackets spread to high schoolers in non-sport activities; in the 1980s NBA/NFL franchises made their own versions.
