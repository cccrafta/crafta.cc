## Summary
The house's own product-history blurb for the Tabi. Read directly via curl; very short, and the maker's account of its own design.

## Key Claims
- "The Tabi is the house's first shoe design, presented during our first collection in 1988."
- The house describes it as "inspired by a 15th century Japanese sock design, cut to follow the contour of the foot."
- The page gives only the year 1988 (no season); the first collection was the one titled SS89, shown in October 1988 according to secondary sources not read here.
- Construction as the house tells it (added from a second read via WebFetch, a model summary): the first version sat on "a wide cylindrical heel mirroring the diameter of the human heel", which the house says it still makes; base preparation takes up to four hours, 11 artisans are involved, and each shoe is "finished with a single white stitch".
- At the first-ever show the soles were "dipped in red paint" and left split-toed footprints on the white runway; the page also mentions a MaisonMargiela/Folders exhibition in Chengdu of Tabi collectors' personal collections.
