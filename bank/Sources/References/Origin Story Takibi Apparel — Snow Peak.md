## Summary
Snow Peak's own blog account (Savanna Frimoth, 14 March 2022) of its flame-retardant Takibi apparel, explicitly "inspired by classic workwear styles". Maker material, summarised via WebFetch.

## Key Claims
- Snow Peak apparel launched in 2015; in 2016 designers set out to make a specialty line for time around the fire.
- Reason stated: flame-retardant fabric lets wearers stand near fire without worrying about sparks; existing flame-retardant cloth from military, firefighter and first-responder uniforms was rarely comfortable for casual wear.
- Fabric developed with Teijin from aramid blended with Kanecaron (modacrylic), mixed with cotton, linen and other conventional fabrics; heavier aramid blends for winter, Kanecaron for lightweight layers; research and testing in Niigata.
- The pieces are "inspired by classic workwear styles" and put function first with utilitarian loops, pouches and large pockets.
- Stated intent "to blur the boundaries between urban and rural environments".
- Claims each garment keeps flame resistance through washing (as stated by the maker; separate product pages say the removable liner of the ripstop jacket is not fire-resistant).
- Snow Peak was founded in 1958 in Sanjo, Niigata by Yukio Yamai, a mountaineer who made climbing equipment (retailer/secondary sources).
