## Summary
Spalding's fall 1917 athletic goods catalogue (page footers read 5 August 1917; Internet Archive's own date field of 1972 is a cataloguing error). It documents, in the maker's words, why knit jerseys were fashioned rather than cut and sewn, the sweater as the standard athletic garment, and canvas rubber-soled shoes in the year Converse launched the All Star. Read via OCR text (spaldingfallcata00agsp).

## Key Claims
- Spalding Intercollegiate Jerseys, No. 1P: "Full regular made; that is, fashioned or knit to exact shape on the machine and then put together by hand, altogether different from cutting them out of a piece of material and sewing them up on a machine, as are a majority of garments known as jerseys." Special quality worsted, $5.00 each.
- "All Spalding jerseys and other knit goods are made in our own sanitary factories in Brooklyn, N.Y., Chicago, Ill., or San Francisco, Cal."
- Spalding cotton jerseys (No. 5 light weight, No. 6 fashioned, $1.50) were sold alongside worsted ones, in black, navy, gray and maroon.
- "The Sweater, as the athletic garment par excellence, has achieved its position through sheer merit and to-day it is valued more than ever, by those who want comfort when outdoors in any kind of weather." Reversible-collar worsted sweaters with raglan sleeves, $16.50.
- Stretch allowance: "We allow two inches for stretch in all our jerseys" and four inches in sweaters when marking sizes.
- Heavy 10-ounce brown canvas is specified for football protective jackets and padding (reinforced inseams); canvas is used as a tough padding cover, not yet a garment.
- Canvas shoes: Spalding High Grade Canvas Shoes with "Best White Rubber Soles, Best Grade White Canvas Uppers", high cut men's $2.00, low cut $1.75; Special Grade $1.40. A "Special Canvas Top Basket Ball Shoe" with "special quality soft rubber soles. These soles absolutely hold on the most slippery floor. Light weight, durable, correct in design", $3.50.
- Gymnasium shoes "must be comfortable and easy, yet fit snugly and give the wearer a sure footing; they must also be durable."
- Baseball undershirt No. US: "for wearing under regular base ball shirt", gray; cotton shirts with wool back and fleece lining (No. TH, $3.00) were sold as training shirts.
