## Summary
V&A collection record for a grey flannel coat-and-skirt "costume" made by Redfern in London around 1911, worn by Heather Firbank. The record's summary doubles as a short museum statement on how a couturier's tennis, yachting and archery clothes became everyday wear for fashionable women from the 1870s. Read via a summarising fetch.

## Key Claims
- Object: coat and skirt, ca. 1911, London, Redfern (John Redfern); wool flannel and velvet lined with silk; tailored; accession T.39&A-1960; part of a Heather Firbank (1888-1954) wardrobe the museum holds from 1905 to 1921, acquired 1960.
- V&A text: from the 1870s John Redfern made practical tailored clothes for increasingly active women, who had taken up tennis, yachting and archery alongside riding.
- His clothes for these activities "were adopted as everyday wear" by royalty, actresses and fashionable women.
- Redfern opened houses in London and Paris in 1881, New York in 1884 and Rhode Island the following year; closed 1932, briefly reopened 1936, closed 1940.
- Coat-and-skirt sets like this were not called suits until after the First World War.
- Corroboration: Jean Williams describes the "Redfern Suit" and Alexandra's patronage (see Kit introduction note).
