## Summary
Kevin Steinberger's explainer on loopwheel knitting and how to identify it. Frames loopwheeling as the knitwear equivalent of selvedge: a slow, obsolete production method kept alive by demand for its fabric.

## Key Claims
- Loopwheeling knits fabric on a circular tube-knitting machine in a near tensionless process; used for sweatshirt fabric from the mid-1920s.
- Faster tube-knit machines appeared and loopwheelers began to disappear in the 1940s and 50s.
- Most sweatshirts today are made on sinker circular machines, about 30 times faster, cheaper and easier to maintain.
- Only a few loopwheel machines still run, in small factories in Japan, Canada and Germany.
- Loopwheeled fabric feels plump with a slightly irregular, slubby texture; tubular construction means no side seams (not a reliable test alone).
- Loopwheeled items cost roughly twice as much as non-loopwheeled.
- Specialists named: Studio D'Artisan, Strike Gold, Merz b. Schwanen, Momotaro. Example prices: Real McCoy's 10 oz parka $255, Iron Heart 14 oz zip hoodie $310.
- Caveat in the article: a non-loopwheeled sweatshirt of good cotton can still be excellent.
