## Summary
Trade-press centenary feature (Sept 2019), largely drawn from Champion's own account, that gives the patent dates and product milestones cleanly. Use for patent facts; treat the hoodie and "first" claims as the company's own.

## Key Claims
- 1919: brothers Abraham and William Feinbloom founded Knickerbocker Knitting Mills in Rochester, NY, to supply college sports teams; the University of Michigan was the first major university tie.
- 1930s: renamed Champion Knitting Mills Inc., supplying colleges, universities and physical-education programmes.
- 9 August 1938: Samuel N. Friedland received a patent assigned to Champion Knitwear Co. Inc. for a new way of making athletic shirts: turning the tubular knit 90 degrees before cutting, marketed as Reverse Weave.
- 14 October 1952: Friedland and William Feinbloom granted a second patent, "Athletic Garment Or Like", covering sweatshirts and sweatpants (side gussets; see the Houyhnhnm note).
- Champion says it created the hooded sweatshirt as a sideline warm-up layer (the article places this in the 1970s-80s; other sources place the sideline parka earlier, so date is unverified).
- A Reverse Weave hoodie appeared in MoMA's 2017 "Items: Is Fashion Modern?" exhibition.
- Reversible "Double Sided" tee for splitting gym classes into teams; claims first breathable nylon mesh, which became a basketball uniform staple.
- 1989-1997 official NBA outfitter (27 teams); outfitted the 1992 US Olympic "Dream Team".
