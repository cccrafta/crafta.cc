## Summary
Blog post (26 Oct 2020) from the UK Mountain Heritage Trust summarising the Mallory Clothing Replica Project, which analysed fabric fragments from George Mallory's 1924 Everest kit and rebuilt and field-tested it. Read via WebFetch summary, not verbatim.
## Key Claims
- Mallory and Irvine disappeared on Everest in 1924; clothing fragments were found on Mallory's body in 1999, prompting the project.
- The Mallory Clothing Replica Project ran 2002 to 2006, funded by the Heritage Lottery Fund and the Pasold Research Fund, led by Professor Mary B. Rose of Lancaster University.
- Teams at the universities of Southampton, Derby, Leeds and Lancaster analysed the fragments; historians also used 1920s kit lists, receipts and photographs.
- Burberry made the replica outer jacket and breeches; knitted garments were reconstructed from patterns including the Joyce Meader knitting pattern collection.
- Graham Hoyland field-tested a duplicate set on Everest at 5,150 m (Base Camp) and 6,340 m. He found it very comfortable, warm and easy to move in.
- Hoyland judged the clothing probably not warm enough to survive a high-altitude bivouac if something went wrong (a limit: good for movement, poor at rest).
