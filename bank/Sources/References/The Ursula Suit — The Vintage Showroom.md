## Summary
Specialist archive article on Barbour's Ursula submarine suit, covering the prototype run, the surviving prototype in the Barbour Archive and the revised Royal Navy version. Rated T2 (specialist archive); read via a fetch summary only, and the page carries no stated publication date.

## Key Claims
- Prototype dated 1939, with two chest pockets and two pleated skirt pockets.
- One surviving prototype in the Barbour Archive has a brown/tan cotton lining.
- A revised version commissioned 1941/42 for all Royal Navy submarines dropped the chest pockets and replaced the civilian tartan lining with "heavy dark-blue cotton".
- "It is likely that less than twenty suits were manufactured" in the prototype run; this is the author's own estimate, not a documented figure.
- Phillips paid for the prototypes himself.
- Lawrence Nedas & Co of Kent also built the suit identically.
- The author says he confirmed production dates with Barbour.

## Notes
- Date conflict: this page dates the prototype to 1939; Barbour's own history page says 1940 ([[Sources/References/Barbour History — Barbour]]). The two corroborate the standard-issue and Phillips anchor claims but disagree on the year.
- The author's claim that the Ursula suit inspired the International reverses Barbour's own order (International 1936, two-piece 1940).
