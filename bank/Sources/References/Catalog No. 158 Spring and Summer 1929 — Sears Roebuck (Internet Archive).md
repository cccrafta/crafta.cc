## Summary
Mail-order catalogue showing baseball uniforms as mass-market goods by 1929, sold in "Major League" and "Semi-Pro League" grades, with the sweat shirt appearing as a separate item in the index (page 394). The OCR text is column-scrambled, so only legible phrases are quoted.

## Key Claims
- "Major League" Baseball Uniforms: "Professional in every respect", of "Extra heavy athletic flannel, 13 ounces to the double width" containing wool, "strong and durable". Sold complete (shirt, pants, belt, cap, hose) in team lots of nine or more.
- Shirt style: "Professional" V-neck, button front, with a ventilating hole under the arm.
- Semi-Pro grade uniforms use a cheaper cotton athletic flannel, so wool flannel (as in the 1840s uniform) persists as the premium fabric.
- Flannel baseball pants: "A sweat shirt with these pants makes an ideal combination", with a cross-reference "SWEAT SHIRTS SEE INDEX" (index entry: Sweat Shirts, p. 394). The sweat shirt is sold as a separate athletic item by 1929.
- Felt letters are sold separately "only when uniforms are ordered from us" and can be "easily sewed on by purchaser", showing the letter as an applied, ordered item.
- Caveat: the sweatshirt page itself (394) was not read; only the baseball section and index entry are verified.
