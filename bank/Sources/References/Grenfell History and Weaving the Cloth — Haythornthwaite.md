## Summary
The mill's own history pages for Grenfell Cloth, a dense Egyptian cotton developed in 1922-23 for Labrador and later used on Everest and polar expeditions. Pages give dates, requirements and expedition lists but not thread counts. Read through WebFetch summaries with a few verbatim quotes.
## Key Claims
- Dr (later Sir) Wilfred Grenfell lectured in Burnley in 1922 at the invitation of mill-owner Walter Haythornthwaite and remarked on how hard it was to find suitable arctic cloth.
- Requirements as stated: light (walking behind dog teams), strong, weatherproof against rain and snow, windproof, and able to let body moisture escape, which the page calls the most important.
- After a year of experiments the cloth was produced in 1923; Grenfell's letter says: "It is light, durable and very fine looking." Grenfell suggested the name Grenfell Cloth.
- Spun from "the finest Egyptian Cotton"; the looms "had to be specially strengthened"; the cloth was so naturally waterproof that dyeing it was almost impossible. Thread count is not given on the pages.
- A separate marketing company, Haythornthwaite & Sons Ltd, was formed in 1931 solely to market Grenfell Cloth and garments (the weaving firm was T. Haythornthwaite & Sons Ltd, Lodge Mill, Burnley from 1918).
- 1933 Everest expedition: a Grenfell tent pitched at 27,000 feet, in which F. S. Smythe slept 13 hours; "again went to Everest in 1936"; the page's list includes Oxford Ellesmere Land 1934/5, British Graham Land 1934/7 and the 1954 Daily Mail Abominable Snowman expedition.
- The pages do not mention Everest 1924, Shackleton, Antarctica or the RAF, so those claims found elsewhere are unconfirmed by the maker.
- Grenfell Cloth is a cotton, no-coating, high-density weave for windproof breathability, the same principle later associated with Ventile.
