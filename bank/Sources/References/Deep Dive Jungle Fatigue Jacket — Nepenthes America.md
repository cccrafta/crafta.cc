## Summary
Retailer/editorial essay (27 Jan 2022) on the US jungle jacket's design history, covering Natick Labs' 1962 origin and the changes across its versions. Read as a fetch summary only; weights here come from a retailer essay, not a T1 military spec.

## Key Claims
- The US jungle jacket (Tropical Combat Coat) was designed by Natick Labs from 1962 for Special Forces in Vietnam.
- The first troops wore OG-107 cotton sateen, which was too heavy for the heat.
- Type I (1963) was 5.5oz "wind-resistant" cotton poplin.
- Type II (Aug 1964) had flaps over the exposed buttons that snagged on undergrowth, and was 6oz.
- Type III (late 1967) dropped the take-up tabs, gas flap and epaulettes and used "sturdier" cotton ripstop.
- The pocket layout was inspired by the WWII M-42 paratrooper jacket.
- The Engineered Garments SS22 jungle fatigue jacket is listed on Nepenthes (see the Engineered Garments note).

## Notes
- Fetch summary only. Type numbering is contested: Permanent Style says 3 versions, RareGearUSA says 5 patterns. Corroboration is only RareGearUSA and Arzach (T3/T4, snippet).
- No T1 spec (MIL-C) found, so these weights are not triangulated and should not anchor a claim alone.
