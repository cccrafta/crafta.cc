## Summary
A 1979-81 US patent on a water-resistant uniform fabric that cites a printed Ashton Brothers sheet describing Ventile as a long-staple cotton Oxford weave. It is a secondary record of Ventile's construction (not the Shirley Institute patent, which was not found) and gives a measured thread count for a Ventile-type cloth. Page was readable in full.
## Key Claims
- Inventors Howard M. Zins and Edward L. Morris Jr.; assignee Angelica Corp; filed 17 December 1979, granted 25 August 1981; expired.
- Quotes an Ashton Brothers & Company Limited sheet: "VENTILE FABRICS are made from fine, long staple cotton which is spun into yarn and then doubled for additional strength."
- Same sheet: "The cloth is woven in an Oxford weave which ensures that the threads are set in the most compact arrangement."
- Describes the prior all-cotton Oxford fabric as Pima cotton, 72/2 ends and 60/2 picks, 184 by 80 per inch greige and 196 by 82 finished.
- Prior-art fabric wetting time is "only five seconds", falling to two seconds after fifty washings; the invention is a 50/50 cotton/polyester plain weave of 140 by 78 per inch finished with a 1 minute 54 second greige wetting time.
- Says earlier teaching favoured tighter weaves for water resistance; the invention reaches long wetting time with lower combined surface area.
- No fabric weights appear. Treat the Ventile quotes as a 1970s trade description, not the 1940s specification.
