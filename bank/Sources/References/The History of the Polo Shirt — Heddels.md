## Summary
Specialist overview tracing the polo from Indian-origin equestrian shirts to Lacoste's tennis shirt, Izod's US licence and Ralph Lauren's 1972 piqué polo. Useful for the tool-to-fashion arc. Its claim that Lacoste won the US Open in the short-sleeve shirt in 1926-27 conflicts with Lacoste's own 1933 invention date.

## Key Claims
- Early 1900s tennis whites were long-sleeved button-downs, often worn with a tie.
- Early polo players pinned or buttoned collars to stop them flapping; John E. Brooks of Brooks Brothers saw this in England in the late 1800s; Brooks Brothers launched "The Original Button-Down Polo Shirt" in 1896.
- René Lacoste introduced a short-sleeved, three-button shirt in breathable piqué cotton with a crocodile on the left chest; Heddels says he wore it winning the US Open in 1926 and 1927 [disputed: Lacoste's own pages date the L.12.12 to 1933].
- 1933: Lacoste and knitwear maker André Gillier founded La Chemise Lacoste, widely regarded as the first brand with visible external branding.
- Lacoste was not first to sell knitted polos; John Smedley already made them.
- 1951: Izod licensed to make Lacoste shirts in the US; 1960s Ivy Leaguers wore them under sports jackets; polyester began replacing piqué for fade resistance.
- 1972: Ralph Lauren launched a piqué cotton shirt in 24 colours, tagline "it gets better with age", preferring the patina of worn cotton.
