## Summary
A blog reproducing passages from Soetsu Yanagi's The Unknown Craftsman (Kodansha, adapted by Bernard Leach, English first published 1972). SECONDARY quotation: the page gives no page number or translator, so confirm against the printed book (an archive.org lending copy exists: unknowncraftsman0000yana) before publishing. The pdf of The Beauty of Everyday Things had no text layer and could not be read.

## Key Claims
- "Just as men who work hard are usually healthy... so objects that fulfill the daily functions of life are necessarily wholesome."
- "Utility does not permit unsoundness or frailty, for between use and beauty there is a close relation."
- "Utility demands faithfulness in objects; it does not condone human self-indulgence."
- "In creating an object intended for practical use, the maker does not push himself to the foreground"; "This may be one reason why useful goods are beautiful."
- "Objects whose makers remain anonymous have, it seems to me, an easy access to beauty".
- The Unknown Craftsman is the English introduction to the mingei (folk craft) movement; search results attribute "Man is most free when his tools are proportionate to his needs" to it via user-submitted Goodreads (unverified).
