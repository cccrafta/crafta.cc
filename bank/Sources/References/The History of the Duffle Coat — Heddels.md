## Summary
T2 account of the duffle's military origin and Gloverall's civilianisation; dates differ slightly from Gloverall's own.

## Key Claims
- The British duffle is generally credited to John Partridge in the 1850s; the Royal Navy bought large quantities in the late 19th century and sailors called it the 'convoy coat'.
- Military logic: roomy over a uniform, large toggles usable with gloves, a hood that fits over a naval cap, dense water-resisting wool, patch pockets for binoculars.
- Gloverall 'acquired a large number of duffle coats in the early 1950s' and introduced the first fully civilian duffle in 1953 as surplus ran low, with smaller pockets and hood and lighter cloth.
- Today's Original Monty is made from an Italian wool blend with cotton herringbone webbing interior.
