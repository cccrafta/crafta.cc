## Summary
WWD review of the FW26 show (published 2026-04-01) at a Shanghai shipping-container dock, a hybrid of Artisanal and coed ready-to-wear. Full text read directly via curl (not a model summary). Includes Martens quotes on how Artisanal and ready-to-wear relate.

## Key Claims
- The show took place on Wednesday 2026-04-01 at a shipping container dock on the outskirts of Shanghai; WWD calls it a "hybrid of its latest Artisanal and coed ready-to-wear collections" and the house's first traveling runway show.
- "The Shanghai runway show was comprised of 20 percent Artisanal gowns, which were made available for order after the show"; for the first time in years the Artisanal and ready-to-wear teams worked on one project.
- 70-odd looks; Martens avoided 74 because four is considered bad feng shui in Chinese culture.
- Beeswax: a laced mask, waxed and cracked, started the Artisanal lineup, paired with a Victorian-era mourning dress also coated in beeswax; WWD ties the treatment to beeswax's use as a binding agent in ancient China.
- Martens dropped latex white paint over embellished ballgowns, then peeled off the edges; another gown was gold leaf that flicked away as the model moved; 150,000 mini-star stickers on one gown.
- Cascading taffeta gown: 22 metres of fabric, 400 hand-sculpted points, nearly 200 hours.
- Finale dress: a 5 m painting sourced from Marché de Saint-Ouen reassembled into a column dress.
- Martens: "The artisanal has always been embedded within the ready-to-wear, and tailoring has always been one of our hero programs"; the difference now is "we are now much more gown-oriented than in the 1990s."
- A free one-week exhibition on Yandang Road covered Artisanal pieces from 1989 through 2025; the Shenzhen event let guests paint their own garments white, "as the house has been doing to chairs, lamps, you-name-it since 1988."
