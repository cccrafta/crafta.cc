## Summary
DuPont patent for polyether-urethane-urea elastic filaments proposed as a replacement for rubber thread in textiles. It is the documented origin of elastane/spandex (later Lycra) used in stretch workwear and climbing pants. Only the first 100,000 of 129,426 characters were read.
## Key Claims
- Inventor Walter Steuber; filed 31 January 1955, granted 22 March 1960; original assignee E. I. du Pont de Nemours and Company.
- Object: "to provide new synthetic filaments which possess high elastic recovery."
- Need: "There is a particular need in the textile field for elastic filaments to replace rubber".
- Compares against rubber thread: the new filaments have higher modulus and better abrasion resistance.
- Garment rationale quoted: "a garment made of yarns having high tenacity and high stretch modulus will not only be durable"; example application is surgical stockings; fabrics are "more comfortable to wear".
- Defines stretch modulus: "Stretch modulus measures the force required to elongate the yarn a given percentage."
- Does not mention workwear or climbing; the link to stretch cotton and climbing trousers is inference from later use.
