## Summary
IWM collection record for a four-pocket Aertex bush jacket worn in Burma in 1944, with a short account of how the British replaced khaki drill in the Far East. It is the British parallel to the American jungle-clothing development and is in scope (British wearer, Southeast Asia).

## Key Claims
- Fighting in Singapore, Malaya and Burma in khaki drill showed that KD was wrong in colour and material for the jungle.
- Dyeing the KD was a stop-gap; jungle-green Aertex clothing followed, including a battledress-style blouse in widespread use from early 1944.
- The blouse rode up and chafed the waist, so the four-pocket, epauletted Aertex bush jacket was adopted as the more acceptable fix.
- The object belonged to Lance Corporal Perry, 1st Bn Bedfordshire & Hertfordshire Regiment, who wore it on Operation Thursday (the Chindit operation), 1944.

## Notes
- The IWM object carries an "Indian pattern" label; that is a manufacturing label, and the wearer was British, in Burma. Scope check: in scope as British / Southeast Asian; no Indian-subcontinent topic is used.
- IWM sources disagree on the date (1943 vs 1944).
