## Summary
Retailer reference page on the Vietnam-era Tropical Combat Coat, giving its origin in a 1962 Special Forces request to Natick and its first-pattern features. It is the only source in the research for the 1962-63 origin, so those claims stay flagged.

## Key Claims
- [single-source] On 11 Oct 1962 Army Materiel Command asked Natick (then QMR&E) for a specialized Special Forces uniform for Vietnam; Natick designed the coat and trousers in 1962.
- [single-source] The cloth was 5.5-oz OG-107 wind-resistant cotton poplin to spec MIL-C-43199.
- [single-source] A 20 Jun 1963 DA directive made the uniform Army-wide for tropical areas.
- First-pattern features: two slanted chest bellows pockets and two lower pockets, exposed pocket buttons, epaulets, waist take-up tabs, pen pockets behind the chest pockets, and an inner gas flap (also described by Heddels and Rare Gear USA, so the description is multi-source).

## Notes
- The page cites US Army Materiel Command, "Support to US Army in Vietnam: Clothing & Equipment" (Feb 1967); that report was NOT located.
- The 11 Oct, 22 Oct (Gen. Rosson) and 7 Nov 1962 dates are forum or search-snippet only (Wikipedia OG-107, US Militaria Forum snippets). Treat the Special Forces / Natick origin as secondary; the Natick/AMC primary documents and the MIL-C-43199 text were not found.
- "Cut patterned on the WWII parachutist jacket" appeared only in a retailer snippet [single-source] and is not recorded as a claim.
