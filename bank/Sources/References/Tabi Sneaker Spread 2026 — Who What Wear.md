## Summary
Who What Wear UK shopping feature by Marina Avraam, published 2026-08-22 (headline variant: "Tabi Flats Were Once Fashion's Weirdest Shoe"). WebFetch returned only the headline, so the page was read in full via curl and parsed locally (first-hand text, not a model summary). ## Key Claims
- "The moment Martin Margiela debuted his now-iconic Tabi boot in 1988": models in white lab coats walked with soles dipped in red paint, leaving split-toe footprints.
- Tabis later spread beyond the boot into loafers, heels and the ballet flats that are "one of the house's most recognisable designs"; the split toe is traced to Japan, where tabi were split-toe socks worn with thong sandals, and reportedly inspired Margiela "after a trip to Japan".
- 2026 framing: "2026 feels like the year the Tabi has finally crossed over", citing Kylie Jenner wearing hers on a Citi Bike in New York.
- Prices (UK): Margiela Tabi ballet flats £750 to £950; "you no longer need to spend a cool £700"; Anthropologie (Matisse) £98, Free People/Jeffrey Campbell Pit Lane £158, Steve Madden Cyra £130.
- Square-toe "Tabi-adjacent" flats from Massimo Dutti (£120), COS (£85), M&S (£36), Alohas (£150) and Free People (£158) are presented as the same "slightly offbeat" movement without the split toe.
- Not on the page: any Margiela x Louboutin pairing, Nike Air Rift, FDMTL x Vans, Kostadinov x ASICS (see [[Sources/References/NikeSKIMS Mesh Rift — Hypebae]], [[Sources/References/FDMTL x Vans Authentic Tabi — Hypebeast]], [[Sources/References/Kiko Kostadinov x ASICS LYASA FF Tabi — Hypebeast]]).
