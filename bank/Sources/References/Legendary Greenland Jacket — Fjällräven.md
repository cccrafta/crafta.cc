## Summary
Fjällräven's own account of the 1968 Greenland Jacket, G-1000 and Greenland Wax, with owner testimonials about mending. Maker page (T1 for the brand's origin story); the blend percentages come from retailer blogs, not this page. Summary from WebFetch.

## Key Claims
- Åke Nordin released the Greenland Jacket in 1968 in his new G-1000 fabric, "waxed to offer water- and wind-resistance, with his specially created Greenland Wax".
- Brand states roots to 1960 ("Since 1960, nature's been in our DNA").
- Retailer sources (not the maker page) say the 1966 Scandinavian Greenland expedition prompted it, that G-1000 is 65% polyester / 35% cotton, and that it started as a lightweight tent fabric; treat as secondary.
- The page states the jacket "set the standard for how we develop our jackets today" and the mission "to inspire and enable more people to walk with nature".
- Durability evidence offered is testimonial only: one owner bought in 1976 and a replacement in 2011 and says she has "mended it several times"; another bought two in 1980. No lab data.
- Claim: "a few of the original jackets are still around".
- Takeaway for design: the fabric blend is a tent-cloth/workwear-style dense weave re-purposed for body-worn outdoor use, then user-waxed to adjust performance, a repairable-by-the-owner model.
