## Summary
Collector reference page on the early US jungle and camouflage uniforms of the Pacific, from the Panama trials to the 1944 Army camouflage designation. It corroborates the Green Book on the 1942 one-piece suit and adds the Marine camouflage utilities.

## Key Claims
- Cresson Kearny's Panama jungle trials of 1941-42 are described here too, matching the Green Book (so Kearny is two-source).
- AGF approved a one-piece HBT jungle suit on 28 Jul 1942, with cargo pockets, tight cuffs and internal suspenders to take weight off the shoulders (better ventilation, fewer insect bites); this is the second independent source for the claim carried in the Green Book.
- Marine camouflage utilities (reversible frogskin) were first contracted in Oct 1942; combat debut was Bougainville (Nov 1943) and Tarawa. The report pairs this page with the Leatherneck article "Camouflaging the Corps" (Marine Corps Association, T2) for these Marine details.
- The Marine utilities had small pockets, snaps that pulled through the cloth, and a pattern that was too visible.
- Army camouflage went "limited standard" on 30 Mar 1944.

## Notes
- The Marine "P44" jacket detail is NOT sourced anywhere in the report; no adoption date was found.
- Publication is rated T3 in the vault; the report treated this page as T2 for these claims.
