## Summary
V&A object record for a man's three-piece pin-striped flannel boating suit. The museum's own design note records the crossover of sporting dress into informal everyday dress in the late 19th century. Retrieved from the V&A API (system number O78731).

## Key Claims
- Accession number T.113 to B-1934; made in Great Britain 1890-1900; maker unknown; given by Dr C. W. Cunnington. Cream wool with blue pinstripe, hand- and machine-sewn.
- "Light-coloured suits such as this became popular from the 1890s. Matching coats, trousers and waistcoats, known as 'dittos', in pin-striped flannel were accepted dress for summer sports and holidays." Often completed with a straw boater.
- "Striped jackets were originally worn for cricket, tennis and rowing and became fashionable for seaside wear during the 1880s."
- Museum interpretation: "The infiltration of sporting dress into informal styles of clothing shows how social conventions were relaxing in the late 19th century."
- Cites Mrs Humphry's etiquette book Manners for Men (1897) for the principle that dress had to suit the occasion.
