## Overview
Follow-up to [[Research/Maison Margiela Under Glenn Martens]]. The three signatures are not famous for the same reason. The tabi and the GAT (Margiela's Replica) became visible through the market and social media; the bianchetto is being pushed by the house itself, through the FW26 show and a China programme. Premise check: no source found shows the bianchetto is "famous" in the mass sense. Its evidence is one event, the FW26 collection and some TikTok posts. Spelling: the technique is *bianchetto* (the user's "bianchetta" was a typo).

Cross-cutting pattern (inference, not sourced): each started as an existing object with one small change (split toe on a boot; white paint over any object; a surplus trainer reproduced almost unchanged). Tabi and GAT both come from work or military footwear.

## Historical Timeline
- **15th century (house, V&A, MoMA)** The split-toe tabi sock, the house's stated source [^8] [^13] [^14]. ABC gives a longer chain (reaches Japan from China in the 5th century as a sock; single-piece leather in the 15th century) from one WebFetch summary [^3]
- **Late Meiji to Taisho** Rubber is stitched or glued to tabi soles to make the jika-tabi. Marugo dates its sewn-on rickshaw-tyre version to about 1917 (Kurashiki city) or 1919 (company history) [^26] [^28]; Japanese Wikipedia credits Ishibashi Tokujiro's glued-on version, sold from 1922 and registered as a utility model in October 1923 [^30]. ABC names his younger brother Shojiro Ishibashi for 1922 [^3]. The inventor is therefore contested; Marugo's chairman calls it a worldwide simultaneous development (Converse began rubber-soled sneakers in 1917) [^27]
- **1970s** The West German Army seeks new training shoes; adidas and Puma both bid [^21] [^24]
- **1982** adidas receives the contract for the white indoor model; made at ZDA Partizanske, Czechoslovakia (now Slovakia); about 500,000 pairs by 1989 [^22] [^1]. German Wikipedia gives the 1982 date without a footnote
- **1988** Margiela's debut show, Café de la Gare, Paris; the Tabi boot appears on the first model [^13] [^14] [^16] [^8]. This is the SS1989 season: the house dates the Tabi to its "first collection in 1988" [^8], museums say "debut catwalk show in 1988" [^13] [^14], and the Galliera retrospective starts at "spring-summer 1989" [^15]. SSENSE calls the second show FW1989, which implies the first was SS1989 [^16]. The month (October 1988) appears only in search summaries, not in a page read here. Truss and Highsnobiety use "1989" for the Tabi's first season [^17] [^35]. So 1988 is the show year and 1989 the season label (inference from [^15] [^16])
- **1989** Bianchetto: Margiela first overpaints a collection of Salvation Army furniture for the design studio, and the same year extends it to the Tabi shoe [^9]. Press repeats "debuted at the first show in 1989" [^5]; the house's own account is the furniture, so use that
- **Late 1990s (contested)** Margiela finds BW Sport trainers in Austria (a label-story claim, reported by Grailed and Truss) and shows refurbished originals at an "SS1999 Artisanal presentation" [^1] [^21] [^17]. The date is reported by three secondary sources; Hypebeast (2016) says "early 2000s" and other pieces say 1998 [^23] (see Key Details). No house source dates it
- **Early to mid 2000s** The house makes its own Replica [^1] [^21]; the 2001-2002 pairs with painted soles and handwritten notes are the best known early ones [^21]
- **2006** The house's first Artisanal collection under that name [^12]
- **2023** Lyst Index Q3: the Margiela Tabi Mary Jane is the world's #1 hottest product, searches up 342% in September, after the "Tabi Swiper" TikTok story [^18]
- **2024** Hype around the GAT builds; Vogue India names Margiela tabis "shoes of the year" [^2] [^3]
- **2025** GAT resurgence peaks; adidas relaunches BW Army; Kim Jones's last Dior menswear collection (FW25) revives a GAT-like shape [^2]; British Vogue calls the tabi "unexpectedly mainstream" (July) [^4]
- **2026-01-26** NikeSKIMS Mesh Rift, a split-toe sneaker based on the Nike Air Rift, goes on sale [^33]; April: FDMTL x Vans Authentic Tabi previewed at FDMTL FW26 and Kiko Kostadinov x ASICS LYASA FF Tabi released 11 April [^31] [^32]
- **2026-04-11/12** Bianchetto Atelier Experience, Shenzhen [^5]
- **2026-08-22** Who What Wear: "2026 feels like the year the Tabi has finally crossed over" [^7]
- **Summer 2026** Tabi peak; **September 2026** Teen Vogue headline "Tabis Are Over" [^4]
- **2026** GAT called a renaissance for AW26; adidas BW Army sold as the cheap version [^6] [^24]

## Key Details

### Tabi: why it spread
- **Meaning came from exposure, not form.** Greenwood: a split toe has no inherent avant-garde quality; runways, editorials, celebrities and criticism attached it to Margiela, and wearing it signalled insider knowledge because the shape had to be recognised, unlike a logo [^4].
- **Viral and celebrity moments.** TikTok story of $1,800 tabis stolen on a Tinder date (2023, per ABC); Zendaya, Pedro Pascal, Dua Lipa; Vogue India "shoes of the year" [^3]. Lyst's own Q3 2023 Index confirms the TikTok story (more than 178M followers reported) and ranks the Tabi Mary Jane #1 [^18]. That ranking is a 2023 fact only: the Q3 2025 top 10 contains no Tabi and Margiela is outside the top 20 brands [^19]. No Lyst data for 2026 was found.
- **First shoe, hard to make.** No traditional cobbler would take on the split toe; the first boots were made by an Italian craftsman, Mr Zagato, introduced by Antwerp retailer Geert Bruloot (SSENSE, single account) [^16]. The house says the first heel was a wide cylinder matching the diameter of a human heel [^8].
- **Easier versions.** The ballet flat and mule take the split toe onto familiar shapes; the house's flats cost £750 to £950, against £98 to £158 for split-toe flats at Anthropologie, Free People and Steve Madden [^7]. Highsnobiety reports a $1,290 Tabi Daily Mule built on the Birkenstock Boston shape and argues the split toe is now "basic" [^36].
- **Sportswear and mass-market spread.** NikeSKIMS Mesh Rift (a split-toe sneaker with "a similar base design to the popular Nike Air Rift"; Hypebae does not mention a separate relaunch of the original Air Rift) [^33]; FDMTL x Vans Authentic Tabi in boro-patchwork indigo denim with sashiko-style stitching, release listed only as "2026" [^31]; Kostadinov x ASICS LYASA FF Tabi, drawing on ASICS' 1970s Tabi marathon shoes [^32]. The earlier claim that Margiela paired the split toe with Louboutin's red sole in spring 2025 is not on the Who What Wear page and is dropped.
- **Backlash.** Once the knowledge circulated widely the shoe lost its insider-signal power; Greenwood: "not dead, only mainstream" [^4].
- **Japanese side (maker sources).**
  - *Jika-tabi origin:* Marugo's own history credits Fujiki Itaro with sewing reshaped rickshaw tyres to tabi (company history: 1919; Kurashiki city: about 1917), at a time when people wore straw sandals or wooden clogs outdoors [^26] [^28]. Japanese Wikipedia credits Ishibashi Tokujiro (Nippon Tabi, Kurume) with the glued-on sole, sold from 1922 [^30]; ABC's "Shojiro Ishibashi, 1922" looks like a conflation of the brothers (inference) [^3]. Marugo's chairman says rubber soles emerged worldwide at once [^27].
  - *Why the Western market mattered to Marugo:* demand for jika-tabi itself had been tapering; overseas buyers asked for fashionable versions, and Marugo launched ASSABOOTS in Europe in 2009 and in Japan in 2012, then Tabirira (2014) without the metal clasps [^27] [^28]. This agrees with Namidome's ABC quote that Western popularity helps keep the tradition alive [^3].
  - *Where it stands:* SOU・SOU says only three companies still make jika-tabi in Japan and about 98% of those sold are made abroad (a company press story, 2024) [^29]; ABC's "a dozen makers once, now three major ones" comes from the same company, so it is not independent [^3]. Japanese Wikipedia puts Rikio at 60 to 70% of the market and Marugo at about 30%, unsourced [^30]. Marugo restarted jika-tabi production in Japan in 2012 [^26]. SOU・SOU sells about 20,000 pairs a year; its jika-tabi are made by Takasago Sangyo in Hyogo, which at first refused the order [^29].
  - *Work use:* jika-tabi are still worn on building sites and at festivals; Marugo's festival tabi hold about 60% of that segment and wearers are said to feel the ground and keep balance (maker claims) [^27] [^3]. Margiela reportedly first saw tabi on construction workers [^3]; the V&A and the house say only "a trip to Japan" or a Japanese sock [^13] [^8].

### Bianchetto: why it is visible
- **Technique (house, T1):** a matt white overpaint "that leaves visible brush strokes", applied to objects and clothes "in the same way as walls are painted"; the hand-made paint "can crackle with use" and fades, which the house presents as recording wear [^9] [^10]. Matt finish was chosen on purpose "to record the story of each object" [^9]. Care: garments washed inside out; shoes and bags not washed [^10].
- **On leather:** black nappa hand-painted with a white coating that "intentionally chips off over time" (i-D, partner content for Reebok, 2021) [^25].
- **Not documented anywhere found:** paint chemistry, number of coats, any heat or baking step, drying, whether garments are painted flat or on forms. The FW26 tailcoat "painted white, baked and cracked open" comes from a runway review (Daily Front Row), not the house; the house's own FW26 technique page says nothing about method [^34] [^11]. Hube and Collater.al on the Shenzhen event give "layers of white paint" and "visible brushstrokes" only (read; no source notes made, nothing beyond the house pages).
- **Pushed by the house.** FW26 Shanghai included a bianchetto tailcoat [^34]; the FW26 technique page names Bianchetto knee boots ($2,790) and pumps ($1,750) [^11]; the Shenzhen Atelier Experience (April 2026) let guests paint their own garment under Paris atelier staff, wearing a blouse blanche, as part of MaisonMargiela/folders, an archive-sharing project [^5].
- **Social interest.** A TikTok video (August 2025) calls it a "blueprint" (search-result text only, not read). TikTok posts from about September 2026 say the house sent buckets of white paint to fashion influencers; no editorial outlet confirms it, so treat it as unverified and keep it out of a post.
- **Open question:** whether the visibility is a trend or a brand programme. Evidence points to the latter.

### GAT / Replica: why it is visible
- **Samba effect.** Buyers wanted a flat shoe less obvious than the overexposed Samba; the GAT is the quieter option [^2]. Ape to Gentleman ties the comeback to low-profile sneakers such as the Samba and Puma Speedcat, and calls it a "renaissance" for AW26 [^24] [^6].
- **Ordinary by design.** Highsnobiety: born of Margiela's wish to reclaim the quintessential normal shoe [^2].
- **Price ladder.** Margiela about $750 (writer's figure) [^2]; adidas BW Army $115 [^1]; Allevol $475, Adnitt $327, Wallace Mercantile $289 [^1]. The "$120" price in the earlier stub is not on the Ape to Gentleman page and is dropped [^6].
- **Luxury and clone spread.** Dior FW25, Officine Creative, Valentino, Givenchy, Louis Vuitton, MM6 variants, a GU clone [^2].
- **Heritage underneath.** About half a million pairs by 1989; cheap surplus, little overt military baggage, European cult following by the 1990s [^21] [^22] [^1].
- **Designer: contested, not resolved.** What is documented: adidas received the 1982 contract for the white indoor model, which resembles the Samba, made at ZDA Partizanske (German Wikipedia, T4; only the factory claim is footnoted) [^22]; an adidas spokesperson confirmed adidas "designed a trainer, called the BW-Sport" (via Leitch, WSJ 2015) [^20]; the Bundeswehr History Museum in Dresden told Leitch it "only had records that the design was made by Puma", "at least in the testing phase", and Puma says it has no records of making it [^20] [^21]. Every account found traces to Leitch's one report, so the Puma record is a single-source claim and the museum record itself has not been seen. The shoes carry no branding by design [^20]. Two explanations in print: adidas built it unbranded to order (Richardson's conjecture) [^21], or the Bundeswehr wrote the spec and used several contractors (Heddels; Ape to Gentleman: "contracts shifted between manufacturers") [^1] [^24]. Safest wording for a post: adidas produced the 1980s issue shoe; who first designed it is disputed. A Bundeswehr specification and the Dresden record were not found.
- **Margiela's first use: date unresolved.** Heddels, Grailed (2018) and Truss say refurbished originals appeared at an SS1999 Artisanal presentation [^1] [^21] [^17]; Grailed and Truss both draw on the shoe's label story, so they may not be independent. Hypebeast (2016) says "early 2000s" [^23]; Complex and The Modest Man give 1998 (search summaries only). The house's own About page dates its "first Artisanal collection" to 2006 and does not mention the trainer, so "Artisanal" in the SS1999 claim may be a retroactive label (inference) [^12]. Use "late 1990s" in a post.
