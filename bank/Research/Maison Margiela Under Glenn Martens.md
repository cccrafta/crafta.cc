## Overview
Glenn Martens took over Margiela from John Galliano (creative director from January 2025) and has shown four collections in about 15 months, each at a different point on the couture to ready-to-wear line. The direction since July 2025 is toward clothes that are worn, with the couture-level handwork kept in a small Artisanal share. Evidence is mostly runway reviews (T2-T3) plus the house's own show notes and pages (T1); construction detail for FW26 is the richest part. Crafta angle: the handwork and the technique lineage (bianchetto, tailoring vs flou, upcycling), not the brand story. Owner scope filter: French/Belgian/Italian-owned house, in scope; luxury cachet is not the interest.

## Historical Timeline
- **2025-01** Martens becomes creative director, succeeding Galliano [^5] [^14]
- **2025-07-09** Artisanal FW25-26 debut, Paris couture week, scheduled for 7:30 p.m. [^21]; staged at 104, every model masked; FashionNetwork called it a "stumbling debut" [^17]. Other outlets' reviews were not read.
- **2025-10** SS26, first ready-to-wear, Paris (Centquatre): children's orchestra in oversized tuxedos, four-stitch mouthpieces, leather, denim, asymmetric tailoring [^2]
- **2026-02-17** OTB reports 2025: group revenue €1.7bn down 4.8%; Margiela up 8.4% [^4] [^19]
- **2026-04-01** FW26, Shanghai shipping container dock; Artisanal and ready-to-wear in one show [^6]
- **2026-09-29** SS27 "Hard and Soft", Paris, Élysée Montmartre, 62 silhouettes [^10] [^8]
- **2026-09-18** OTB announces Martens leaves Diesel after 2026-09-22 Milan show; stays at Margiela [^5] [^14]

## Key Details
- **FW26 hybrid show.** One show instead of separate couture and ready-to-wear; "20 percent Artisanal gowns", orderable after the show; the Artisanal and ready-to-wear teams worked on one project [^6]. Martens: "The artisanal has always been embedded within the ready-to-wear" [^6].
- **No separate July 2026 Artisanal show.** The house's stated plan under Martens was two ready-to-wear shows and one Artisanal a year [^7]; the FW26 Shanghai show carried the Artisanal gowns [^6], WWD's July 24 calendar says Margiela returned to Paris on Sept. 29 "after opting for Shanghai last season" [^8], and the July 2026 couture-week preview names Margiela only for an auction of Martin Margiela's own pieces [^15]. No source places a Margiela show in July 2026 [inference from absence, backed by the dated statements above].
- **Ceramics dress.** About 500 ceramic pieces; toiled in calico, ceramics fired to the pattern pieces, glazed, fired again, broken by hand, rounded and sewn to organza; 300+ hours [^1]. Single source for the firing and breaking process; Hypebeast confirms dresses made from "actual shattered porcelain" [^22].
- **Bianchetto.** Tailcoats painted with white bianchetto paint [^1] [^22]; WWD describes latex white paint dropped over embellished ballgowns and the edges peeled back [^6]. Daily Front Row's "baked, cracked open" step is single-source [^1]. The house itself names Bianchetto as one of four house codes, a "white overpaint technique" [^12].
- **Upcycling.** Waistcoat from a 19th-century tapestry [^1]; the finale gown from a 5 m painting bought at Marché de Saint-Ouen, reassembled as a column dress [^6], draped over a double corset uncut and reversible [^1].
- **Intent on ready-to-wear.** Martens: "democratic", "never precious", "I want people to wear Margiela" [^2].
- **SS27 "Hard and Soft".** Martens' own show notes, quoted by FashionNetwork, say the research is "rooted in the traditions of Parisian ateliers, divided between the 'tailleur'... and the 'flou'", and that the house is "reclaiming the haute couture practices of the 'tailleur' and the 'flou'" for ready-to-wear [^10]. Design Scene and WWD agree on the mix: rigid corset panels and reinforced darts on draped dresses, soft folded lapels on stiff jackets [^9] [^11]. Construction examples: floral prints coated in resin then unpleated so the surface fractured along old fold lines over a supple back [^10]; trenches with rigid panels [^11]. Accessories: thick leather bags folded like a deflated football, cord-wrapped sandals, silver dishware breastplate, leather trench with double collar over check wool [^3] (Highsnobiety via WebFetch summary).
- **Tailoring vs flou, outside Margiela.** Dior's house museum describes the same split: the flou makes dresses in soft fabrics, the tailleur the more structured outfits, and the two need "a different technique"; Dior today has one atelier of each, about thirty people apiece [^16]. No Fédération, V&A, Galliera or Met page stating the split was found; the claim rests on Martens' notes and one house museum, which is enough for the idea but not for dating its origin.
- **Business.** Margiela grew 8.4% in 2025 (CEO: "in excess of 8%"); OTB does not report brand revenue [^4] [^19]. Group wholesale fell 14.7% and OTB aims for 25-30% of turnover over time [^4] [^19]. OTB spoke of growth goals for 2026 but gave no figures [^4]. Martens now works only at Margiela [^5] [^10]. Backstage at SS27 he said, per a Spanish-language report, that the house must address a much wider clientele than Martin did, which makes it hard to lean so heavily on concepts [single-source: Bad Hombre Magazine, translated, not logged as a source note].
- **Tabi.** The house says the Tabi is its first shoe design, shown in its first collection in 1988 and inspired by a 15th-century Japanese sock design [^20]; Highsnobiety dates it to the "1989 debut collection" (the SS89 season, shown October 1988) [^18]. Highsnobiety argues the split-toe has gone mainstream; the Tabi Daily Mule is priced $1,290 [^18]. The jika-tabi work-shoe lineage is not in the house's wording; see [[Research/Why Margiela Tabi, Bianchetto and the GAT Are Everywhere]].
- **MaisonMargiela/folders.** Four "house codes" (Artisanal, Anonymity masks, Tabi, Bianchetto) shown across China in April 2026: 58 Artisanal looks from 1989 to FW26 in Shanghai, nine Tabi collectors in Chengdu, a guided bianchetto session in Shenzhen; material is shared through internal Dropbox folders, whose contents were not opened [^12].
