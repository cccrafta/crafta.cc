## Overview

Question: which London brands fit the Crafta owner's taste? Filter: real production lineage, named mills, repair, military-to-civilian, function. Cachet labels and brand-story-first labels rejected. Three research lanes (A outerwear/workwear/knit, B tailoring/shoes/hats/leather, C small-batch/shops/archives) ran in parallel; findings merged below. Cache notes: [[Research/Cache/2026-11-06 London Brands A]], [[Research/Cache/2026-11-06 London Brands B]], [[Research/Cache/2026-11-06 London Brands C]].

Recommendation: start with **Blackhorse Lane Ateliers** (denim, own factory, free repairs), **Grenfell** (Haythornthwaite cloth lineage, already tracked in vault), **Margaret Howell** (named mills, own workroom, archive), **Canons** (bespoke shoes rebuilt around repair), and **The Vintage Showroom** (best research archive). Hypotheses about how any of this feeds the collection are `untested` and stay out of posts.

Key filter result: many "London" labels are designed in London but made elsewhere. A brand's postcode is not its lineage; check where it is sewn.

## Tier 2: Worth checking (partial evidence)

- **Gloverall** (London 1951): civilianised Royal Navy duffle; toggle for gloved hands [^glo1][^glo2]. Coats made "at a factory outside London"; Wellingborough claim rests on one blog [single-source]. Current ownership and design base not found.
- **Raeburn** (2009): first collection from genuine military parachutes; PWVC reversible Ventile/parachute jacket used 23 parachutes [^rae1][^rae2]. Own-line production place not stated.
- **Albam** (2006): Ventile Alpine jacket in runs of about 70 (Permanent Style 2010); no named factories in current sources [^alb1].
- **Dashing Tweeds** (2006, one source says 2002): East London studio, woven by UK commission weavers; reflective yarn for Fred Perry; Anderson & Sheppard supplier [^dt1][^dt2]. Founding-year conflict.
- **John Lobb Ltd** (maker says 1849; some sources London from 1866): 5th-generation family bespoke, independent of the Hermes brand [^lobb1]. Production specifics Medium.
- **G.J. Cleverley** (1958): Royal Arcade workshop per search summary only [single-source] [^cle1].
- **Carréducker** (2004): bespoke made in Oxo Tower studio; founders Cordwainers teachers [^car1].
- **James Smith & Sons** (1830): basement workshop, New Oxford St; "last in London to make umbrellas on site" [^js1]. Repair service implied only.
- **Cromford Leather** (1971, renamed 2015): leather tailoring made at 56 Chiltern St; made garments for Margaret Howell [^cro1].
- **London Tradition** (2001): duffles and pea coats cut and sewn in own East London factory; own site only [single-source] [^lt1].
- **Nepenthes London** (store 2019): Engineered Garments, Needles, South2 West8; owner already follows Nepenthes LA [^nep1][^nep2].
- **Clutch Cafe**: widest Japanese workwear range in London per Permanent Style (Buzz Rickson, Real McCoy's, Warehouse); Epperson Mountaineering as the climbing crossover, though PS calls it "a little synthetic" [^clu1].
- **Nicholas Daley** [single-source on mills], **Drake's** (ties made in London; shirts Chard, tailoring Italy, knitwear Scotland) [^dr1], **Globe-Trotter** (vulcanised fibre case, London since c.1932; factory location unconfirmed) [^gt1], **Swaine/Brigg** (umbrella place of making unconfirmed) [^sw1].

## Rejected or watch-list

- **Not London-made (shop or HQ only):** Crockett & Jones, Tricker's, Edward Green, Cheaney (Northampton); Turnbull & Asser (Gloucester); Ettinger (Walsall since 1999); Budd (sewn Andover); Herbert Johnson (Midlands civilian hats).
- **Not London:** Universal Works, Sunspel (Nottingham); Nigel Cabourn (Newcastle); Private White V.C. (Manchester); Community Clothing (Blackburn); Barbour (South Shields); Baracuta (Manchester); Dawson Denim, Hawksmill (Brighton); Rab, Montane, Berghaus.
- **Brief premise wrong:** Paynter Jacket Co is made in Guimaraes, Portugal, designed East London [^pay1]. Good small-batch chore coat, but not London-made.
- **Story louder than lineage:** Percival (capsule only made in England) [^per1], Guards London, Oliver Spencer, YMC (about 40% made in England claim, no named factories), Gieves & Hawkes, Cordings (retailer), Lock & Co (hats from outside makers; keep as service reference).
- **Cachet or no lineage evidence:** Burberry, Aquascutum, Belstaff, Beaufort types, Launer, Wales Bonner, Craig Green, Vollebak, Wax London, Studio Nicholson, Folk.
- **Out of scope:** Story mfg (Indian-subcontinent-inspired), Whitcomb & Shaftesbury (partly made in India).
- **Lead not confirmed:** Kiko Kostadinov (Wikipedia only; production countries unknown) [^kk1].

## Archives and research resources

- **Westminster Menswear Archive** (Harrow, founded 2016): 3,000+ garments from the 1780s; sportswear strength (cycling, hiking, skiing, motorcycling). Access terms unknown [single-source] [^wma1]. Best lead for the body-under-load thread.
- **V&A Order an Object**: free, up to 5 objects, South Kensington or V&A East Storehouse [^va1].
- **IWM Research Room** (Lambeth): 3D objects Mondays; whether uniforms qualify not stated [^iwm1].
- **Fashion & Textile Museum** (Bermondsey): rotating shows only [^ftm1].
- **SRBA apprentice scheme**: 3-6 year training by discipline [^srba1].

## What Publications Are Saying

### Recent (RSS)
Tracked feeds (Heddels, Stridewise, Carryology, Rampboy, Put This On, Vintage Showroom) gave almost no London-specific hits for the tailoring lane. Strongest recent items: Permanent Style on Canons (Jun 2025, Mar 2026) [^can1] and the Herald Business interview with Margaret Howell's managing director (16 Oct 2026) [^mh3].

### Archive (Web Search / Sitemap)
Permanent Style carries most of the tailoring, shoe and repair evidence (nine source notes). Heddels and Denimhunters cover denim and the duffle. Maker pages (T1) anchor workroom claims for Poole, Huntsman, Lobb, Cleverley, Carréducker.

## Gaps — What Hasn't Been Written

- No London maker found for climbing or body-under-load garments; only Epperson via Clutch Cafe and Outsiders retail (single-source). Collection thread must look beyond London or to the Westminster archive.
- No confirmed London user of British Millerain, Ventile, Fox Brothers, Hainsworth or Abraham Moon (Margaret Howell's Fox Brothers link rests on one interview [single-source]).
- Only one London re-waxer found (Ace of Suedes, Edmonton, GBP 40, search summary only); the Barbour re-waxing story is South Shields, not London.
- Not run: V&A API, Internet Archive, Google Patents (Globe-Trotter 1901 fibre patent, Poole and Lobb ledgers are candidates for a deep dive).

## Single-source and unresolved claims

Treat as unverified: Cleverley making location; Brigg/Swaine making location; Drake's factory addresses; Globe-Trotter Broxbourne; Huntsman Inherited Garment Service terms and factory-made made-to-measure; Gieves ownership date; Laird Hertfordshire workshop; Henry Poole founding location; A&S in-house workrooms; Gloverall factory and Wellingborough; Margaret Howell Fox Brothers and 4,000 shirts per year; Blackhorse Lane 10,000 pairs per year; Grenfell Everest 1953/Admiral Byrd (brand-sourced via The Rake); Nigel Cabourn death report 11 Jun 2026 (search-only, moot). Date conflicts: Lobb 1849 vs 1866; A&S move 2004 vs 2005; Albam 2006 vs 2007; Dashing Tweeds 2006 vs 2002; Christys liquidation Feb 2026 (one secondary source).

## Possible Angles
1. **"London Label, Made Elsewhere"** (Culture/Design): postcode vs production lineage; Paynter, Percival, Gloverall, Drake's against Blackhorse Lane and Grenfell.
2. **Blackhorse Lane: rebuilding London denim** (Design): one factory, one 1896 fly pattern, free repairs.
3. **Grenfell: cloth, factory and the Everest claim** (Materials): connects to [[Sources/Publications/Haythornthwaite]]; needs cloth-source triangulation first.
4. **Repair as practice** (Technique): invisible reweaving, Canons' rewired workroom, Huntsman's inherited-garment service.
5. **Military cut, civilian cloth** (History): Dege & Skinner's regiment decline, Gloverall duffle, Raeburn parachutes; ties to [[Research/The Harrington Jacket]] and [[Research/Safari Jacket]].
