## This Week in Craft & Fashion

### Patina Culture on Both Sides of the Atlantic
Heddels ran two Fade Friday features back-to-back this week: an APC Petit New Standard at 10 years and 1 wash, and a ROY RS1 Straight at 15 years and ~20 washes — the latter showing sashiko repairs across the knees. Long John simultaneously published a denim care myth-busting piece, debunking freezing and hot-water washing. The volume of patina content in a single week signals this is still the core cultural driver in heritage denim — not product launches, not new silhouettes.

### Lost Construction Details — A Crafta Angle
Long John published "5 Design Elements That Aren't Standard On A Pair Of Jeans Anymore": suspender buttons, cinch backs, exposed rivets, crotch rivets, and cloth patches. Each explained for its original functional purpose. This is exactly the Crafta territory — construction features that disappeared when manufacturing logic changed. The cinch back especially has a story worth its own post.

### The Denim Institute and Museum Opens in LA (June 2026)
The Denim Institute and Museum is opening in Downtown LA's Gerry Building — 25 global brands in a Legacy Brand Hall, chronological from Levi's through the late 1980s. Founded by Jonny Saven and Loren Cronk. First institution of its kind dedicated to denim preservation and education. Worth tracking for primary source material.

### French Workwear Resurgence
Long John covered L'Usine Bleue — a Stockholm retailer focused on authentic French workwear (Coltin, moleskin jackets, made entirely in France using historic textile mills). Archival Clothing's feed remains slow (last post March 2026) but their earlier coverage of French workwear still resonates with this trend.

### Headlight's HBT Coveralls
Heddels covered Headlight's 10oz "Random" HBT (herringbone twill) work garb — Japanese-made coveralls reviving century-old workwear silhouettes. HBT is underwritten at Crafta and this surfaces it as live material.

## Worth Watching

**1. The cinch back moment**
Long John's lost-construction-details piece is getting traction. The cinch back — the adjustable tab at the rear waist of vintage jeans — is the most mechanically interesting of the five lost features. It predated belt loops as the primary fit mechanism, was eliminated in the 1950s when the belt became standard. A Crafta post on this could be strong.

**2. Denim Museum as primary source**
The LA Denim Institute opening means there will be a physical archive with 25 legacy brands documented chronologically. Worth monitoring as it opens — the exhibited garments and their construction documentation could be T1 sources for future posts.

## From Japan

- **Porter CRAG series** — Houyhnhnm's Minor Good Dictionary spotlighted Porter's underrated CRAG bag line, specifically its two-bath dyeing technique that gives the fabric its distinctive appearance. Porter's main lines get coverage; the CRAG doesn't. Worth a deeper look.
